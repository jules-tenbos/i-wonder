# Notes

## Storyline

### 1. Why XPath?
- The fabric is a tree. XPath is a language for addressing trees.
- Not borrowed from XML — discovered as the natural fit
- Path expression as both address and query

### 2. Traversal as accumulation
- Walking a path isn't just getting to a destination
- Each step accumulates context — the path determines the rules
- Different paths, different metadata, different behaviour
- The fabric is locally shaped, not globally configured

### 3. Two POVs
- Subject reality POV (repo root) is the ceiling
- Process POV (cwd) is the data root
- `/` means here

### 4. Two axes, strict visibility
- Data looks down (self and descendants only)
- Function looks up (self and ancestors only)
- They never cross — position determines both what you can do and what you can do it on

### 5. Six protocols, one pattern
- Three scope filters (data, metadata, raw) times two interpretation levels (opaque, schema-aware)
- Same three operations everywhere
- The combinatorics are small and complete — no special cases

### 6. Flat vs hierarchical
- Default navigation is context-to-context, simple nodes flatten
- The query doesn't change — the result shape does
- Natural property of the structure, not a mode switch

### 7. Raw as ground truth
- Raw sees everything
- Data and metadata are lenses that filter
- The physical reality is always accessible underneath
