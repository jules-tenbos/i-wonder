# P2P

For me, peer-to-peer is not a technology choice. It is the structure of private experience and sharing.

Every experience of reality belongs to a subject — personal, situated, irreducible. Reality is not experienced centrally and then distributed. It is private to each of us. Experienced locally, shared after. Two subjects sharing — that is already a peer-to-peer network. The most fundamental one.

SPLectrum builds on the seed's principles. If the [seed](/seed) holds — being implies language, language is the medium of experience, subjects share through language, languages are inter-relational with equal standing in potential — then decentralisation is not a design preference. It conforms to how reality is constituted.

## Why it matters

The centralised internet concentrates control over data, identity, and computation. Someone else holds your data. Someone else decides what you can run. Someone else owns the infrastructure your digital life depends on. This is not a technical problem — it is a structural one. Centralisation assumes a view from above. The seed says there is no view from above.

P2P gives us the capability to reverse the direction. Data lives where it belongs — with the subject. Identity is self-sovereign. Computation happens at the edge. No intermediary decides what is allowed. The architecture matches the structure of experience rather than contradicting it.

## The tools for P2P and beyond

SPLectrum uses [Bare](/engineering/substrate/bare/) as the default runtime. The main reasons: it is minimal, it is not strongly typed, and it opens access to [Pear](https://docs.pear.holepunch.to) for P2P. Bare and Pear are both built by [Holepunch](https://holepunch.to) — the team behind the distributed infrastructure that makes peer-to-peer practical. Not theoretical P2P. Working P2P.

The centralised internet is not the enemy to be dismantled. It is the substrate. Decentralised ecosystems emerge on top of what already exists. Not revolution, but emergence. Not replacement, but transcendence.

Another class of emergent technology plays an important public role in decentralisation: blockchains and the applications built on them. Two stand out for me. Midnight, for privacy. Cardano, for the philosophical foundations it is built on — the first and still only blockchain with decentralised governance. Wouldn't you think they all should?

## The deeper point

Decentralisation is often argued on ethical or political grounds — freedom, privacy, resistance to power. Those arguments matter. But for me the seed goes deeper. Decentralisation is not primarily about what we should build. It is about what reality already is. Every subject constitutes its own reality through its own language. Shared reality is the overlap, not the imposition.

Building P2P is building in alignment with that structure. Building centralised is building against it. Both work. One is swimming with the current.
