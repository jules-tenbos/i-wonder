In the previous post I took time out from our journey into metaphysics to make an important point: each of us has the agency to shape their own reality, to fill it with their beliefs and convictions. This is of no less importance when dealing with ethics, which deals with the values a subject or community holds towards other subjects or communties. 

SPLectrum's ethics is not normative but descriptive. The picture of the values we project towards others is far more subtle than a lot of ethics discussion make you belief. And the main reason for that is that - in a sense - we live in multiple realities. Being part of a family, of a local community, going to school, being at work, being in public ... these can be very different realities - where the rules of engagement are quite different from on to the other. These different realities are governed by different 'language games'.

It is important to realise this, because each of these games have their own rules, in a sense their own ethics. You are in a game, you have a role and the values ethics is concerned about are attached to that role. How come one often want to project values as good or bad at the community level, when there are so many different games in play within the community? Because implicitly language games get interpreted in specific ways and shoehorned into an overall good / bad package. But why? To be a good parent, a good citizen, a good judge, a good soldier - good means quite different things for each of those games. A good soldier is there to kill, at times own citizens. A good judge is there to convict, and potentially cause hardship for innocent family and friends.

SPLectrum prefers an equal standing approach - there is no absolute ethical standard. But through belonging there is convergence on what a community considers appropriate - good if one wants to call it that way. But there is always a price to pay - a good soldier kills people, innocent people. 

Ethics, being concerned with the values a subject emanates towards others, is in my opinion a bit like ontology: static and descriptive. It is with politics that the dynamic comes alive and the values are applied. Politics is for another post, it is important to first focus on the value landscape as presented by the multiple language games.

I think the focus on an 'universal' good is misguided, and it makes much more sense to first focus on the different activities one is involved in. I like a descriptive approach to ethics and see it as a prerequisite to informed decisions - agency in action. It is important to realise that (ethical) decisions are not binary. It is not: do I do this or not? In fact it is: do I play the game and perform the role or not, or do I not play the game? Those are three options. Not belonging is a valid and important option.

So my belonging to my community is not binary, as I may choose not to take part in a number of (language) games that are part of it. Belonging is fuzzy, black and white only exists at the imagined threshold of being in or out. It is all about resonance, how strong it is. And our natural language reflects this: 'I have never felt really part of this.' is a statement of fuzzy belonging. And this impacts inevitable the ethical side.

## A. Bridge — from fuzzy belonging to the concrete

- One or two lines turning the resonance point (line 37) into "let me make this concrete." The examples that follow are each a reading of belonging-structure, not just illustrations.

## B. Worked examples — each carries a belonging-structure point, then stops

- **Family** — the warm, universal case, and the home of the *level-of-mixing* point:
  - values arrive by belonging — you are born into the first game, hold its values before you ever choose them.
  - and the mixing beat (folded in from old §7): the separation between family and work is *not* enforced or required — it occurs where it occurs, and its degree is the subject's own call (family business = little/none, nothing wrong with that). This is the privacy value read correctly — a degree the subject sets, not a wall the framework mandates.
  - so the agency here is: setting how separate or blended your belongings are held — the same faculty as the three-options move above, at the level of *mixing* rather than *joining/leaving*.
- **The soldier** — nested belonging and the shared game across the line. Three value-sets:
  - *us* (the unit — loyalty, not leaving a man; the belonging that carries the values is the unit, not the uniform),
  - *us-and-them* (the war game both sides co-inhabit — laws/customs of war, prisoners, "fair warfare," held *across* the enemy line; the enemy is a co-member of the war game, not the pure other),
  - *off-game* (same person as civilian — the killing licence simply absent).
  - The sharp point: deny the shared game and the values toward the enemy don't flip, they *vanish* — dehumanisation is the withdrawal of shared-game membership, which is what removes the fair-warfare constraints. (This example makes "resonance / shared base" visible at its starkest.)
- **Free market / advertising** — a first look at non-resonant co-primacy: a game that manufactures the wants the flourishing game then can't satisfy.
  - ⚠ LIGHTEST HAND. This image tips toward verdict fastest. Keep it doing the structural job (what non-resonant co-primacy looks like); stay OFF the "late capitalism is bad" register. Show the shape, don't call the verdict. It also sets up the culture-scale spectrum below.

## C. Zoom out to the culture — reading the whole game-mix for resonance

- Move the reading up a scale: from a subject's several games to a *culture's* game-mix and its primary axis. Same resonance reading, larger object.
- The spectrum:
  - Homogeneous: games resonate around a shared primary axis; the broad good is coherent, legible.
  - Heterogeneous: many games, weak resonance, no settled primary axis; the broad good is thin or plural — but NOT self-contradicting.
  - Polarised: two+ games raised to co-primacy that do NOT resonate; the culture contradicts itself at the level of what it calls good.
- Hold the distinction hard: **polarisation is not heterogeneity.** Polarisation is contradiction at the primary axis — a culture professing a primary good while giving primacy to a game that spends what that good is made of.
- The instrumental/celebrated hinge (FLAGGED NOT-YET-SETTLED): every culture has a military and a market — that's not the diagnostic. The cut is instrumental-and-subordinate (war as means to protect the good life — resonant) vs celebrated-and-co-primary (martial glory as itself a pinnacle — polarising). Handle lightly; the hinge is not firm yet — keep examples illustrative and abstract, NOT pinned to a named living culture. This is exactly where the "show the tool, don't draw the verdict" line bites hardest.

## D. Close — hand to politics; leave the tool in the reader's hands

- The standing configuration is what ethics reads (static); the movement — how these coexist, shift, and settle — is politics, next (dynamic). Light handoff, don't over-explain the pillar symmetry.
- Land the modesty deliberately: the reader now has the reading as a tool. The verdicts on any actual culture are withheld on purpose — that infilling comes later, on surfaces built to carry it.
