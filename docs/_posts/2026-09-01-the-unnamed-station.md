Once cognition was wide enough — making-sense rather than a possession of minds — something happened that is always slightly embarrassing and always welcome: the wide word turned out to be already at work here. Unnamed, but working.

Look at [P2](/seed/original/): *language is the medium through which a subject experiences reality.* The gloss beneath it walks through what that means — experience requires accumulation, a subject's reality is the totality of what has been retained — and then ends on this sentence: *knowledge is what the subject comes to recognise within that accumulation.* Recognise. *Re-cognoscere* — to become acquainted again. The word has been sitting inside the principle the whole time, one prefix away from its name.

It is not alone. The page on [language](/seed/language/) says, almost in passing: *to understand something is to have created a language for it.* Understanding as an act of creation — something made, made of language, and added to the medium. That is a large thing to say in passing, and the page says it and moves on, because the page is about language and the sentence is about something else. Something that had no page.

So take stock of what the principles say and what they leave unsaid. The medium is named. The experiencing is named, and the accumulation side of it is well developed — [historicity](/seed/historicity/): retention becoming memory, the point of existence stretched into a span that carries its past and leans into its future. What stays unsaid is the far end of the accumulating: what is *made* of it all. That making is what keeps surfacing in fragments — *recognise* here, *created a language for it* there — and the fragments agree with each other. They describe one position that no page holds.

Say it as three stations. **Language** — the substrate, the medium itself. **Experience** — the interaction with it, what flows in and is retained. **Cognition** — what is made of what has been retained; which creates more language. And then the caution that matters more than the list: these are aspects, not steps. Nothing travels around a circuit here. A subject is not first in language, then experiencing, then cognising, then back — all three are going on in every moment of being in a medium at all. There is no lap, so there is no first lap, and no puzzle about where the first medium came from before anything could have made it. Cognition creating language is not a return to the start, because language was never left.

Two more things the fragments carry. Cognition sits *inside* the medium it works on — it extracts from language and it deposits language, sandwiched, not a mechanism operating on the medium from a floor above. And the extracting has a direction worth naming: it reduces. Out of everything the accumulation holds in play, some is held and the rest is let go — a purifying. Experience floods in; what is made of it is always less than what arrived, and sharper. That direction is the station's signature. Recognising is exactly this shape: out of the totality retained, *this* stands forward.

The noticing has a history, and it is the history this series has been living. Months of reading models of how a brain keeps going — selection, competition, error correction — and the machine-learning systems those models are now read against. Every one of those accounts, whatever else it claimed, was answering the same three questions: what is the medium, what flows in, what is made of it. Ask three questions often enough and they wear a groove; eventually the groove wants a name. The name was waiting in the gloss of P2.

So nothing has been added to the seed. That is the point of telling it this way. The station was in the principle's own sentence, one prefix away; the pages had been describing its work in fragments and filing the fragments under other headings. Giving the position its name is not an amendment. It is an introduction — this is cognition, which you have already met.
