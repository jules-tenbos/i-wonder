Recently a new core word was added to SPLectrum: cognition. It got its own definition: *Cognition is the process of acquiring knowledge and understanding through experience*.

The previous post here widened a word. Cognition, freed of two quiet restrictions, came out as the making-sense a subject does — wherever there is a subject to do it. What I did not say is what the widened word is *for*. That surfaced later, through a phrase that would not leave me alone.

The phrase is [Heidegger](/positioning/persons/h/heidegger/)'s, and it is deceptively simple: *we are always already in the world.* There is no starting line. No moment at which a subject stands outside meaning, surveys it, and steps in. Wherever you catch yourself, the world is already meaningful — and the catching happens in meaning too. He stated it as a fact, and the fact holds. But state a fact this large and a question comes with it. *How?* What keeps it so?

Put the question to the seed and something interesting happens. The [second principle](/seed/original/) says: language is the medium through which a subject experiences reality. Read its gloss slowly and it is quietly demanding an answer of its own: experience requires accumulation; a subject's reality is the totality of what has been retained; knowledge is what the subject comes to *recognise* within that accumulation. Retaining, accumulating, recognising — none of it happens by itself. For the principle to stand, something must be making something of what arrives. Take that away and nothing is left but a medium executing — every signal delivered, nothing made of any of it, flawless and going nowhere. No experience, no reality for anyone. So there it is, and it is the main thing this post has to say: *cognition is essential to being a subject.* Not a refinement some subjects add to their being in the world; part of what being a subject is.

And that is how Heidegger's fact holds. The making-sense never wasn't running. There was no first delivery of neutral input waiting for treatment — a subject with a medium is a subject already making something of it, already feeding what it makes back into the medium it makes it from. No circuit is being run, so there is no first lap, and no puzzle about how one first gets into meaning from outside. Always already in — because the process that makes "in" meaningful has no before.

But naming cognition essential is not crowning it, and this matters just as much. The temptation runs the other way: make it the primary relation — first we cognise, then we have a world — and you have rebuilt the spectator picture Heidegger broke, the subject contemplating its world from nowhere. The order is the reverse. Being in language is the fact; cognition runs *inside* it — internal to the dynamics of the language game, extracting from the medium and feeding the medium, never above it and never before it. Essential and demoted at the same time: load-bearing in function, subordinate in rank. The world does not wait for cognition to finish. The two were never apart.

How the making-sense actually runs — in brains, in machines that learn, in whatever else turns out to hold the position — is a field of its own, crowded with models and quarrels, and it is where my reading has been living these past months. That territory keeps. What this post needed was smaller and firmer: whatever the process turns out to be in any particular subject, some process there must be. The seed does not have to pick a model to know the station is occupied. The world was never entered. It was always being made sense of — and that is the how.
