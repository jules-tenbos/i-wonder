In a meeting, someone asks: "Is X equal to Y?" The answer wants to be: "Well, it depends what you mean by equal — they cohere up to a certain transformation, but the transformation is non-trivial and the choice of it matters for the question you're really asking..." And the asker is already irritated. "Just tell me yes or no."

This is the friction. One side is asking a 1-categorical question. The other is tracking something that lives one or more dimensions up. The asker thinks the other side is being evasive or vague. The other side thinks the asker is collapsing the structure being tracked. Both are working in good faith. Neither is wrong at their own level.

Most formal training in the modern world is 1-categorical. Mathematics in the standard curriculum, logic, computation, analytic philosophy — all of them habituate the mind to questions of the form "is X equal to Y?" or "what is the right answer?" or "define X precisely." Those are powerful tools. They have built much of the formal apparatus civilisation runs on. The trouble is they install a default. The default is that *precision lives in equality claims*. Anything that doesn't collapse to a clean equality feels imprecise.

When the substance is higher-dimensional, that default is exactly wrong. Precision at the right level looks like coherence-up-to-transformation, not equality. It looks like "these structures agree in the ways that matter for this question, with a specific accounting of the ways they diverge." That answer is *more* precise than "yes" or "no" — it carries more information, in a form that respects the actual structure. But to a 1-categorical ear, it sounds like equivocation.

The asymmetry is worth noticing. 1-categorical thinkers can be drawn into higher-dimensional thinking by good examples — the meaning-pattern travel test works well here, because once someone sees a pattern recognised across contexts, the structural identity is felt. The reverse move, though, is not really available. You can't go from higher-dimensional structure back to a 1-categorical answer without collapsing what was being tracked.

There are practical moves for working across the mismatch. The first is to lead with wrappers. Articulate the 1-categorical version of what's being said — name it, package it, give the other side something to grasp — even though the substance is higher. The wrapper is something to argue with. The substance is there for those who'll look further. The second move is to surface the mismatch when friction hits. *"We're working at different dimensions here. You're asking for an equality. I'm tracking coherence-up-to-transformation. Both are valid; they answer different questions."* Naming the mismatch often dissolves the friction immediately. The asker isn't being unreasonable; they're working with the tools they were given.

The deeper observation is that 1-categorical insistence is often also a metaphysical demand. *Give me one stable answer to hold.* Higher-dimensional thinking releases that demand without abandoning rigour. The release feels like loss until people see what was gained. What's gained is the ability to track structure at the level it actually lives, not the level our training prefers.

The friction is real. It's not a failure of communication. It's the working-through of a tool upgrade — from 1-categorical to higher-dimensional — happening in slow motion across a culture. Each conversation where someone surfaces the mismatch is one step in that working-through.
