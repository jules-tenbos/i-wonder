After the three posts in the Reality series - [Building from the Seed](), [Flying the Flag]() and [SPLectrum's Aesthetics]() - I think it is appropriate to reflect on where we are. Splectrum approaches reality from three core values: belonging, privacy and creativity. This is in contrast with SPLectrum approach to the structure of reality which is governed by the seed principles.

We are left in the middle of exploring the type of SPLectrum's metaphysics. There is already a clear delineation of the first three pillars: epistemology, ontology and aesthetics. Epistemology or the process of learning is very much an interaction between belonging and privacy - feeding back the way we experience reality privately into what we already know, the shared, belonging. Ontology is about the concepts of our knowledge, aesthetics about how those concepts express the reality in private. If you wonder where aesthetics comes from, if it doesn't belong better in the real of art: yes it does. And it is an art to apply language with skill in the expression of reality.

These three pillars provide the dynamic of disclosure. A reality that discloses itself to me, but very much shaped by the my language, by the way I interact with it. This is not in competition with or a threath to our shared reality where we apply the language of science. In fact this reality of a language of science is not even shared by all humans. It is in the privacy that an individual can explore and be different and feed back to others. It there that aesthetics earns its keep.

With SPLectrum I don't want to tell anyone what to do, or even how to do it. SPLectrum wants to throw light on how we relate to reality, on the agency available to all of us, on the how. Our agency is in language and meaning.

While working this all out the following sentence came to mind for SPLectrum: Reality disclosed in language. Those four words say a lot. SPLectrum is not about positing or claiming, it feels comfortable taking a minimalistic approach: don't say anything about what you don't know. That combined with the disclosive nature of reality into a private realm followed by sharing into a shared realm puts the experience of reality firmly in the camp of language learning and communication. 

And not without reason. We take it for granted, but every bit of experience is wrapped in bits of meaning, concepts in relation to each other. What we see, what we hear, what we feel, what we smell, what we taste and what we consider pure abstract thinking - all annotated with meaning. This is fascinating: how primitive can such experience language be? An on/off trigger in a cell sensing concentration of a specific molecule? It feels like the class system has ceased to exist in meaning land, and democracy is taking hold. Away with the privileged, in come equals standing. 

My position on language has landed me in the midst of philosophy, forced to apply more rigeur and study deeper. A lot of the blog posts is the conversation of me dealing with it. A reason a lot of posts are about a small step forward. This post is no exception. After realising how important the dynamic of expression of maening is to SPLectrum, I thought it makes sense to position the body of SPLectrum's metaphysics before proceeding with ethics and politics. 

metaphysics of disclosure: SPLectrum's strapline is reality (disclosed) in language.

All experience is private and has to be shared. Knowledge the way we know it can only be achieved by subjects coming together - subjective convergence. The fact that subjects use shared language for there private experience is a big stabiliser, subjective convergence is no free for all. SPLectrum's 'process theory' is all around language. Objectivity or truth stabilises the same way as any other concept in language, and language evolve, new language are created to tighten the convergence more and more. The question of absolute objectivity and truth is a red herring, it does not make any difference in our persuit of more precise knowledge.
On the contrary, adherence to the absolute tends to spill into 'absolute theories' - like TOEs - and a rejections of pluralism and the equal standing of languages.

# DRAFT (for blending) — A Metaphysics of Disclosure

Three posts into the Reality series, it is worth stopping to see the shape of what we have built. It started from the seed. The seed speaks a meta-language — it says how reality is structured, not what fills it. [Building from the Seed](/blog/label/reality/) took the step from that structure to the content side, and there three values came with the grain of being a subject: belonging, privacy, creativity. Flying the Flag raised those three as SPLectrum's colours. And SPLectrum's Kind of Aesthetics found, after some struggle, that aesthetics is not the art-appendix of philosophy but the *dynamics of meaning* — the third field standing beside epistemology and ontology.

Those three are the body of SPLectrum's metaphysics. Epistemology is the process of learning: private experience fed back into the shared, the way what I come to know is anchored by coming together with others. Ontology is the concepts themselves, the units of meaning — the settled, standing side. Aesthetics is what happens between them: the living play of meaning and language, concepts expressing a reality from the inside. Take the three together and they describe a single movement — reality disclosing itself to a subject, shaped at every step by the language that subject holds. That is the dynamic of disclosure.

And here is the thing I most want to be clear about. This is not a philosophy that tells you what to believe, or what to do, or even how to do it. It does not posit a world behind experience and ask you to accept it. It describes something more modest and, I think, more useful: *how we relate to reality at all*. The how, not the what. SPLectrum is comfortable with a minimal stance — don't claim what you don't know — and what is left when you stop claiming is the relating itself, which is there to be looked at.

Look at it, and two things stand out. The first is that subjectivity is where our agency lives. Reality is disclosed *to a subject*, from its own seat — not surveyed from nowhere. That seat is not a limitation to apologise for; it is the place from which anything can be seen, changed, or made new at all. To be a subject is to be a participant in reality, not a spectator of it. The second is that the means of that agency is meaning and language. Every scrap of experience arrives already wrapped in meaning — what we see, hear, touch, and even what we take for pure abstract thought, all of it annotated, concepts standing in relation to concepts. That medium reaches further down than we usually credit: how primitive can an experience-language be? A cell flipping a switch at the scent of one molecule? In meaning-land the old class system has fallen away — no privileged vantage, no master language, just languages of equal standing, and the subject working within them.

Because this is a description of the *how*, it does not compete with what anyone believes. A scientist, a person of faith, a sceptic — each relates to reality through a subject's seat and through the meaning they hold, whatever the content of their convictions. SPLectrum does not adjudicate the content. It points at the shared mechanism underneath all of it, the agency and the means that are available to everyone. This is also why the question of an absolute — a single true account, a theory of everything that would settle reality once and for all — is a red herring here. Knowledge stabilises the way any meaning stabilises: subjects converging, held steady by a shared language, tightening the fit as new language is made. Chasing the absolute does not add to that work; it mostly tempts us to reject the plurality and the equal standing of languages that the work depends on.

So this is where the Reality series has arrived: the body of a metaphysics — epistemology, ontology, aesthetics — read not as claims about what reality is, but as the description of how reality is disclosed to us, and of the agency and means we each already hold in that disclosure. With that body in place, the next questions come into view: how we should live with one another, and how we organise together — ethics and politics.
