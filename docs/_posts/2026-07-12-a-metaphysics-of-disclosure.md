Three posts into the Reality series, it is worth stopping to see the shape of what we have built. It started from [Building from the Seed](/blog/label/reality/), where I made the transition from talking about the structure of reality — the seed series — to talking about what populates reality. Three values come with the grain of being a subject: belonging, privacy, creativity.

Those three values set the tone for the three fields of SPLectrum's metaphysics: epistemology, ontology, and its special kind of aesthetics. Epistemology is the process of learning: private experience fed back into the shared, the way what I come to know is anchored by coming together with others. Ontology is about the units of meaning — the concepts, the settled, standing side. SPLectrum's aesthetics is not the art-appendix of philosophy but is concerned with the *dynamics of meaning* — the art of expression. Take the three together and they describe a single movement — reality disclosing itself to a subject, shaped at every step by the language that subject holds. That is the dynamic of disclosure.

And here is the thing I most want to be clear about. This is not a philosophy that tells you what to believe, or what to do, or even how to do it. It does not posit a world behind experience and ask you to accept it. It describes something more modest, and I think more useful: *how we relate to reality* — and the agency we have in that relating.

Look at it, and two things stand out. The first is that subjectivity is where our agency lives. Reality is disclosed *to a subject*, from its own seat — not surveyed from nowhere. That seat is not a limitation to apologise for; it is the place from which anything can be seen, engaged, and worked at all. To be a subject is to be a participant in reality, not a spectator of it. The second is that the means of that agency is meaning and language. Every scrap of experience arrives already wrapped in meaning — what we see, hear, touch, and even what we take for pure abstract thought, all of it annotated, concepts standing in relation to concepts. That is the arriving, and the subject does not author it. What the subject does is work it — the private dialogue in which what arrived is turned over, questioned, recombined, and made new. That is where the agency actually lives: not in authoring the disclosure, but in what one makes of it from one's own seat. And the medium of that work reaches further down than we usually credit: how primitive can an experience-language be? A cell flipping a switch at the scent of one molecule? In meaning-land the old class system has fallen away — no privileged vantage, no master language, just languages of equal standing, and the subject working within them.

Feel free in your beliefs. A scientist, a person of faith, a sceptic — each relates to reality through a subject's seat and through the meaning they hold, whatever the content of their convictions. None of this is in competition with our shared reality, the one where we apply the language of science — a language that is not, in any case, shared by all humans. It sits underneath that reality, describing how any of us come to hold it at all. Because the description is of the *how*, it leaves the *what* to you. That is also why the question of an absolute — a single true account, a theory of everything that would settle reality once and for all — is the wrong question here. Knowledge stabilises the way any meaning stabilises: subjects converging, held steady by a shared language, tightening the fit as new language is made. Imposing the absolute impinges on that privacy, on the subject's agency to decide their beliefs.

I think it is important to have this thought clear as we move further into metaphysics, on to the fields of ethics and politics.
