You cannot step in the same river twice. Everyone knows the line, and everyone attributes it to [Heraclitus](/positioning/persons/h/heraclitus/). But the fragment with the strongest claim to authenticity — B12 — says something different: *on those stepping into the same rivers, ever-different waters flow.* The rivers are the same. The waters are different. Both clauses, in the same breath.

The popular version strips out half the insight. It keeps the flux and drops the constancy. But constancy through flux is the more interesting claim. The river is a river *because* the water flows through it. Stop the flow and it ceases to be a river. What persists is not what stays put — it is what retains its form through change.

That is historicity in the seed's terms. [The previous post](/blog/2026/05/the-seed-and-language/) described language as contingent, personal, relational. But language as described there could still be static — a structure that exists but goes nowhere. Historicity is what makes it go somewhere. When a relational activity leaves a trace, and the trace accumulates, a subject arises.

The body makes this undeniable. The matter you were made of seven years ago is not the matter you are made of now. Cells flow in and out. At the cellular level, the flux is total. And the person persists. So retention cannot live in the matter — the matter has nothing to retain. The body is held together by what it speaks: chemical, neural, metabolic, immunological signalling by which cells coordinate, replace, repair. Disrupt the language and the pattern dies even if every cell is fine. Sustain the language and the pattern persists even though no cell does. No one can deny this about themselves.

[Husserl](/positioning/persons/h/husserl/) saw this from the inside: experience has temporal structure. Every present moment carries traces of what came before — retention — and anticipation of what comes next. Without retention, things happen and nothing is changed by them. [Merleau-Ponty](/positioning/persons/m/merleau-ponty/) extended this into the body: the habitual body accumulates, carries its history forward, and it is this accumulation that makes experience possible. What the seed adds is that this is not limited to human bodies or human experience. Wherever a trace is retained, a subject arises — not because philosophy assumes a subject, but because retention produces one.

The river is held by the concept *river* doing its work. The person by the concept *person* doing its work. The flux is what is there. The concept is what retains. And concepts are not only human. A cell's self/non-self distinction is concept-work at the cellular level — a cut maintained immunologically and metabolically. Membrane physics does inside/outside as a concept-cut. Historicity does not begin with humans; it begins wherever any cut is being held. The gradient runs from chemistry to human life because concept-making accumulates, not because it switches on somewhere.

This is where being and subject part company. Being arises through differentiation (P0) — but differentiation alone could be static, a difference that leaves no trace. A subject is a being whose activity turns trace into retention. The trace accumulates: retention becomes memory, memory enables interpretation, interpretation builds reality. A subject's reality is the totality of its accumulated retention. That is P2: language is the medium through which a subject experiences reality.

And historicity does not stop at the individual subject. Sharing through language requires something accumulated to share — a subject without history has nothing to bring to the encounter. Subjects that share a language can synchronise their retention, and through synchronisation a shared reality forms. But that is the next post: [human reality](/blog/2026/05/seed-and-human-reality/).

One thing to notice about how we see reality through our languages. Classical science built time-symmetric laws — equations that work the same forwards and backwards. Entropy gives time a direction, but only statistically. [Rovelli](/positioning/persons/r/rovelli/)'s work on memory and time suggests that what we call the direction of time may be inseparable from the retention that constitutes subjects. The arrow of time might not be in the physics — it might be in the historicity. Something to hold lightly, but it is worth noticing how deeply the seed's account of retention connects to open questions in physics.

The river keeps flowing. The waters keep changing. And wherever something retains the trace of what flowed through it, a subject is already there — experiencing, accumulating, building reality from within. Historicity is where the seed comes alive.
