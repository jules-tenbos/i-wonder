Having the seed as foundation offers unique opportunities. There is already so much implicitly present in those 6 principles that tells us about the nature of things, without having to speculate. This is the advantage when one starts from an axiomatic base. And why gaining confidence in the principles through careful positioning is so important.

These unique opportunities are the reason that there is a seed section on the site. There are already several interpretations on the site and more will follow. The aim is to slot category theory in as a formal language for the seed — and the more that can be extracted at the foundational level, the more flows directly into that formal layer.

Looking at the seed through human glasses is one of the most important ones. Why? Because we are so anthropocentric, we have difficulties believing that there are viewpoints other than ours. In the light of the seed this is very understandable. Our anthropocentricity is not a personal experience thing, it sits in the shared space. And the seed has some very important things to tell us about that shared space.

P2 is the keystone. As a person I am at the centre of my experience — a body, sensing, always already in some language. A doctor sees pathology where others see discomfort. A musician hears structure where others hear sound. What reaches us comes through the languages we hold — different languages, different reality. In [Language, Experience and Reality](/blog/2026/04/language-and-experience/) I explored this through fire: the flames already labelled, the body absorbing warmth, sound, light before a single conscious thought crosses our mind. That is P2 at work.

But there is a step that is easy to miss, somewhat hidden in P2. Experience is the dynamic — retention in action. What it produces is memory, the stored imprint. Knowledge is the interpretation of memory, in whatever language holds it — and the depth of that interpretation depends on the language available. Reality, then, is the totality after interpretation: the world as it has disclosed itself to you. Different languages, different interpretations, different realities. The pluralism is structural — built into how experience works. This is personal, from within, and it is where the human reading starts.

Now the shared space. It follows from P2 that we never interact directly. Think about it — if all my experience is mediated by language, I never reach you unmediated. What we experience as conversation, as being together, is emergent. One of us acts on the environment — speaks, writes, gestures — and another interacts with the effect and extracts similar information from it. Speaking the same language, in seed terms, means being able to take both sides of the same interaction: producing and receiving, matched. A family builds its sense of what matters around the dinner table this way. A profession builds its standards through decades of this shared practice.

But what happens *between* us when subjects meet? The seed gives the structural fact — we don't interact directly. What that leaves room for is the next move.
