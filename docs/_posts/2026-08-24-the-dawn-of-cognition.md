Meaning expressed in language, I have been chewing a lot on this lately. What does it take to come to meaning in language? And how come our experience seems always already fully loaded with meaning? How are we able to create such a complex web of meanings that at the same time adds richness to the reality we experience individually?

Let's trace it back from the beginning. It started with the [seed principles](/seed/original/) *P2 Language is the medium through which a subject experiences reality* and *P3 Language is where subjects share knowledge about reality*. Meaning is the hidden currency that is traded at the level of individual experience and when we share with others. SPLectrum translates this in a metaphysics where epistemology is about how we learn from experience and sharing, ontology deals with concepts (in language) of reality and aesthetics is concerned with [the dynamic of meaning and language](/blog/2026/06/splectrums-kind-of-aesthetics/). All of this remains quite abstract.

As a continuation of the journey I started to do research on the more practical side: on process models of the brain, on parallels with the current AI developments, on philosophical areas of interest like enactivism. I found myself surrounded by many things that seemed to point in the same direction but unable to bring them under one umbrella. The penny did drop.

And then a word dropped out: cognition. From the Oxford dictionary: *Cognition is the mental action or process of acquiring knowledge and understanding through thought, experience, and the senses.* This definition has the human mind as the centre of activity. However, at the same time, it reads very much as a process associated with SPLectrum's seed principles. Why did it take me so long? It should have been there all along. There are so many disciplines in science and philosophy with the word cognitive in their name.

Now, what is the relation between meaning and cognition? And here it becomes interesting, because it is twofold. There is meaning as what cognition produces, but also meaning as what cognition works in.

Meaning as what cognition produces. Cognition makes sense of what came in; meaning is that sense. Fits the making-sense position directly, and fits extraction-as-reduction — a distinction held rather than everything held is what having a meaning amounts to.

Meaning as what cognition works in. The medium is already meaning-bearing before any particular extraction. Cognition doesn't confer meaning, it operates where meaning already is. Fits the sandwich better: you can't extract from an unmeaning substrate, since there'd be nothing there to extract.

Cognition in its standard definition is very anthropocentric, and from SPLectrum's point of view this is unnecessary — more of a hindrance than a help. SPLectrum's definition simplifies the entry while keeping its core. There are two changes:

Firstly, the definition talks about acquiring knowledge and understanding through thought, experience and the senses. There are two redundancies here: thought and the senses are part of experience. In fact all experience is built up from the senses, and thoughts arise from meanings derived from sensory input. So acquiring knowledge and understanding through experience will do.

Secondly, why define cognition as a mental action or process? Yes, the brain is where we first encountered it and where the main focus of study has been. But that is in my opinion not a good reason to constrain it. At this moment this has become a barrier to recognising and dealing with other expressions of cognition — e.g. distributed cognition in ant colonies or behaviours exhibited by AI. Maybe more correct would be: an action or process, in language, of acquiring. For SPLectrum's definition, mental can simply be dropped.

That leaves us with: *Cognition is the process of acquiring knowledge and understanding through experience.* The next post will focus on cognition and the seed principles.
