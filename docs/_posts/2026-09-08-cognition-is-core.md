Recently a new core word entered SPLectrum: cognition, with a widened definition and constraints removed. As a process it was placed firmly within the seed: the dynamic process that discloses reality to a subject. Beneath both sits a claim that deserves its own ground: *cognition is not a secondary process.* Nothing is received first and made sense of after. Knowledge and understanding is acquired with the experiencing itself — all in one, not add-on.

The picture being declined is an intuitive one and deserves attention. The world delivers raw material through the senses, there are experiences, there is thought; cognition then comes into action — it sorts it, interprets it, it adds meaning. A sequence of receiving, absorbing and processing. Most textbook diagrams of perception have this shape, and under that interpretation cognition is indeed secondary: important, but downstream.

Within philosophy this picture has been refused by influential philosophers, from several directions at once. One strand of the [turn in Western philosophy](/positioning/seed/the-turn-in-western-philosophy/) traced here before, the long letting-go of the outside view, now walked in the key of cognition. Kant made the opening move: intuitions without concepts are blind — the mind's activity runs *in* all experience, not after it. The phenomenologists radicalised the point. Heidegger: we are always already in the world, with no moment before meaning. Merleau-Ponty: perception arrives whole and already significant, the body doing the work the textbook assigns to inference. Gadamer: all understanding runs on pre-judgement that tradition delivers — the ground of knowing, not its contamination. And the analytic wing reached the same refusal by its own route: Wittgenstein telling us not to think of understanding as a mental process at all; Ryle showing that the inner-act picture ends in regress; Peirce, earlier than either, dissolving the line between perceiving and inferring.

A third current carried the fusion the whole way. Bergson: there is no perception which is not full of memories — the unloaded percept never occurs. James put the relations inside the stream of experience, given there, not added by an act afterwards. Dewey retired the spectator and made knowing a mode of doing. Whitehead generalised furthest of all: every actual entity takes up its world — experience-like process all the way down, with human cognition an elaborated case of something utterly general.

Nor is the refusal a Western possession. [Yogācāra](/positioning/subjects/y/yogacara/) Buddhism analysed experience as arising with no split between grasper and grasped, every perception conditioned by the seeds of past experience. [Abhinavagupta](/positioning/persons/a/abhinavagupta/)'s school held manifestation and awareness strictly inseparable — and named itself, a thousand years ago, the school of recognition. [Zhuangzi](/positioning/persons/z/zhuangzi/)'s cook cuts the ox with nothing deliberative running behind the knife: the knowing is in the doing. The fusion has been stated in every register philosophy possesses.

SPLectrum's seed principles place it inside this family by construction. In *P2: Language is the medium through which a subject experiences reality* there is no pipeline for a secondary process to be a stage of: language, experience and cognition are aspects of one running, and the process runs within the experiencing — no separation, no before. What the family holds, where the resonances lie and where the seed goes further, is gathered point by point in the cognition subject page [Cognition in Core Philosophy](/positioning/subjects/c/cognition/philosophy/core/) and in the close-affinity piece on [cognition and core philosophy](/positioning/seed/cognition/); I decided to create a meta subject on cognition (the SPLectrum definition) so that the different angles on cognition — scattered for historical reasons across science and philosophy — can be brought together over time.

Core, then — not crown. Cognition does not rule experience from somewhere upstream; it runs within it, and was never anywhere else. That is what the widened word was always naming.
