I have been doing quite a few posts just around the SPLectrum seed, and for a good reason. Throught the seed principles I am exploring what is common to all languages, what is common to all of us: personal experience, shared reality, evolving vocabularies and understanding. It opened up a view of what I call interrelational pluralism, and it is opening up a route for a more formal treatment of the relational structure of language. However that is only one side of the coin, there is another side to SPLectrum. 

When talking about the seed, SPLectrum speaks a meta-language of structure. When talking about building from the seed the SPLectrum will speak a 'content' language - and this alongside so many languages in the pluralistic landscape. I think it is very important to have two such distinct sides of the same coin. When we use a content language we use it to express our view - let's say our ontology or epistemology for example. If however at the same time we bring in structural views on the type of language plurarlism start walking out the door and it becomes one big competition.

So, my idea was: where can I be rigid, and where am I just one voice of (hopefully) wisdom amongst many? Be rigid on what we have structurally in common, and pluralistic on what we all have to say. But in what I say I can have well defined principles - it will be the principles of the seed - but not any more with the strict P2 or P4 reference. The metaphysics I want to develop out of the seed will start from a set of core values that resonate with the principles but without the straight jacket.

From the way we are 'thrown in the world' to paraphrase Heidegger, whatever worldview I build has to be from the seat of the subject. That is consistent with the principles. Reality - my reality - is in the way I relate to what is around me. Now, the way I experience that is strongly shaped by my toolset of perception - the language tools given to me by my species, my human community - that give me value number one: belonging. But the on the other hand what I experience is internal to me, I have no way to let other look through my eyes, hear through my ears, of feel through my skin - the second value: privacy. Everyone lives the same, strong resonances on what we share contrasted by the acute privacy of the experience itself. Where does change come from? It can only come from one place: the lived tension between what is shared and what is privately mine - value number three: creativity has its seat in each of us. Creatvity points back to the creational principle P0. It is the tension that lives in all beings between the difference (what make a being a being) and what is held in common. The other side of the coin, three values: belonging, privacy and creativity.

"Value foundation, not foundation foundation" — that's the whole distinction in five words. And "these three I can identify with as primary" is the right warrant: first-person, contingent, the strongest thing the content side has. Simplicity earns its place too — three you can hold at once, not a system.

-----

So the question turns from structure to content. Stand on this ground, let the principles run — subjects experiencing, subjects sharing — and a reality grows, carrying a few values that come with the grain.
A subject makes its reality by mixing what it already holds, so creation is not a gift some have and others lack but what being a subject is. What it makes is its own: produced within, unreachable by anything that needs its uptake and doesn't get it. You can hold tools the whole world shares and still own a reality no one else can enter — privacy in the deep sense, the dignity of a within that is yours. And some of what is yours lives also in others, held in common and shared from within rather than joined or imposed — belonging, the same making one scale up.

Make, own, share — not three goods rationed against each other, but one self-creation showing three faces. Put together they are a single liberating force: you make your reality, it is yours, and you share it without losing it. That is what freedom turns out to be here — not a fourth value, but what the three come to together.
