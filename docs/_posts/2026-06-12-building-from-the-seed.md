I have been doing quite a few posts just around the SPLectrum seed, and for a good reason. I am attempting to give insight into what is common to all of us: personal experience, shared reality, evolving vocabularies and understanding. But this is only one side of SPLetrum - important - where the focus is on the structure that underpins our languages. SPLectrum wants to build its own 'content' language, have its own language in metaphysics. 

In a sense I have been opening doors towards it. The seed on interrelational pluralism laid out a solid bridge from common structural layer to a world view. (values as an arc: belonging, privacy, creativity)

[Unification of the two pronged is simple in essence: start from a minimalist position (Rorty's) and introduce a formal process theory that is not invasive. The seed avoid invasiveness by focussing on common structure and dynamic of language - a meta language that is not in competiion with 'content' languages. SPLectrum is about 'equivalence' and not 'truth' in the absolute sense. The common base is the shared reality, and we stay away from claims beyond. To be non-judgemental about language games, but passionate about your own 'content' language game and your own community without invading others. There is room for community based ethics - what is our shared reality - and judged by those standards 'nature' can be cruel and unforgiven. But we need to appreciate the value of 'our reality', 'our shared reality' and stay away from 'the reality' where the bulldozer may be just around the corner. You choose values by making them part of your life/reality or keeping them out of it. Building from the seed is about how we are 'free to create' the reality we live in.]

The last stretch of the seed has been structural work — saying what kind of thing this framework is before saying much about the world it sees. Three pieces carried most of it: a pluralism of co-arising realities, a two-pronged anti-representationalism, and a process theory built from a single relational primitive. This post sets them down together, as one foundation, and then turns to look at what grows on it.

Brought together, they stop being three positions and become one stance. The anti-representationalism keeps the rest honest: whatever we build is one language among many, never the structure beneath. The process theory supplies the dynamic — distinction, mixing, historicity, a small set of moves that compose without limit. The pluralism is what those moves yield once they run: realities in the plural, co-arising, none of them THE reality. Each leans on the other two. That interlock is what's ours — a minimalist process theory of the common structure of language, that knows it is one language among many, and that issues in a plural world rather than a single true one.

What's worth noticing about that foundation is what it refuses to do. It is strict about structure and silent about content. It describes a common dynamic; it competes with no one's account of the world. A meta-language that imposes nothing — including on what rises from it. So it doesn't hand you a reality. It clears a space. The same ground holds whatever you raise on it, including realities that deny everything it stands on, and that is not a flaw to be patched: it is why what we build is a choice rather than a verdict handed down.

So the question turns from structure to content. Stand on this ground, let the principles run — subjects experiencing, subjects sharing — and a reality grows, carrying a few values that come with the grain.
A subject makes its reality by mixing what it already holds, so creation is not a gift some have and others lack but what being a subject is. What it makes is its own: produced within, unreachable by anything that needs its uptake and doesn't get it. You can hold tools the whole world shares and still own a reality no one else can enter — privacy in the deep sense, the dignity of a within that is yours. And some of what is yours lives also in others, held in common and shared from within rather than joined or imposed — belonging, the same making one scale up.

Make, own, share — not three goods rationed against each other, but one self-creation showing three faces. Put together they are a single liberating force: you make your reality, it is yours, and you share it without losing it. That is what freedom turns out to be here — not a fourth value, but what the three come to together.

From here the seed stops laying its floor and starts building its reality. What that reality looks like — the world SPLectrum sees, and the way it sees it — is the work ahead. This post is the doorway in.
