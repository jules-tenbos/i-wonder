[The previous post](/blog/2026/05/the-birth-of-p0-being-is-tension/) explored P0 as a creational principle of being. But being implies language, every differentiation creates both at once. In this post I want to focus on the language side of that creation. 

In [Language, Experience and Reality](/blog/2026/04/language-and-experience/) I made the point that everything we experience is already labelled with meaning. For instance what we see is wrapped in concepts that are part of a language, something relational. We experience not being able to make sense of stuff as confusing, even frightening. Although this labeled experience is personal, the concept labels themselves are nearly always in a shared language, from shared reality. We just assume we see the same things, something objective. But think about it — that is by convention, because we agreed on it. Ultimately, every experience of such concepts and relations remains deeply personal and is even not necessarily shared.

When something new is discovered, it makes its way from personal experience into the shared space. A new act of creation must be added through language to shared reality. Not the creation itself, but the language that expresses it. And there is no prescribed recipe — it could have happened differently. The act of creation is private and contingent.

This is where [Brandom](/positioning/persons/b/brandom/) sharpens the picture. Language is not representation — it is commitment. Meaning lives not in what a word stands for but in what follows from it, what is incompatible with it, what you commit to when you use it. To assert is to take a position in a network of moves. Other speakers respond, challenge, keep score. Meaning is relational — position in a web, not a label on a thing. P1 in its sharpest form: language is relational, all the way down.

And this contingency is not a defect. [Rorty](/positioning/persons/r/rorty/) saw this clearly: there is no language that escapes its own contingency by being more careful, more rigorous, more formal. Every language is the language of the cut that produced it. It tells us what it tells us — no more. [Philosophy as conversation](/blog/2026/03/philosophy-as-conversation/), not mirror-holding. No privileged vocabulary, no final description. Just the ongoing creation of new ways to relate.

It reminds me of the discussion around [RQM](/blog/2026/05/the-relational-reality-of-rqm/) — how do the local interactions (measurements) get shared? How do we deal with this when there is no outside view to referee from? The answer there will eventually be the same: convergence among participants, not correspondence with something above them.

Now think about the following: our human language — our way of thinking — has been evolving through personal experience and sharing for thousands of years. We don't just think anything. We are preconditioned. But that should not feel like a constraint. Aside from what we share, the freedom of personal experience is yours if you choose to avail of it. And that is what people with new ideas do. To understand something is to have created a language for it — borrowing helps, but the act of creation cannot be skipped.

It is the broad sense of language that runs through the seed. Cellular signalling, gravitational binding, a footprint in mud — each is a way of relating. Human communication is only one species in a much wider genus. The seed does not start with human language and generalise outward. It starts with the relational and finds human language within it. However, as a human you only create and understand through human language. Likewise for other subjects — forms of life — they create and understand in their own language.

And if every language is contingent on its own creation, no language can claim to be the one that got it right. There is no master vocabulary, no privileged description of how things really are. Each language accesses what its differentiations make available — real, but partial. Always partial. This is where pluralism stops being a moral position and becomes a structural fact. P4: languages have equal standing in potential. Not because we chose tolerance, but because it is the nature of language.

The plurality is fertile ground. Languages that interrelate create overlaps. Overlaps are where understanding forms — and at the fringes of the shared, new differentiations arise. New language, new territory, new being. P0 fires again. The web grows by creation: each new language opens new possibilities for interrelation, each interrelation opens new possibilities for creation. P5: a web of growing complexity. This is the creative engine that interrelational pluralism names — but that is for a later post.

One thing is left open. Language as described here could still be static — a structure that exists but goes nowhere. What happens when the relational activity leaves a trace? When it accumulates? That is where the subject enters, and with it experience and reality. That is the next post: historicity.
