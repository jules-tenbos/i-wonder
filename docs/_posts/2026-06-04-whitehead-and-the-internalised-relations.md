Reality is not made of enduring substances but of events that arise, achieve their satisfaction, and perish, each one taking up what came before and contributing to what comes after. Nothing exists in isolation — every actual entity is constituted by its relations to other actual entities.

This in a nutshell describes Whiteheads philosophy of organisms. His theory is an interesting one for what it does and doesn't do. 

It was inspired by the new physics - quantummechanics - but is expressed using particle envelopes..

His language is not very accessible, one could say that it takes affort to get into. He is worthy to sit on the bench next to Hegel.

The complexity of the his theory lies in its ontological treatment of the relational. 

He calls prehension how one actual entity takes account of another. Prehensions are the “concrete facts of relatedness” — the relations are not external to the entities; they constitute them. This whole move internalise the relational, locks it up in entities. But it describes something outside the entities - how they relate. What he in fact most against was was an outside actor driving the relational dynamics, the process.



For Husserl, transcendental subjectivity is the singular site at which world appears — it is foundational, structuring, and (controversially) on some readings still asymmetrical with respect to nature. For Whitehead, something like subjectivity goes all the way down: every actual entity has its own perspective, its own felt taking-up of its world. This is his panexperientialism, and it is a far more radical democratisation of mindedness than anything in Husserl. You could put it this way: Husserl gives us a phenomenology of human consciousness as the place where being shows up; Whitehead gives us a cosmology in which a kind of proto-experience is a feature of every event.

Their accounts of relation are interestingly close and interestingly different. Husserl's intentionality says consciousness is always consciousness of something — relation is built into the structure of awareness. Whitehead's prehension says every actual entity is constituted by its taking-account-of others — relation is built into the structure of being. Both are relational thinkers and both reject the self-enclosed Cartesian substance. But Husserl's relation is intentional and meaning-laden in a way that requires consciousness, while Whitehead's prehension is broader, encompassing what he calls "causal feeling" — the way a stone, in a stripped-down sense, takes account of its environment.

So Whitehead is the curious case of an explicitly anti-representational, relational philosopher whose metaphysical mechanism re-imports representationalism through the back door. He wants relations first and entities derivative — but he organises the relations by housing them inside entities, and once a relation is housed inside one of its relata it has become an internal representation of the other relatum.
