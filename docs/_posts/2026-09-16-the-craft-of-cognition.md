Hand someone the complete equipment of sculpture — the chisels, the technique, the accumulated tradition of the workshop — and something essential has still not been handed over: the knowing-how of expressing *in* sculpture. That is acquired in the doing, and it is nobody else's. No two sculptors cut alike, and the difference is not in the toolbox.

Every process has two sides: how it is implemented — the nuts and bolts — and how it is run, what is done with it. Cognition is no exception. *The process of acquiring knowledge and understanding through experience* names not only an implementation that runs but a running with a manner of its own — and the manner is itself acquired. Among the things cognition acquires, through experience, is the knowing-how of its own running.

The previous posts placed cognition [within the seed](/seed/cognition/): the process that runs as a subject experiences reality, the process in whose running reality is disclosed. This post follows the second angle to what it implies. If cognition is the process that discloses reality, then how the process is run has a big influence on the type of reality that is disclosed. Two subjects can hold the same tooling — the same language, the same tradition, the same workshop — and still live in different realities. The difference is the hand.

Followed this far, the how turns out to be aesthetic territory. Working with meaning to create meaning, the manner of going about using what one holds — this is the ground SPLectrum's [aesthetics](/reality/aesthetics/) named as expression of meaning. One process, read by two pillars: as disclosure it is the heart of [epistemology](/reality/epistemology/); as craft it runs deep into aesthetics. The journey from one to the other is no distance at all — it is the same running, seen from its two faces.

And here the position can be said in full. Reality is disclosed — and the disclosing is relational: it happens in the interaction between being and the other, each subject's process running its side of that interaction in its own hands. But the running never starts from nothing. Every act of the craft is a variation to an existing tune — the creativity of a subject adds to an existing building, it does not build something completely new. The tooling arrives already loaded with others' acquiring; the tune was playing first. The [core values](/reality/core-values/) turn out to be the anatomy of this one process: belonging funds it, privacy is where it runs, creativity is how it goes.

And the craft is not exercised on the world from a distance. The subject is in the world, not detached from it, and the subject's attitude sets the tone of the interaction. The deepest part of the sculptor's knowing-how is knowing what not to carve — and so with cognition: choose wisely what you allow within your reality, because the knowledge and understanding you acquire and run with disclose the reality you will have to live in.

Which leaves the tune itself. Its historicity runs long, but the tune is no remnant of a distant past — it is alive, played and reshaped by living subjects, every one of us included. Making peace with belonging is coming to terms with a living thing, and it is not for everyone easy — creativity wants to feel entirely one's own, and discovering that it runs on inherited tooling can feel like a debt, or a cage. It is neither. The tune is not what limits the variation; it is what makes variation possible at all. The building is not what constrains the new wing; it is what holds it up.

That is the position, and it fits in a sentence: with the help of belonging, we are creative in private.
