I started a seed section on the site because I thought it was important to separate what can be deduced from the principles from what gets built on top of it. Even if that means the section is small. How wrong I was, I had no idea of the multitude of things that follow from the seed principles. 10+ pages? Not a problem.

This time the focus is on human reality. Why? Because we are very anthropocentric, our reality starts with being human. On the downside we end up having difficulties realising that there are viewpoints other than ours. By default we seem to happily dismiss what we don't understand. And there is good reason for this blinkered approach: our toolbox to experience reality is after all the human toolbox! As I discussed in my previous post [on language and historicity](/blog/2026/05/the-seed-and-language/) our language, form of life is completely shaped by our evolution into human beings, our historicity, our anthropocentrism.

OK, our anthropocentricity sets the nature of our shared space. And this then feeds back in our personal experience. As a person I am at the centre of my experience — a body, sensing, always already in some language. A doctor sees pathology where others see discomfort. A musician hears structure where others hear sound. What reaches us comes through the languages we hold — different languages, different reality. In [Language, Experience and Reality](/blog/2026/04/language-and-experience/) I explored this through fire: the flames already labelled, the body absorbing warmth, sound, light before a single conscious thought crosses our mind. Experience is personal and private, but soaked in human ancestry.

This makes it easy for something to be missed: we can shape our experience, influence what makes it into our reality. Experience is the dynamic — retention in action. It produces memory, the stored imprint which we then go on to interpret into knowledge, in whatever language holds it — and the nature of that interpretation depends on the language applied. Depending on the language that we foster internally we shape that path into knowledge. Reality, then, is the totality after interpretation: the world as it has disclosed itself to you. Different languages, different interpretations, different realities. The pluralism is structural and starts with personal choice — it is built into how experience works. This is where the human reading starts. This determines what we bring to the table when we share.

Now the shared space. It follows from P2 that we never interact directly. Think about it — if all my experience is mediated by language, I never reach you unmediated. What we experience as conversation, as being together, is emergent. One of us acts on the environment — speaks, writes, gestures — and another interacts with the effect and extracts similar information from it. Speaking the same language, in seed terms, means being able to take both sides of the same interaction: producing and receiving, matched. A family builds its sense of what matters around the dinner table this way. A profession builds its standards through decades of this shared practice.

But what happens *between* us when subjects meet? The seed gives the structural fact — we don't interact directly. What that leaves room for is the next move.
