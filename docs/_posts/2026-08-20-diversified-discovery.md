If creation conforms to discovery — the discovery of relational paths that were available but hadn't been walked yet — then how discovery is organised matters. One explorer walks one path. That path makes certain things visible and leaves the rest in the dark. Extended cognition — AI that extends me — walks my corridor further, faster. Useful. Genuinely useful. But still one corridor. The breadth of what can be discovered is limited by the position from which the discovery starts.

Two explorers walking different paths discover more than the sum of their individual discoveries. This is not arithmetic. Each discovery changes the landscape of what is discoverable next — a discovered path makes adjacent paths visible that were not visible before. The discoveries interact. They open each other's horizons. Diversification — different positions, different languages, different starting points — is how discovery works in a relational space too vast for any single position to see the whole. Not a strategy for hedging bets. How discovery operates.

P4 — languages have equal standing in potential — is not philosophical courtesy. It tracks how discovery works. Privilege one language, one approach, one perspective, and you walk one corridor. That corridor may be deep and well-lit, but it is one corridor. Multiple languages walk multiple corridors. And the corridors connect — because the relational space they traverse is the same space, approached from different positions. Equal standing is not a value judgement. It is the condition under which diversified discovery operates.

The inverse holds. Monoculture — biological, cultural, linguistic, technological — reduces discovery. One path walked repeatedly discovers nothing new. The same corridor, the same view, the same findings, reinforced until they feel like the whole picture. This isn't a moral argument. It's a question of how discovery works. The centralised platform that routes everyone through the same corridor isn't wrong in an ethical sense. It is narrow in the discovery sense — it reduces the number of paths being walked.

Decentralisation, then, is not an ethical position or a political programme. It is diversified discovery — the natural organisation of exploration in a relational space where no single position sees everything. The architecture the engineering already builds takes this shape. Multiple processes, multiple positions, multiple paths through the same fabric. The pattern emerges from the relational space itself, not because someone decided cooperation is a virtue. The relational space rewards diversification because that is how discovery works.
