You hear it in the DAO governance debates, the public-goods funding conversations, the ReFi proposals, the network-state pitches. Someone is making the case for decentralised coordination — and at some point the vocabulary shifts. Nature cooperates. Organisms form mutual arrangements. The biological pattern is invoked as evidence: this is how living systems work, and we should build the same way.

The observations are real. [Mutualism](/positioning/subjects/m/mutualism/) saw real things — decentralised arrangements in living systems, diversity sustaining itself, self-organisation without central command. [Kropotkin](/positioning/persons/k/kropotkin/)'s *Mutual Aid* (1902) remains one of the most sustained evidence-based arguments for cooperation as a factor in evolution. When people reach for this vocabulary, they are reaching for something with substance behind it.

But the vocabulary carries a colouring, and the colouring bites.

## The path is a command chain

The Web 3 landscape is full of attempts at decentralisation, and each follows the same path. A design team identifies the right governance framework. Tokenomics prescribes what incentives should look like. Protocols compete over which lending model, which curve, which oracle design is correct. Public-goods funding mechanisms each attempt the right coordination structure. Network states pitch intent-design at the scale of polity.

Each is a real attempt. Each follows the same structure: *identify the right arrangement, build toward it, judge participation against it*. The decentralisation lives in the protocol layer; the originating-and-implementing layer is concentrated. The path is a command chain, even when the destination is named decentralised.

The colouring is not in the destination. It is in the path.

## An old game in new clothes

This is not new. The [mutualism tradition](/positioning/subjects/m/mutualism/social-and-economic-thought/) has been arguing with itself in exactly this mode since the 1840s.

Marx attacks [Proudhon](/positioning/persons/p/proudhon/)'s free-credit programme; Proudhon rejects political action. Both are prescribing arrangements; they disagree on which. A century later, Rothbard claims the American individualists for anarcho-capitalism and Carson reclaims them for free-market anti-capitalism — the same source material, opposite prescriptions, both insisting they are doing structural analysis.

The question "is mutualism more Marxist or more free-market?" stays entirely inside the colouring. Both sides play the same game; the dispute is over which arrangement, and the frame is shared. What is happening in Web 3 governance debates right now is structurally identical — the colouring persists because the vocabulary carries it.

## Patterns emerge from configurations

Cooperatives do not succeed because the founders had the right ethical commitments. They succeed where conditions support them. The Rochdale Pioneers (1844) succeeded; Owen's New Harmony (1825) failed. Same Owenite formation, similar commitments, different configurations — Rochdale had density, shared need, and a dividend mechanism that aligned incentives; New Harmony designed a complete utopian community from first principles into a sparsely populated frontier.

Uniswap outran Bancor and the alternative-curve designs not through better ethics or better intent, but because its simplicity happened to fit the composability configuration DeFi needed at that moment. Mutual banks failed in France under Proudhon and thrived in Germany under Raiffeisen and in Quebec under Desjardins. Same idea; different configurations; different outcomes.

The intent-frame reads success and failure as moral or analytical questions — *did they get the design right?* The answer lives in configurations the agents did not design and largely did not control.

## Sweet-spots are transient

The intent stance wants the sustained right structure — find the design that works, institutionalise it, defend it. But beneficial configurations are transient. The Rochdale principles worked in mid-nineteenth-century Lancashire; they do not transfer cleanly to platform cooperativism today. Protocols canonical in one Web 3 cycle are deprecated by the next; DAOs that raised eight figures go dormant within a few years; even the AMM lead has fragmented across chains rather than settling.

The intent-frame reads this as crisis — "Web 3 has failed." The dynamic reads it as how the landscape evolves. Most Owenite communities failed. TCP/IP became universal through the displacement of X.25, IPX, AppleTalk and many others. Stripping out the failures would have made the emergence impossible.

## Convergence without the command chain

The alternative to design-then-adopt is convergence through shared language. The Linux kernel community, Wikipedia, scientific communities at their working best: coordination through shared vocabulary that allows independent action from each contributor's own ground. No one instructs what to build. Convergence is on the shared language of the problem space, and it won the early web through processes its designers did not control.

The catch: making explicit vocabulary for a domain is a different act from designing the arrangement the vocabulary will produce. The first is conditions-for-emergence work. The second is intent-style prescription. They look alike from a distance; they are structurally different.

## What changes when the colouring is set aside

Not less committed engagement — more committed engagement, in a different mode.

Don't funnel. The intent-move is to identify the right design and recruit toward it; the alternative is many attempts in parallel, no canonical answer. Encourage diversity knowing the dropout rate will be high, because phases of loss are how configurations shift, not a problem to engineer out.

Concentrate on the fertile ground instead — shared substrate, shared vocabulary developing across the space, low cost to try. Work on what makes attempts possible, not on what they should look like. And use what is already there: the existing configuration, centralised platforms and capital flows included, is part of the ground rather than the enemy. There is a direction; the route to it is evolutionary from what exists, not built from blueprint.

The mutualism vocabulary wants to make the case — cooperation is natural, centralisation is unnatural, build accordingly. Setting the colouring aside does not mean setting the direction aside. It means engaging with what is actually there, enabling what can emerge from it, and holding the direction through meta-conditional values — equal standing, openness — rather than through prescribed arrangements.
