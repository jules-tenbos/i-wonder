Having the seed as foundation offers unique opportunities. There is already so much implicitly present in those 6 principles that tells us about the nature of things, without having to speculate. This is the advantage when one starts from an axiomatic base. And why gaining confidence in the principles through careful positioning is so important.

These unique opportunities are the reason that there is a seed section on the site. There are already several interpretations on the site and more will follow. The main reason for looking in different directions just from the seed is because I want to match up the principles of the seed with category theory, to slot in a formal language. The more that can be said just from the seed, the more there is for category theory to work with.

Looking at the seed through human glasses is one of the most important ones. Why? Because we are so anthropocentric, we have difficulties believing that there are viewpoints other than ours. In the light of the seed this is very understandable. Our anthropocentricity is not a personal experience thing, it sits in the shared space. And the seed has some very important things to tell us about that shared space.

P2 is the keystone. As a person I am at the centre of my experience — a body, sensing, always already in some language. A doctor sees pathology where others see discomfort. A musician hears structure where others hear sound. What reaches us comes through the languages we hold — different languages, different reality. In [Language, Experience and Reality](/blog/2026/04/language-and-experience/) I explored this through fire: the flames already labelled, the body absorbing warmth, sound, light before a single conscious thought crosses our mind. That is P2 at work.

But there is a step the seed makes explicit that is easy to miss: for experience to exist there must be retention. A lasting imprint. Without it, interaction leaves no trace — there is nothing to accumulate. Reality, in this reading, is the accumulation from a life of experience. The world as it has disclosed itself to me. Knowledge is its insight factor. This is personal, from within, and it is where the human reading starts.

Now the shared space. It follows from P2 that we never interact directly. Think about it — if all my experience is mediated by language, I never reach you unmediated. What we experience as conversation, as being together, is emergent. One of us acts on the environment — speaks, writes, gestures — and another interacts with the effect and extracts similar information from it. Speaking the same language, in seed terms, means being able to take both sides of the same interaction: producing and receiving, matched. A family builds its sense of what matters around the dinner table this way. A profession builds its standards through decades of this shared practice.

Sharing, then, is the synchronisation of language between us. And knowledge is what we are able to transfer given the level of synchronisation achieved — the more aligned, the more can pass through. This is why anthropocentrism sits in the shared space and not in the personal: it is the product of human communities synchronising their languages over millennia, reinforcing the same perspective until it feels like the only one. Not a personal bias — a communal achievement.

Through this synchronisation a shared realm forms. What a family, a profession, a culture holds as real is what its members have converged on. And this is where P0 comes back into the picture. The group that has synchronised enough to share a reality is a community — and its formation is an act of creation. It draws a boundary: us and them, baked into the differentiation. Not hostility — structure. There is no "us" without what is not us.

Once sharing brings language to life, something further follows. Where languages overlap, that overlap becomes a starting point — new language can grow from it. Medicine emerges from the overlap of biology and practice. A pidgin from two languages meeting in a port. P4 is the creational principle for languages themselves: every overlap is a potential origin. And none of these languages is privileged in principle, however different in current reach.

The human web evolves through this cycle. We synchronise, knowledge transfers, new languages emerge from the overlap, and those in turn synchronise with others. The complexity is in the relating, not in any single community. And the seed tells us all of this — without speculation, from six principles.
