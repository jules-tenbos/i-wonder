When something new shows up, we file it. Three drawers, and everyone knows the labels. *Discovered* — it was there all along, and someone finally found it: a continent, a comet, penicillin. *Invented* — it was not there, and someone made it: the telephone, the sonnet, the wheel. *Evolved* — nobody made it, it grew on its own: the eye, the market, the language you are reading. The filing system is so familiar it does not feel like a system. It feels like a fact about the world.

Then you look closer, and the drawers leak. Calculus: did Newton discover it or invent it? Mathematicians have argued both sides for three centuries, which is a long time for a filing question. Penicillin was discovered — but the systematic method that found it was invented, so what exactly happened in that petri dish? A new word is invented by no one in particular and evolves through everyone in general. The longer you look, the more the drawers turn out to hold each other's contents.

They leak because they are not three mechanisms. They are three seats on one motion. The motion is always the same: a relational path that was available but unwalked gets walked — a [discovery](/reality/discovery/), strictly, every time; we only ever stumble across what the space already held. But the face the motion shows depends on where you sit. From the walker's seat, it is *invention*: nothing was there, I made it — the truest description of how creating feels, and real as experience, even though the from-nothing part was never true. Described from the structure, it is *discovery*: what was always possible, now expressed. And watched from far enough back that no walker is visible any more — many paths, many walkers, no author — it is *evolution*: the same walking, continuing through whoever walks.

Notice what this does for evolution in particular. The word is usually made to work three jobs — Darwinian, cultural, cosmological — and the arguments about which is the "real" evolution never end. From here they dissolve: those are not three senses to adjudicate, just three distances at which you can watch the no-seat face run. Evolution does not have to stretch to cover everything. Something else already covers everything.

That something is the value behind all three seats: [creativity](/reality/core-values/) — the generative motion the previous post put on the table. This series was nearly called *evolution*, and the rename was the point: one face cannot name the value it is a face of. And the faces are not a closed set of three. Wherever a subject acts and reality comes out different from how it went in — down to something as quiet as re-choosing what you belong to — it is the same value at work. There is even a face turned inward, creativity working on the subject's own reality; that one has its own story, for later in the series' neighbourhood.

So: one value, one motion, three familiar faces, and an open door. What the series does next is take the seats one at a time — starting with the seat where the filing system bites hardest and the illusion is sweetest: the walker's own. What actually happens when we create? The next post takes that one on directly.
