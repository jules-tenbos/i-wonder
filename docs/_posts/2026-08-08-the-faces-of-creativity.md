When something new shows up, we file it. Three drawers, and everyone knows the labels. *Discovered* — it was there all along, and someone finally found it: a continent, a comet, penicillin. *Invented* — it was not there, and someone made it: the telephone, the sonnet, the wheel. *Evolved* — nobody made it, it grew on its own: the eye, the market, the language you are reading. The filing system is so familiar it does not feel like a system. It feels like a fact about the world.

But look at the two confident labels and both overreach. *Discovered* says it was already there — but a continent unsighted, a theorem unproven, is not *there* the way a found thing is there; it is there in potential, waiting on the subject who brings it into reality. And *invented* says it came from nothing — but nothing comes from nothing. You cannot invent what was not already relationally possible; the medium had to be ready for it, the tools to hand, before appropriate use could bring it out. Neither drawer describes what happens. Both mistake a potential for either a thing already sitting there or a thing conjured from thin air.

Then you look closer, and the drawers leak. Calculus: did Newton discover it or invent it? Mathematicians have argued both sides for three centuries, which is a long time for a filing question. Penicillin was discovered — but the systematic method that found it was invented, so what exactly happened in that petri dish? A new word is invented by no one in particular and evolves through everyone in general. The longer you look, the more the drawers turn out to hold each other's contents.

They leak because they are not three mechanisms. They are three seats on one motion. The motion is always the same: a relational path is available but unwalked, and a subject walks it — finds the right key and turns it, and a door that could have opened now has. What was potential becomes real in the walking, not before it. This is [discovery](/reality/discovery/) in the strict sense: not stumbling across a thing that sat there finished, but bringing into reality a possibility the medium was holding. And the face the motion shows depends on where you sit. From the walker's seat it is *invention*: nothing was there, I made it — the truest description of how creating feels, and real as experience, even though nothing came from nothing; the medium had to be ready. Described from the structure it is *discovery*: a relation that was possible, now actual. And watched from far enough back that no walker is visible any more — many paths, many walkers, no author — it is *evolution*: the same walking, continuing through whoever, or whatever, walks. One motion, one metaphysics: a potential held in the medium, brought into reality by a subject who walks it. The three drawers were never three kinds of thing — only three distances from the one. That claim — creation as the walking of an unwalked path, and why the from-nothing picture has to go — is the whole of the next post; here it is enough to see that the drawers leak because of it.

Notice what this does for evolution in particular. The word is usually made to work three jobs — Darwinian, cultural, cosmological — and the arguments about which is the "real" evolution never end. From here they dissolve: those are not three senses to adjudicate, just three distances at which you can watch the no-seat face run. Evolution does not have to stretch to cover everything. Something else already covers everything.

That something is the value behind all three seats: [creativity](/reality/core-values/) — the generative motion the previous post put on the table. This series was nearly called *evolution*, and the rename was the point: one face cannot name the value it is a face of.

But mark what creativity is *not*. It is the engine of change, and it is tempting to hear that as an engine of improvement — change as progress, each turn a step up. It is nothing of the kind. The motion that builds is the motion that destroys; the same walk that opens one door shuts others, and what it brings into reality can be ruin as readily as gift. Creativity is the destructive as much as the constructive — that is not a flaw in it, it is its nature. So change carries no value of its own. Nothing is good merely because it is new. The value is in the creating, in the walking; whether a given change is worth having is a separate verdict, passed later and from outside. Miss this and the whole picture tilts back into the progress story the rename was meant to leave behind.

And the faces are not a closed set of three. Look past *who or what made it* to what the change itself is like, and more faces show. There is the sheer *parallelism* of it: change is potential in every experience, everywhere, all at once — not the rare double-discovery of Newton-and-Leibniz but the standing condition, countless paths being walked across reality at every moment, most of them by no one watching. And there is *error*: the walk taken wrongly still walks a real path. Fleming's dish was a mistake, a contamination he did not aim for, and it opened a door all the same — the motion does not require the walker to have meant it. Neither of these is a fourth or fifth drawer. They are the same value seen from further out, once you stop asking which seat and start asking what change, in the wide view, is actually like.

The reach runs further than the human case, too. *Subject* does not mean *person*: the motion asks only for a subject with agency, primed by the common tools of its kind — which carries the value deep into other forms of life, other mammals, plants and fungi, all the way down toward the single cell. How far down it runs is a walk of its own; here it is enough that the door opens in that direction too. There is even a face turned inward, creativity working on the subject's own reality; that one has its own story, for later in the series' neighbourhood.

So: one value, one motion, many faces. The creativity series takes us on a journey that visits those many faces - some expected, others not so.
