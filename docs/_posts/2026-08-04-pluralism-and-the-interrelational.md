A while ago when I was analysing the seed principles I wrote the [From Seed to Interrelational Pluralism](/blog/2026/05/from-seed-to-interrelational-pluralism/) post calling SPLectrum the home of Interrelational Pluralism. That may have been seen as early days to put such a stake down. As the conversations continued I recently outlined SPLectrum's metaphysical position and thought it the right moment to come back to the subject of pluralism and expand a bit further on SPLectrum's difference.

Pluralism is not one doctrine but a family, and philosophy kept joining it by accident. Ask what reality is made of, and some answered: many kinds of things, not one substance. Ask how we know, and some answered: many valid ways, no single method. Ask about language: many games, none ranking another. Ask about the good: many values that genuinely conflict and will not be ranked on one scale. Ask about power: many centres, dispersed, no container holding all authority. Five different questions, asked in different centuries by people who mostly never read each other — and the same conclusion every time. Many, none privileged. What strikes me most about this landscape is that it consists of a number of similar tracks but never joined up.

So where do the tracks join? At the level none of them stopped to look at: language. Each track asked its own question — about substance, about knowing, about the good, about power — and each got its answer the same way, by asking, answering and sharing through language. Five different questions produced the same shape of answer because all five were probing the same medium without knowing it. That is the join: a common how, carried by many languages of equal standing. We all take from the same source, but brew our own.

Equal standing is not a conclusion it argued its way to — it is baked into how the position is built, there from the first move. That is why it names itself into the family: interrelational pluralism. And the first word carries what it adds. The family, by and large, states a count: there are many. Interrelational pluralism states a relation: how the many stand toward each other — same source, own brew, none above the rest.

I like to throw some more clarity on how those tracks are joined first by looking at the borders of pluralism and next by the disputes within the pluralist family.

Pluralism has two borders: on the one side there is monism - single measure, one substance, one method, one good - and the other side relativism - anything goes.
Pluralism defines itself in contrast to monism, but has an internal debate on the kind of pluralism - value pluralism with Isaiah Berlin, or James' pluralistic universe. SPLectrum takes one step back and defines its pluralism at the language level - equal standing. Each language - read culture, community - has within its language game freedom of expression, no imposition. It is only when coexistence comes into play that common understanding and convergence/divergence comes into play. 

And this leads us directly in to the second border - relativism.
If there are many and none is privileged does anything go? No, for two reasons. First subjective convergence within one's own community And second, the dynamic of coexistence between communities will explore convergence/divergence from their base of common understanding. There is no relativism - what would it be compared with - but an internal dynamic that drives convergence. Reality starts in private with each subject already belonging and the private is then shared back.

The pluralist claim of no privileged view is often refuted as being itself a privileged view. But I think this is a confused stance. From a SPLectrum point of view there is no privileged view, all languages are of equal standing. There is freedom of expression of reality - the what. But there is a privileged view in the sense that everyone has reality experienced through the medium of language. We share the same structural approach. The seed principles are where SPLectrum sees us all as unified in the way we experience and share reality.

A single approach on the how, an equal standing approach on the what.

I find the realism question with pluralism the most interesting, and it is here that I stop arguing and start showing where I stand. The realism question: if there are many valid accounts and none is privileged, can any of them be called real — or does "many, none privileged" collapse into "none really true, it's all just perspective"? To begin with a few questions: what is a valid account? what does really true mean? Doesn't that depend on the language one speaks, how meaning is expressed? What is the standard for an absolute viewpoint apart from 'because I say so'? I say: have confidence in the many and value the creativity of each. Around us we see strong evidence of the power of subjective convergence, in the strength of identity of humans, animals and plants alike. They all belong, and each member individually lives it.

On the question of how many realities are genuinely held SPLectrum has a simple answer: as many realities as there are subjects. And probably more in the intended meaning of the question: how many shared realities are being held? A lot less and subject realities are strongly clustered around these realities: all subjects belong. Subjective convergence is everywhere. Belonging is a big part of our identity. Will it eventually converge into one, is there a world out there independently? I don't know, and I don't think it matters. Why should our accounts have to be answerable to something that is not known?

There is one pluralist thinker that SPLectrum has close affinity with, and this is Putnam. An upcoming post will explore that affinity.

That is the position, restated where it lives. It does not ask to be won as an argument. It asks to be read — the way it reads everything else: a landscape, one reality among many, making more.
