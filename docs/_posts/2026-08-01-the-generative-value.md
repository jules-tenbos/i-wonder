This post marks a new beginning: the beginning of the creativity series. It is a breakout from the reality series, because it deals with one specific aspect — the dynamic of change. Change happens in many places and in many different ways, but it always traces back to where reality is experienced: to the subject.

By now you will know that my blog posts are conversations about my thoughts, and that I like to develop them step by step. Never mind the far corners I want to reach — they will have to be reached one step at a time. Here it is no different. Creativity at the human level is the easy first step into insight, and it sets us up for the journey into the non-anthropocentric.

SPLectrum holds creativity as one of the [three core values](/reality/core-values/) of the subject: belonging, privacy, creativity. A subject takes its nature — the tools it uses to experience reality — from belonging, but that does not mean reality is experienced identically across all subjects. Each subject experiences in private, applies the tools slightly differently, has its own view on reality. Subjects sharing back these differences is the driver that all sources of change have in common.

I think the interplay — creativity as difference made in private, and its feedback into the community — is aptly illustrated by a musician playing music from a score. The shared tools are plentiful: the instrument, the notes on the score, the skill to play the instrument, the tradition of how to play. All of it arrives from the shared side — little or none of it is the musician's own. But the musician makes it his own, plays as an individual. Many don't go beyond what was shared with them; others take the music to new grounds. And some of these innovations of performance make it all the way back, become standards for others to follow. Individual creativity generates a dynamic of change — creativity as the generative value.

The impact of a single individual, one subject, can be enormous. Here not only on the fellow musicians, but on the audiences in ever wider circles. What started in the privacy of individual experience eventually belongs to countless many. Just imagine: it all started thanks to the agency of a single subject.

That was the whole loop — the difference made, shared back, adopted. This first post claims only the beginning of it: creativity is the difference a subject makes in private with what it received. Not the sharing, not the adoption — those are what the difference can go on to do. And it is not the art department of life: the child bending a word until it fits, the cook who stops following the recipe — wherever a subject takes in the shared and gives back something its own, the value is at work.

Narrower still: the difference is simply what comes out other than expected — no value judgement in it. Good, skilful, a success: those are verdicts the outside passes later. In the seat where they are made, the masterstroke and the slip are the same kind of thing — embryonic creativity, raw material before the sorting begins. That is the generative value, kept to the human case. From outside, only the small part that surfaces is ever seen, and the outside has names for it: discovery, invention, evolution. Those names, and what they sort, are where the series goes next.
