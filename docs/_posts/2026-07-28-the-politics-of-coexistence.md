This is the final post where the metaphysics position is laid down, skimming the surface before the more detailed working-out that comes after. It deals with politics, which in SPLectrum's world plays the dynamic counterpart to ethics the way aesthetics does to ontology: ethics reads the standing configuration of a subject's values toward others, and politics reads the same territory in motion.

At the base level SPLectrum attaches ethical values to the language games. As a subject we are part of multiple, belong to many. And this is reflected in the values we carry, not a simple one size fits all, but a distribution. We have choice and agency to tailor this to who we are as a subject. 

SPLectrum sees politics as concerned with the art of coexistence. This covers the more traditional fields of public life, but SPLectrum sees this concern wherever there is coexistence. It goes all the way down into family and private life. Can you live happily being a father and a soldier at the same time?

At the heart of effective coexistence lies understanding. Understanding requires enough shared language, enough shared expression of meaning. This common ground matters far more than the fact that there is agreement or disagreement. One could put it as: does the language game we play have powerful enough meaning expression for the game of coexistence to be played? Agreement can come later, or never; what coexistence needs first is that the moves and roles are legible to each other.

Coexistence, then, is the dynamic of figuring out where we play together and where we play apart. A subject or community is an active participant with three choices: play a role in agreement, disagree with a role, or not play at all. The last choice matters most — we have a choice to exclude from our reality what we don't participate in. And here is the turn: in choosing which games to play, a subject or community is choosing which values are live in its reality. The still picture ethics reads — this subject holds these values — is the settled trace of these choices playing out. To say it in a few words: life is a choice of games — and the choice matters.

Out of all this playing, a balance establishes itself. Not decreed from above, not handed down by anyone with the authority to say how the games should sit together — there is no such seat. It needs to be converged upon, and the result can be beneficial or not. An ongoing dynamic where the balance shifts as the play shifts. 

With politics set down, the metaphysics is complete — and it has a clear shape. Epistemology, ontology and aesthetics are one realm — call it the realm of *realisation*: the subject acquiring meaning, holding it as concepts, and expressing it in language. Reality is disclosed to the subject and enacted by it in the same movement, and realisation carries both senses at once — coming to see, and making real. Ethics and politics are the second — the realm of *coexistence*: the subject's standing and its ongoing interactions with *other* subjects and communities — others who have agency of their own, who play their own games and make their own choices. Two realms of relation, realisation then coexistence, and across both the same refusal holds: no universal good handed down from above, no one seated above the games to decide how they should coexist.

I think this is a sound approach to metaphysics — laid out in principle, not a finished system — a solid ground to build on. What is left is not a verdict but a position to read from. As a next step I would like to bring in various figure heads of the different streams of pluralism and explore how SPLectrum's metaphysics fits alongside — taken up not as an argument to win but as a landscape to read.
