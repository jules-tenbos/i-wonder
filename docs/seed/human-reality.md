# The seed and Human Reality

How the seed relates to us as humans, to our daily life, to our perception of reality. This is traditionally the most applied angle of inquiry — humans trying to make sense of their existence. The seed provides a SPLectrum foundation to build this enquiry on.

**P0 — Being implies language.**<br>
When a community comes into existence, it is an act of creation — and like all creation in the seed, it involves differentiation. There is no "us" without what is not us. The boundary that creates a community is the same boundary that defines the other side.

**P1 — Language is relational.**<br>
What a language gives access to depends on what it relates to. A language's reach is the relations it holds — different relations, different reach.

**P2 — Language is the medium through which a subject experiences reality.**<br>
This is the keystone for human reality. As an individual we are at the centre of our experience — a body, sensing, situated, always already in some language. All interaction is mediated by language; what reaches us comes through the languages we hold. But for experience to exist there must be retention — a lasting imprint. Without it, interaction leaves no trace. Reality, in this reading, is the accumulation from a life of experience — the world as it has disclosed itself to me. Knowledge is its insight factor.

**P3 — Language is where subjects share knowledge about reality.**<br>
It follows from P2 that we never interact directly. What we experience as conversation, exchange, being together is emergent: one of us acts on the environment — speaks, writes, gestures — and another interacts with the effect and extracts similar information from it. Two people who can sustain this are two people with sufficiently overlapping language — able to take both sides of the same interaction, action and reception. Speaking the same language, in seed terms, is exactly this: matched pairs of producing and receiving. Sharing is the synchronisation of language between us. Knowledge is what we are able to transfer given the level of synchronisation achieved — the more aligned, the more can pass through. Through this synchronisation, a shared realm forms. What a family, a profession, a culture holds as real is what its members have converged on. This is where P0 comes back: the group that has synchronised enough to share a reality is a community — and its formation is an act of creation that draws a boundary. Us and them, baked into the differentiation.

**P4 — Languages are inter-relational and have equal standing in potential.**<br>
Once sharing brings language to life through synchronisation, P4 is the creational principle for languages themselves. Where our languages overlap, that overlap becomes a starting point — new language can grow from it. This happens in all dimensions: horizontally, when peer communities influence each other; vertically, when one spawns another or is decomposed into another. Medicine emerges from the overlap of biology and practice; a pidgin from two languages meeting in a port. Every overlap is a potential origin. None of these languages is privileged in principle, however different in current reach — each has equal standing as a way of relating.

**P5 — Together they form a web of growing complexity.**<br>
Our web evolves. Each transfer of knowledge adds to it; each new language born from overlap extends it. What drives the growth is not any single community becoming richer, but the flow between them — knowledge passing, accumulating, feeding back. We synchronise, knowledge transfers, new languages emerge from the overlap, and those in turn synchronise with others. The human web grows through this cycle, and its complexity is in the relating.
