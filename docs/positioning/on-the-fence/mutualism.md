# Mutualism

See [Mutualism](/positioning/subjects/m/mutualism) in the subjects section for the concept on its own terms. This page examines where the mutualism narrative resonates with SPLectrum and where it diverges.

## The resonance

The [complex adaptive systems](/positioning/subjects/m/mutualism) reading of mutualism resonates strongly. Patterns emerge from local interactions without central coordination. No actor decides that mutualism should happen — it arises from the dynamics of interacting subjects, each with its own experience of reality, each moving where that experience nudges it. This is close to what the seed describes: shared reality constituted from below, not imposed from above.

The case for decentralisation is at its most stark seen through the seed. All experiences of reality are decentralised. Every experience belongs to a subject — personal, situated, irreducible. Reality is not experienced centrally and then distributed. It is experienced locally and then shared. Two subjects sharing their experience of reality — that is already a peer-to-peer network. The most fundamental one.

## The dissonance

The mutualism narrative — even in its complex adaptive systems form — carries a vocabulary of cooperation, benefit, and mutual advantage. This vocabulary imports intent. A fungus extending its hyphae into a root system is not cooperating. It is doing what the interaction between its reality and its environment produces.

The ethical framing — build cooperative systems, decentralise because it is the right thing to do — imports intent into a domain without it. The attempt to organise emergence is itself a centralising move. SPLectrum's seed principles (P0, P3, P4) are observations about how reality is constituted. Decentralisation conforms to the structure of experience — not a design choice or a moral position, but a structural property.

## Mutualism without intent

Not the outward move — not pushing, not organising, not telling others what to do. That is the centralising reflex dressed in cooperative language. The move is inward. A subject shaping its own relation with its own reality — bringing into its personal reality what favours that relation. Shared reality is not imposed from above. It is constituted from below, from personal realities that overlap.
