# Below the human

Below the human-conceptual layer the dynamic does not stop. The last region of the scatter runs out of anything the philosophy of art would recognise — meaning-making before and beneath language, in the sign-life of organisms, from the cell upward. Here the question stops being which thinker named the dynamic and becomes how far down it goes.

---

**[Semiotics](/positioning/subjects/s/semiotics/)**, in [Peirce](/positioning/persons/p/peirce/): the sign is triadic — a sign stands for an object by producing an *interpretant*, itself a further sign, so meaning passes endlessly onward in what Peirce called *unlimited semiosis*. Two things follow that matter here. The first is the never-closing: Peirce's onward passage of interpretants arrives at the same pole as Derrida's *différance*, from a wholly different ground — logic and the theory of inquiry, not the critique of presence — which is what makes that pole a recurring finding rather than the signature of any one school. The second is that, unlike any other voice in the cast, Peirce's sign needs no human in it: an icon, an index, a symbol do their work wherever one thing stands for another. That is the opening through which semiotics walks below the human.

**Biosemiotics** took exactly that opening. Carried forward by [Thomas Sebeok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_A._Sebeok), grounded in [Jakob von Uexküll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Johann_von_Uexküll)'s *Umwelt* — the world as it is disclosed to a creature through its own signs — and developed in [Jesper Hoffmeyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesper_Hoffmeyer)'s reading of signs from the cell upward, it treats meaning-making as running across the whole of life, well beneath language. The fuller account lives on the [semiotics](/positioning/subjects/s/semiotics/) page; what matters here is its claim — that interpretation does not begin with us.

That claim draws the standard objection: that it reads interpretation, and with it intention, into processes that have neither — anthropomorphism run down the tree of life. SPLectrum's reach is not to insist that the cell intends, but to trade the clean cut between the intentional and the intentionless for a *gradient* — meaning-making thinning out by degrees rather than switching off at a line, an embryonic teleology with no sharp floor. That reach runs toward territory already on the site, where the dynamic shades into the substrate: [autopoiesis](/positioning/subjects/a/autopoiesis/), [assembly theory](/positioning/subjects/a/assembly-theory/), [mineral evolution](/positioning/subjects/m/mineral-evolution/), [complex adaptive systems](/positioning/subjects/c/complex-adaptive-systems/), [relational quantum mechanics](/positioning/subjects/r/relational-quantum-mechanics/). It is held here as a direction the branch points, not a destination it argues.

And at the far end sits the evolution horizon — the longer loop in which generative play builds new tooling over time, and meaning-making becomes a source of the variation life runs on. The branch points at it and no further; it is where this is heading, not what it settles here.

---

One named thinker, one downstream line, and a reach past both. This is the region of the scatter that runs out of the human — the dynamic at work in the sign-life of organisms, shading down by degrees toward a floor that may not be there. Below the named traditions it keeps going, into the substrate the site maps elsewhere. This is the edge where the surface hands off to the gradient.
