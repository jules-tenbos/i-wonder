# Semiotics

Semiotics is the study of signs and of how meaning is made — and in its account meaning is restless, each sign giving rise to a further one, passing onward without end. Where the other branches stay with human expression, semiotics runs below the human: into the sign-life of organisms, meaning-making before and beneath language, from the cell upward.

---

**[Semiotics](/positioning/subjects/s/semiotics/)**, in [Peirce](/positioning/persons/p/peirce/): the sign is triadic — a sign stands for an object by producing an *interpretant*, itself a further sign, so meaning passes endlessly onward in what Peirce called *unlimited semiosis*. That onward passage of interpretants reaches the same never-closing pole as Derrida's *différance*, from a wholly different ground — logic and the theory of inquiry. And unlike the others gathered here, Peirce's sign needs no human in it: an icon, an index, a symbol do their work wherever one thing stands for another. That is the opening through which semiotics walks below the human.

**Biosemiotics** took exactly that opening. Carried forward by [Thomas Sebeok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_A._Sebeok), grounded in [Jakob von Uexküll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Johann_von_Uexküll)'s *Umwelt* — the world as it is disclosed to a creature through its own signs — and developed in [Jesper Hoffmeyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesper_Hoffmeyer)'s reading of signs from the cell upward, it treats meaning-making as running across the whole of life, well beneath language. The fuller account lives on the [semiotics](/positioning/subjects/s/semiotics/) page; what matters here is its claim — that interpretation does not begin with us.

Where this runs next is the SPLectrum side — a gradient rather than a clean cut between the intentional and the intentionless, with no sharp floor, shading down through [autopoiesis](/positioning/subjects/a/autopoiesis/), [assembly theory](/positioning/subjects/a/assembly-theory/), [mineral evolution](/positioning/subjects/m/mineral-evolution/), [complex adaptive systems](/positioning/subjects/c/complex-adaptive-systems/), and [relational quantum mechanics](/positioning/subjects/r/relational-quantum-mechanics/) toward the substrate, and on to the evolution horizon: a direction the branch points, not one it argues here.

---

Peirce's triadic sign, and the biosemiotic line that walked through its opening: the dynamic at work in the sign-life of organisms, meaning-making that does not begin with us — and keeps going, by degrees, down toward the substrate the site maps elsewhere.
