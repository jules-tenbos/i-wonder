# Basal Cognition

Biology's empirical work on cognition runs below the nervous system. The **basal cognition** programme — [Pamela Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basal_cognition), [Michael Levin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Levin_(biologist)) and others — studies capacities the word was built for in systems that have no brain to house them: sensing and signalling in single cells, memory and habituation in plants, valence-guided behaviour in microbes, collective problem-solving in tissues. Lyon's biogenic approach starts the study of cognition from what life does rather than from what minds report; Levin's work on bioelectric signalling treats tissues and cell collectives as problem-solving agents — navigating anatomical and physiological spaces the way animals navigate physical ones.

This is the research front that tests the word's reach empirically. If cells, plants and tissues do these things, then cognition has no complexity threshold and no privileged substrate — the process runs wherever a system acquires, retains, and uses what its history of interaction teaches it. Whether to call that cognition or metaphor is the definitional question the field has not settled; the wide reading stated philosophically — from Uexküll's Umwelt to the Santiago theory — is treated at [Cognition as a Property of Life](/positioning/subjects/c/cognition/philosophy/biology/).
