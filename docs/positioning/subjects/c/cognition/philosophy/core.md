# Cognition in Core Philosophy

The approaches in core philosophy that hold cognising as something running *within* experience — not an inner process that receives experience first and works it up after.

## Western philosophy

Within Western philosophy this is one movement, not five. Kant makes the opening move — the turn — and each later angle carries the letting-go further: into constitution, world and body, into practice, into performance, into process. The angles below are stations of that one unfolding.

### The turn

The deep root of cognition-talk, and the move nearly every later angle descends from — the turn: the reversal that put the mind's own activity inside all experience. It became possible when [Descartes](/positioning/persons/d/descartes/) placed the thinking subject at the centre of philosophy; with the subject there, the question could be asked.

- **[Kant](/positioning/persons/k/kant/)** made the turn itself. He reversed the question — not how the mind conforms to objects, but how objects conform to the mind — and the understanding's synthesis runs in all experience: "thoughts without content are empty, intuitions without concepts are blind." No experiencing first and cognising after.

### The phenomenological angle

[Phenomenology](/positioning/subjects/p/phenomenology/) radicalised the turn: meaning already at work in experience before any deliberate act of knowing — in constitution, world, body, and tradition.

- **[Husserl](/positioning/persons/h/husserl/)** found sense-constitution running beneath active thought — his passive synthesis: experience arrives already structured before attention does any work.
- **[Heidegger](/positioning/persons/h/heidegger/)** placed us always already in a world disclosed through involvement, making the detached knowing subject a derivative stance rather than the basic relation.
- **[Merleau-Ponty](/positioning/persons/m/merleau-ponty/)** carried the argument into the body: perception arrives whole and significant, the habitual body doing the work the intellectualist picture assigns to inference.
- **[Gadamer](/positioning/persons/g/gadamer/)** grounded understanding in pre-judgement delivered by tradition — the ground of cognition, not its contamination.

### The pragmatist angle

[Pragmatism](/positioning/subjects/p/pragmatism/) reached the same territory by its own route, relocating knowing inside practice and the stream of experience — dissolving the gap between undergoing the world and making sense of it.

- **[Peirce](/positioning/persons/p/peirce/)** dissolved the line between perceiving and inferring, treating perceptual judgments as extreme cases of abduction.
- **[James](/positioning/persons/j/james/)** put the relations inside experience itself: on his radical empiricism the connective tissue is given in the stream, not added by a cognitive act afterwards.
- **[Dewey](/positioning/persons/d/dewey/)** retired the "spectator theory of knowledge" and made knowing a mode of doing — inquiry as the reworking of a situation from within it.

### The anti-intellectualist angle

The analytic wing of the same refusal: intelligent performance does not require an inner act of cognising running behind it.

- **[Ryle](/positioning/persons/r/ryle/)** showed that the inner-act picture ends in regress — knowing-how is not knowing-that applied.
- **[Wittgenstein](/positioning/persons/w/wittgenstein/)** told philosophy to stop thinking of understanding as a mental process at all: understanding shows itself in what one can do, against a background of custom and practice.
- **[Polanyi](/positioning/persons/p/polanyi/)** made the same point through tacit knowing: we know more than we can tell, and the explicit always rides on a tacit ground.

### The process angle

[Process philosophy](/positioning/subjects/p/process-philosophy/) carried the letting-go the whole way: experience as ongoing process, with cognition-like taking-up reaching far beyond the human case.

- **[Bergson](/positioning/persons/b/bergson/)** held that there is no perception which is not full of memories — the pure, unloaded percept never occurs — and that perception selects what is relevant to action out of a richer whole.
- **[Whitehead](/positioning/persons/w/whitehead/)** generalised furthest: every actual entity *prehends* its world, so experience-like taking-up goes all the way down and human cognition is a highly elaborated case of something utterly general.
- **[Hegel](/positioning/persons/h/hegel/)** stands behind both as the philosopher of *Erfahrung* — experience as self-transforming, the experiencing reshaping the experiencer.

## Eastern philosophy

Several Asian traditions reached the same territory independently, and earlier — not stations of the Western movement but arrivals of their own: the fusion of awareness and what appears, and knowing located in conditioned arising and skilled doing.

- **[Yogācāra](/positioning/subjects/y/yogacara/)** analysed experience as arising with no split between grasper and grasped, each perception conditioned by the seeds of past experience — the *ālaya-vijñāna*, the storehouse consciousness.
- **[Candrakīrti](/positioning/persons/c/candrakirti/)** and the Madhyamaka made dependent origination basic: knower, known, and knowing each arise with their conditions, none standing on its own essence.
- **[Abhinavagupta](/positioning/persons/a/abhinavagupta/)**'s Pratyabhijñā school — the school of *recognition* — held manifestation (*prakāśa*) and reflective awareness (*vimarśa*) strictly inseparable.
- **[Zhuangzi](/positioning/persons/z/zhuangzi/)**'s craftsman stories locate knowing in skilled doing, with nothing deliberative running behind the knife.
- **[Nishida Kitarō](/positioning/persons/n/nishida/)**'s "pure experience" — prior to any subject–object split, developed out of his reading of James — carried the fusion into modern philosophy from the Kyoto School.
