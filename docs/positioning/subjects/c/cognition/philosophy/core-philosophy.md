# Cognition in Core Philosophy

The philosophical argument over cognition has one recurring axis: whether cognising is an inner process that operates *on* experience — receiving it, interpreting it, adding the meaning — or something that runs *within* experience, so that there is no experiencing first and cognising after. The classical root sits at the head of that axis; a set of traditions — never a single school, and never under one name — moved, each in its own vocabulary and from its own direction, to the second position. This page holds the angles, one by one.

---

## The classical root

The deep root of cognition-talk is [Kant](/positioning/persons/k/kant/). Against the empiricist picture of knowledge as received impressions, he argued that cognition (*Erkenntnis*) is constituted by the mind's own activity: the understanding's synthesis runs in all experience, and "thoughts without content are empty, intuitions without concepts are blind." There is, for Kant, no experiencing first and cognising after — experience *is* already the product of cognitive activity. Nearly every later angle on this page descends from that move, including those that turn it against him: what Kant assigned to a fixed, universal apparatus of categories, later traditions hand to history, the body, practice, or life itself.

## The phenomenological angle

[Husserl](/positioning/persons/h/husserl/) found sense-constitution running *beneath* active thought — his "passive synthesis": experience arrives already structured before attention does any work. [Heidegger](/positioning/persons/h/heidegger/) radicalised the point: we are always already in a world that is meaningful before any act of knowing, and the detached knowing subject — the spectator contemplating objects — is a derivative stance, not the basic relation. [Merleau-Ponty](/positioning/persons/m/merleau-ponty/) carried the argument into the body: perception is not sensation plus judgment but arrives whole and already significant, the habitual body doing work the intellectualist picture assigns to inference. [Gadamer](/positioning/persons/g/gadamer/) extended it to understanding as such, which always runs on pre-judgement delivered by tradition — the ground of cognition, not its contamination.

## The pragmatist angle

[Peirce](/positioning/persons/p/peirce/) dissolved the line between perceiving and inferring — perceptual judgments as extreme cases of abduction. [James](/positioning/persons/j/james/)'s radical empiricism put the relations *inside* experience: the connective tissue is given in the stream, not added by a cognitive act afterwards. [Dewey](/positioning/persons/d/dewey/) named the enemy picture the "spectator theory of knowledge" and relocated knowing inside experience as a mode of doing — inquiry as the reworking of a situation from within it.

## The anti-intellectualist angle

[Ryle](/positioning/persons/r/ryle/) argued that intelligent performance cannot require a prior inner act of cognising, on pain of regress — knowing-how is not knowing-that applied. [Wittgenstein](/positioning/persons/w/wittgenstein/) told philosophy to stop thinking of understanding as a mental process at all: understanding shows itself in use, against a background of training and practice, not in an inner event. [Polanyi](/positioning/persons/p/polanyi/)'s tacit knowing belongs to the same family — we know more than we can tell, and the explicit always rides on a tacit ground.

## The process angle

[Bergson](/positioning/persons/b/bergson/) held that there is no perception which is not full of memories — the pure, unloaded percept never occurs — and that perception is selective, carving what is relevant to action out of a richer whole. [Whitehead](/positioning/persons/w/whitehead/) generalised furthest: every actual entity *prehends* — takes up — its world, so that experience-like taking-up goes all the way down, and human cognition is a highly elaborated case of something utterly general. [Hegel](/positioning/persons/h/hegel/) stands behind both as the philosopher of *Erfahrung* — experience as self-transforming, the experiencing reshaping the experiencer.

## The Eastern angles

Several Asian traditions reached the same territory independently, and early. [Yogācāra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogachara) Buddhism analysed experience as arising with no split between grasper and grasped, each perception conditioned by the seeds of past experience — the *ālaya-vijñāna*, the storehouse consciousness. The [Madhyamaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhyamaka) of Nāgārjuna and [Candrakīrti](/positioning/persons/c/candrakirti/) made dependent origination basic: nothing in experience — knower, known, or the knowing — stands on its own essence; each arises with its conditions. [Abhinavagupta](/positioning/persons/a/abhinavagupta/)'s Pratyabhijñā school — the school of *recognition* — held manifestation (*prakāśa*) and reflective awareness (*vimarśa*) strictly inseparable: awareness fused with what appears. [Zhuangzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuangzi_(book))'s craftsman stories locate knowing in skilled doing, with nothing deliberative running behind the knife. And [Nishida Kitarō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitaro_Nishida)'s "pure experience" — prior to any subject–object split, developed in explicit dialogue with James — carried the fusion into modern philosophy from the Kyoto School.

## Territory still to be taken up

Philosophy of mind's own quarrels over cognition — representation and its critics, the computational theory of mind, consciousness and the mental as a category, the 4E debates in their philosophical form — are a territory of their own, to be added as the subject grows.
