# Cognition as a Property of Life

Biology's reading of the word: cognition is not fenced to minds but is a property of life — perhaps of any system that maintains itself against its surroundings. Each position forces the definitional question — if a cell or a colony does this, is it cognition or metaphor? — and the field has no agreed answer. The reading runs on into [enactivism](/positioning/subjects/e/enactivism/) and [biosemiotics](/positioning/subjects/b/biosemiotics/).

- **[Von Uexküll](/positioning/persons/v/von-uexkull/)** gave every animal its own *Umwelt* — a world of significance built by its own sensory and motor repertoire, as many worlds as kinds of subject.
- **[Maturana](/positioning/persons/m/maturana/) and [Varela](/positioning/persons/v/varela/)** stated the widest position in the literature with the Santiago theory, grown from their [autopoiesis](/positioning/subjects/a/autopoiesis/): living as a process *is* a process of cognition — a living system, by maintaining itself, already knows its world, nervous system or none.
- **[Piaget](/positioning/persons/p/piaget/)**'s genetic epistemology made knowledge a biological achievement: assimilation and accommodation as the organism's adaptation carried into thought.
