# Cognition

This is a meta subject, organised around a specific definition: *cognition is the process of acquiring knowledge and understanding through experience* — the definition [this site carries](/seed/cognition/), held wide: no mental constraint, and experience encompassing all activity. The subject brings together the resources of interest that fall within that definition's scope — subjects and persons on this site, external references where the territory is self-contained elsewhere. It is under construction: the list grows more exhaustive over time.

---

## Philosophy

**[Cognition in core philosophy](/positioning/subjects/c/cognition/philosophy/core-philosophy/)** — the angles, one page: the classical root in Kant, and the phenomenological, pragmatist, anti-intellectualist, process, and Eastern angles. Further philosophy pieces (philosophy of mind among them) join this section as they are taken up.

## The sciences

**[Cognition — the sciences](/positioning/subjects/c/cognition/sciences/)** — psychology as the word's institutional home, and biology's counter-claim: from Uexküll's Umwelt and the Santiago theory to genetic epistemology, affordances, and the basal cognition programme.

## Held elsewhere for now

Within scope, external until taken up here: the [extended mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_mind_thesis) and [distributed cognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_cognition) · [predictive processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predictive_coding) · machine learning and AI · [animal cognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_cognition) · the intelligence discourse in its stranger registers — octopus minds, swarms, machine intelligence as something genuinely other · collective and institutional cognition.
