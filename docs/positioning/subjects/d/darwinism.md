# Darwinism

Darwinism names the theory of evolution by [natural selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_selection) as proposed by [Charles Darwin](/positioning/persons/d/darwin/) in *On the Origin of Species* (1859) and developed across his subsequent works. The term also covers the broader tradition of evolutionary thought that followed — the [modern synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_synthesis_(20th_century)) of genetics and natural selection in the mid-twentieth century, and the extensions, disputes, and appropriations that have continued since.

---

## The core mechanism

Natural selection operates on three conditions: variation exists within a population, that variation is heritable, and some variants leave more offspring than others in a given environment. Over generations, traits that contribute to reproductive success become more prevalent. The mechanism requires no foresight, no designer, and no direction — it is differential survival and reproduction acting on heritable variation.

Darwin proposed natural selection as the principal but not the sole mechanism of evolutionary change. [Sexual selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_selection) — competition for mates and mate choice — was introduced in *The Descent of Man* (1871) as a second mechanism. The relative weight of natural selection, sexual selection, and other processes (genetic drift, developmental constraint, self-organisation) in explaining evolutionary outcomes has been debated continuously since.

## The modern synthesis

The reconciliation of Darwinian natural selection with [Mendelian genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendelian_inheritance) in the 1930s and 1940s — through the work of [Ronald Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Fisher), [J. B. S. Haldane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._B._S._Haldane), [Sewall Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewall_Wright), [Theodosius Dobzhansky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodosius_Dobzhansky), [Ernst Mayr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Mayr), and others — provided the mathematical and empirical framework that Darwin lacked. Population genetics gave natural selection a quantitative basis; the gene became the unit of heredity; the synthesis established the framework within which most evolutionary biology has operated since.

## Extensions and disputes

The history of Darwinism after the modern synthesis is a history of extensions and boundary disputes:

- **Kin selection** — [W. D. Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._D._Hamilton)'s inclusive fitness theory (1964) explained altruism toward relatives through shared genes.
- **Reciprocal altruism** — [Trivers](/positioning/persons/t/trivers/) (1971) extended cooperation to unrelated individuals through repeated interaction and reciprocity.
- **The selfish gene** — [Richard Dawkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dawkins)' *The Selfish Gene* (1976) reframed natural selection as operating at the level of genes rather than organisms.
- **Neutral theory** — [Motoo Kimura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motoo_Kimura) (1968) argued that most molecular-level evolutionary change is selectively neutral — driven by drift, not selection.
- **Punctuated equilibrium** — [Stephen Jay Gould](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Jay_Gould) and [Niles Eldredge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niles_Eldredge) (1972) proposed that evolutionary change is concentrated in brief episodes of speciation, separated by long periods of stasis.
- **Self-organisation** — [Stuart Kauffman](/positioning/persons/k/kauffman/) argued that order in biological systems can arise spontaneously from the dynamics of complex networks, not only through natural selection.
- **Extended evolutionary synthesis** — an ongoing programme arguing that the modern synthesis needs extending to incorporate developmental plasticity, niche construction, epigenetic inheritance, and other processes.

Whether these amount to revisions within Darwinism or challenges to it depends on where the boundaries of "Darwinism" are drawn — a question the tradition has not settled.

## Social Darwinism

The application of evolutionary vocabulary to social and political questions began in Darwin's own lifetime. [Herbert Spencer](/positioning/persons/s/spencer/)'s "survival of the fittest" (coined 1864, adopted by Darwin from the fifth edition of *Origin*) was taken up to justify laissez-faire economics, colonial expansion, and eugenic programmes. The label "Social Darwinism" was applied retrospectively — [Richard Hofstadter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Hofstadter)'s *Social Darwinism in American Thought* (1944) gave it broad currency. Darwin himself did not advocate these political programmes. Whether Social Darwinism is a misuse of Darwin's theory or an extension of it is itself a question with a long literature.

[Kropotkin](/positioning/persons/k/kropotkin/)'s *Mutual Aid* (1902) was the earliest sustained counter-argument from within natural history — cooperation as a factor in evolution alongside competition.
