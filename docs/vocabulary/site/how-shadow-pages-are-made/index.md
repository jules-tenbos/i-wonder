# How shadow pages are made

Each shadow page lists the vocabulary of a specific content page — the terms and phrases a reader needs to track in order to follow that page. The list is produced by a collaboration between AI agents and a human reader, in two passes with a check between them that can send the content page back for rework.

## Cold pass

The first pass is done by a reader with no broader site context. The instruction is to list load-bearing terms and phrases — what a first-time reader would need to understand to follow the page. This catches the vocabulary the page itself puts in front of the reader, without inherited assumptions from elsewhere on the site.

## Warm pass

The second pass is done with the rest of the site in view. Its job is to catch what the cold pass can't see: terms that look ordinary but carry a narrower meaning on this site, terms used in two senses across different pages, borrowings from other traditions, and cross-references to vocabulary defined elsewhere. Cold gives breadth; warm gives depth.

## Alignment check and rework

Before the list is finalised, the two passes are compared. Does the cold reading reflect the page's aims as the warm pass understands them? If yes, the method proceeds to the table. If not — if the page is not carrying its own intent cleanly — the finding is not a problem with the shadow. It is a problem with the content page. The page gets rewritten to the extent warranted, and the passes are rerun. Only a content page whose cold and warm readings agree is ready to have its vocabulary finalised.

## Trimming to substantial vocabulary

A shadow page is a site-language instrument, not a glossary of the page. Plain general-language words a reader already understands without site context don't earn a seat. A general term belongs in the list only if it does site-specific work — used in a narrowed sense, or serving as an implicit reference the page depends on. The test is whether the term needs the site to mean what it means here.

## The list

The final list is a three-column table: the term or phrase as it appears on the page, the language it comes from (SPLectrum, general English, Wittgenstein, and so on), and a comment giving brief usage context on this specific page. Where the term is already defined in [the SPLectrum vocabulary](/vocabulary/splectrum/) or elsewhere on the site, the comment includes a cross-reference link.

## Alongside tone-of-voice

Shadow pages complement the tone-of-voice material: tone-of-voice describes voice and register; the shadow profiles the specific vocabulary of the personas in play on each page. Together they keep the writing coherent across the voices the site hosts.
