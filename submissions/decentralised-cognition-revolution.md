# The Decentralised Cognition Revolution

## From Industrial to Cognitive: Naming the Next Revolution

The Industrial Revolution can be understood, in part, as the application of science to physical tools that extend human capabilities. The word *industrial* itself does the descriptive work — it points at the domain being transformed: physical labour, material production, bodily effort.

If that framing holds, then the revolution driven by AI demands a parallel name — one that points at *its* domain with the same directness. What is being mechanised, scaled, and reorganised this time is not muscle but cognition.

## Candidates Considered

Several labels were weighed for their precision, honesty, and staying power:

**Extended Thinking.** Plain, structurally parallel to "industrial," and immediately legible. "Extended" does double duty — amplification (thinking further, faster) and externalisation (thinking pushed beyond the skull), echoing Clark and Chalmers' extended mind thesis. It works well as pure description: it names what's happening without making philosophical claims about whether AI "really thinks." However, it preserves an anthropocentric geometry — a centre (the human thinker) with cognition radiating outward. Everything remains an extension *of me*.

**Noetic Revolution.** Economical — from Greek *noesis*, the activity of thinking. But too opaque for plain description.

**Scaled Thinking.** Captures what "industrial" really implied in practice: not just mechanisation but operation at previously impossible scale. But misses the structural shift in *where* cognition happens.

**Extended Cognition Revolution.** "Cognition" improves on "thinking" — it's more neutral on questions of consciousness and understanding, and can be used functionally. But "extended" still implies a single centre reaching further, which creates two problems.

## Why "Decentralised"

The term *decentralised cognition* resolves both problems that "extended" cannot handle:

**Autonomous AI.** Agents increasingly operate independently — making inferences, generating plans, coordinating with each other. Calling this "extended human cognition" is inaccurate. It is cognition happening *elsewhere*, without a human at the hub. "Decentralised" accommodates multiple loci of cognition with no privileged centre.

**Non-human cognition.** The revolution is not only that we built new thinking machines. It is that we are being forced to recognise that cognition was never centralised in the first place — in ecosystems, animal collectives, microbial networks, and the kinds of distributed biological coordination that thinkers like Maturana described. We acted as though cognition was centralised because we only had access to our own. "Decentralised" opens the door to recognising cognitive processes that were always already there.

## The Geometry of Each Term

The distinction between "extended" and "decentralised" is fundamentally geometric:

- **Extended** is *radial* — there is a centre (the human subject), and cognition radiates outward from it. Every tool, every AI, is an extension of that centre. The Cartesian subject is quietly preserved as the origin point.
- **Decentralised** *dissolves that geometry*. It says there are multiple loci of cognition and no privileged centre. The locus of cognition has not just stretched — it has shifted.

## The Decentralised Cognition Revolution

The proposed term: **The Decentralised Cognition Revolution.**

It does three things at once:

1. **Decentralised** captures the structural shift — cognition is no longer locked inside individual biological heads. It is distributed across machines, networks, and human–AI interactions. It implies reorganisation, not merely amplification.
2. **Cognition** names the domain without overcommitting. It is more neutral than "intelligence" or "thinking" on questions of consciousness and understanding. It can be used functionally — pattern recognition, inference, classification, language use — without smuggling in metaphysics.
3. **Revolution** does its usual work.

The term is descriptive rather than theoretical, just as "industrial" was. It does not claim that AI *is* intelligence, nor deny it. It simply names the domain being transformed and the structural character of that transformation. The theoretical questions remain open — which is exactly where they should be.
