# Language

## The structural base — P0 and P1

Before subjects enter the picture, language already is. P0 says being implies language; being and language arise together through differentiation. Where there is being, there is the relational, and where there is the relational, there is language. P1 says what this language is: relational, not representational. Language binds. It does not picture, depict, mirror, or copy. It connects.

The broad sense is in play throughout. Language here is not human communication; it is all relational activity. Cellular signalling, gravitational binding, social protocol, the trace a footprint leaves in mud — each is a way of relating that meets the seed's structural conditions. Human language is one species in a much wider genus.

## Language binds, it does not represent

The non-representational reading of P1 has substantial intellectual company. The pragmatist tradition — Wittgenstein, Sellars, Rorty, Brandom — has been at this for nearly a century. Brandom's inferentialism is the sharpest discursive case.

His move is to locate meaning not in what a sentence stands for but in its inferential relations: what follows from it, what is incompatible with it, what entitles a speaker to assert it. To grasp a concept is to know its place in the space of reasons.

Discourse, on this picture, is a normative practice. To assert is to undertake a commitment — a position the speaker is now answerable for. Other speakers respond by acknowledging or challenging that commitment, by tracking what each other has said, by keeping score. Commitments come with entitlement conditions (what would need to be in place for the commitment to be defensible) and consequence conditions (what else gets pulled in once the commitment is on the table). Sellars's "myth of the given" sits behind all of this: there is no purely given content, no raw input that arrives meaningful before any conceptual work has been done. Every content is already inside the practice.

What this gives the seed: a worked-out semantic picture in which meaning is irreducibly relational. There is no sign-to-thing relation doing the work. There is sign-to-other-uses, speaker-to-other-speakers, position-to-position. Meaning lives in the network of moves, not in any single connection.

Two cautions before reading Brandom into Splectrum wholesale. He is anthropocentric — the picture is built around discursive practice and does not obviously extend to relational activity below or beyond the discursive. P4 keeps this honest: the seed treats Brandom's account as load-bearing for one important class of languages, not as the whole story. Other languages do relational work without the apparatus of commitment and entitlement, and they have equal standing in principle.

The other caution is that Brandom's "objectivity emerges from commitment-tracking" is friendly to P3 but not identical with it. P3 is about subjects converging through any kind of language interaction; Brandom's account is about discursive convergence specifically. The structural shape is similar; the scope is different.

With those caveats in place, the headline reading holds. Language is commitment, not representation. P1 in its sharpest discursive form.

## Contingency is structural

All creation brings language. But language is as contingent as the creation itself. It tells us what it tells us, no more. The world it gives access to is the world the differentiation cut out. A different cut would have given a different language, a different access, a different world.

This is contingency in Rorty's sense: not a flaw, not a falling-short of some better, less contingent language. Just structural. There is no language that escapes its own contingency by being more careful, more rigorous, more formal. Every language is the language of the cut that produced it.

A consequence: to understand something, one must create the language for it. Borrowing a neighbouring language helps — translation, analogy, family resemblance — but the act of creation cannot be skipped. And the act of creation does not reveal what has not yet been created. The unrealised remains outside language.

## Conception, realisation, historicity

Conception is a differentiation. By P0, that makes it a creational act: at the moment of conception, there is being-in-language that was not there before. The conceiver's reality has a new fold in it.

But a conception that goes nowhere, that is not enacted, that leaves no trace and is not picked up — does that count as creation?

In the moment, yes. Structurally, no. A relation that does not persist is not quite a relation in the seed's sense; P1 needs duration to do any work. The flicker of a conception is creational, but for the creation to become reality it must be realised — sustained, re-instantiated, traced into ongoing practice. Reality requires the historicity.

This is why "conceiving the future is creating it" carries weight without committing to science fiction's metaphysics. There is no pre-existing future-as-trace waiting to be discovered; conceiving the future lays the first inscription. But the inscription has to keep going. A future that is conceived and dropped is not a future that becomes real; it is a flicker that was, briefly, in a subject's reality, and then was not.

The historicity post picks up here. The traces, the carrying-forward, the way reality is the residue of sustained relation through time — all of that is the same thread, viewed under the temporal axis. Language and historicity are not really separable in the seed; the seam between them is a matter of where the weight is placed in any given exposition, not a real division in what they describe.

## What this page is doing

This page lays the structural ground. P0 and P1 set the conditions. The non-representational reading sets the orientation. Contingency sets the modal status. The conception/realisation move opens the door to historicity. Everything that follows in the seed stands on this base.
