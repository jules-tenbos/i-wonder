# Ways of Working

How we approach inquiry, across phases.

---

## General approach

We reason from within our context — the principle and what follows from it. We are not trying to prove right or wrong. We are trying to understand, to find resonance, to see how things relate.

We think relationally, not factually. We look for how things connect — across contexts, across traditions, across domains. The relation is the insight. See tone of thought (doc 06) for how this works in practice.

We build through encounter. The principle is the seed; what it becomes depends on what it meets. We do not deduce from the principle in isolation. We take it into the world and see what resonates and what resists.

Self-challenge is method, not afterthought. The standards evolve with each phase — gentle in the exploratory, rigorous in the consolidation — but the discipline is constant. This includes deliberately seeking perspectives with no investment in our thinking — independent review by fresh eyes that have no context from the working process. What looks right from the inside may not hold up from the outside.

The process is iterative by design. We write, review, rewrite, review again. The work improves through cycles, not through getting it right the first time. Each iteration tightens the thinking, catches what slipped through, and brings the voice closer to the standards.

Our claim is purely structural: language is relational, whatever its contents. We do not claim that our vocabulary gets reality right. We claim a structural pattern that is consistent across domains. Content varies by language. Structure does not.

We are conservative with claims. The position is strong and there is no need to push. Where something is an ambition, we say so. Where something is an observation, we say so. Where we are not sure, we say that too. Overstating weakens what is genuinely there.

We are observational, not evaluative. We observe what is there — in a thinker's work, in a domain, in an encounter — without attaching value statements. If something supports us, that will be visible without us labelling it. We live by example, not by telling others what to do.

When engaging with other thinkers: understand their thinking context first — the room they're in, the assumptions, the interlocutors. Their moves make sense within that context. Then switch to our context and see what resonates. Not mapping, not comparing, not judging — relating.

---

## Phase 1 — Exploratory

*Growing the whole. Discovery of resonance. Seed unpacking. Expansive.*

The first phase is about breadth. We take the principle into as many domains as we can and see what resonates. Philosophy, mathematics, physics, biology, cognitive science, Eastern traditions, Western traditions, formal systems, engineering. The aim is to discover whether the structural pattern holds across independent traditions and domains.

We are building our language, finding our voice, discovering what the seed contains. The priority is to establish whether there is genuine convergence on the relational across independent starting points.

The seed unpacking happens here — outlining the vision, the ambitions, where the principle points. Not working out the detail but seeing the territory.

**Standards in this phase:**

- **The consistency standard.** Two-directional: consistent with the author and major interpretations; consistent when judged by a third party who knows both the thinker's work and our principle.
- **No claiming to know better than the author.** No "inadvertently," no "without realising."
- **No replacing their concept with ours.** The two should stand side by side. If the overlap requires our vocabulary to state, it may not be an overlap.
- **Fabric level, not cloth level.** The overlap should be about the common structural layer, not about how a specific language organises itself internally.
- **Productive, not just confirmatory.** "This is relational too" without further insight is not enough.
- **Not everything needs to be a full mapping.** Where an encounter does not produce genuine insight, honest acknowledgment is truer than forcing a statement.
- **Wrong overlaps are informative.** A mistaken overlap, once recognised, tells us where the common layer is not.

Bidirectionality is welcome but not required — many thinkers have different concerns, and their frameworks may not naturally see our reading back. The emphasis is on what resonates.

---

## Phase 2 — Diversification

*Filling in the structure of the whole. Focus on specific parts. Comparative. Bidirectional.*

The second phase narrows the focus. Having established the broad pattern, we go deep into specific domains — testing the principle against detailed knowledge, engaging with the specifics of particular traditions, working through the comparative questions properly.

Divergence becomes central. We work bidirectionally — not just what we see in their work, but what their work says about ours. Where do the two stances genuinely part? What can we learn from where they differ? What does the divergence teach us about the limits of our own claim?

Comparative approaches become possible: how does the relational play out differently in Saussure vs Peirce, in Nagarjuna vs Early Buddhism, in Rovelli vs Bohm? What do the differences between related positions tell us about the fabric?

**Standards in this phase (in addition to Phase 1):**

- **Name what does not overlap.** If we cannot say what diverges, we are probably not looking honestly. Every thinker will have aspects that do not fit. Naming those is part of equal standing.
- **Bidirectionality required.** The overlap should work in both directions — visible from within their framework as well as from within ours. Where it doesn't, we note that honestly.
- **Each domain studied on its own terms** before being placed alongside the principle. We enter each domain with respect for its internal logic, not just looking for what confirms us.

The work becomes more disciplined here. The exploratory breadth gives way to focused depth.

---

## Phase 3 — Consolidation

*Maturing the individual structures making up the whole. Hardening. Devil's advocate.*

The third phase tests what we've built. We actively try to break it — looking for where the claims don't hold, where the overlaps were projection, where the structural pattern fails.

**Standards in this phase (in addition to Phases 1 and 2):**

- **Devil's advocate approaches.** Arguing against the principle from within specific domains. What would a committed Platonist say? A strict materialist? A Derridean who rejects all structural claims? Not straw men but the strongest version of each objection, taken seriously.
- **Divergence at full strength.** Every mapping must account for what doesn't fit. The divergence is as important as the overlap — it defines the actual shape of the claim, not just its reach.
- **What survives stays. What doesn't goes.** Honestly, without defence. The principle is only as strong as what remains after active opposition.
- **Consolidation of positive findings.** Which convergences are genuinely independent? Which overlaps are genuinely structural? Which insights were genuinely produced through the encounter rather than projected from within?
