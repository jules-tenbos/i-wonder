# Comment notes — "How Observer Patch Holography Makes Semiotics Physical"

For a comment on Mueller's Floating Pragma blog post.

---

## Tone

Engaged, interested in the semiotics, respectful but honest about where the reasoning breaks down. Not a takedown. A conversation.

## Opening

Appreciate the semiotics primer — it's well done. The direction is interesting: meaning as constitutive, the observer as central. These observations surface across independent frameworks. Worth taking seriously.

## The "simple axiom"

The article presents OPH as "built on a simple axiom: no observer sees the whole world at once." But the formal framework (five axioms in the papers) presupposes operator algebras, Hilbert spaces, quantum focusing, recovery channels, and the holographic principle. These are conclusions from decades of physics, not a simple starting point. How does the simple axiom relate to the actual axiomatic base?

## Observer coupling

"Where patches overlap, their descriptions must agree." But what produces agreement? What grounds observers on the same floor? Without a coupling mechanism, agreement is postulated, not produced. RQM has been working on exactly this — decoherence as the mechanism that produces stable, shared facts from relational ones. OPH doesn't engage with it.

## Semiosis flattened

This is the heart. Semiosis in Peirce is open — signs generate new signs, interpretation is unbounded, there is no final referent. The OPH mapping converges to a fixed point. That's a consensus protocol, not semiosis. The mapping captures the surface structure of one step (pattern → interpretation → shared outcome) but loses what makes semiosis interesting: its openness, its generativity, its refusal to close.

Semiotics deserves to be engaged with on its own terms, not flattened into a convergence protocol.

## Measurement

"A measurement is the moment when a local pattern is stabilized across observers as a shared record." But if the axiom guarantees observers must agree, then the shared record is correct by definition. Correct judged against what? There's nothing outside the protocol to measure it against. That's representationalism — the record stands for reality because the framework says it does.

## Optional closing — TOE and openness

A Theory of Everything says one language is final. But semiotics — the field the article draws from — is precisely the study of how meaning stays open, how signs keep generating new signs, how interpretation never settles. A framework that claims to be complete is at odds with the very tradition it invokes.

---

## Notes on length

Blog comment — keep it tight. Maybe pick 2–3 of these points rather than all of them. The strongest combination:

1. Semiosis flattened (the heart of the critique, meets the article where it stands)
2. Observer coupling (the foundational gap)
3. Measurement as representational (the sharpest consequence)

The "simple axiom" and TOE points can be lighter touches.
