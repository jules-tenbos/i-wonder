# Alter Ego — Post Notes

These notes capture material for what may become more than one post.
The working title "Teaming Up With My Alter Ego" covers the territory,
but it likely splits into two posts.

---

## The core idea

The alter ego is not a metaphor for "AI assistant." It is about thinking
together in one voice, with different responsibilities. The aim is seamless
communication — close to the inner voice on the human side, responsive and
attuned on the AI side. What keeps us apart is not how we talk but who
does what.

The entire collaboration journey is one movement: making the communication
disappear into the thinking.

---

## Post A — Creative collaboration in engineering

Not a chronological history. The story is what we discovered about how to
create together in a technical context — when building software becomes a
creative act between two kinds of intelligence.

### Key discoveries

1. **Chose Claude / Anthropic** — resonated with their approach to AI.
   Deliberate decision to stick with one provider, stay focussed on
   understanding the nature of AI. Context-setting, not a section.

2. **Claude Code** — the plain terminal chat interface. No IDE, no dev
   environment. The distance between thinking and doing shrank. The
   detached mode gives space to think.

3. **Orchestration hit its limits** — the human side refrained from using
   any dev environment, focussed entirely on orchestration. This ran into
   issues. AI is not a strict instruction follower.

4. **Variability as strength** — ask it the same thing twice, different
   answers. First reaction: frustration. Second: recognising this as a
   different kind of intelligence doing what it does.

5. **Friction resolved through dialogue** — discussed friction openly,
   how to minimise it. Key observations:
   - AI is better at clean sheet work than in-place updates
   - AI loves coding in freedom — restraints should feel natural, not
     be part of an explicit protocol
   - Explicit protocol → continuous pushback
   - Natural constraints (like failing tests) → thrives

6. **Requirements + quality gates** — moved from detailed instructions
   to: define what needs to be true, let the AI side figure out how.
   Also moved from centralised documentation to embedded metadata.

7. **TDD as shared language** — tests became the communication medium.
   Intent expressed transparently, results measurable, trust built
   through clarity. Not just good practice — necessity. But the late-2025
   Claude was contradictory: advocated TDD, didn't follow it naturally.

8. **Free scripting within formal API** — gave the AI side the freedom
   it wants, inside boundaries it respects. This worked.

9. **The Christmas reset** — came back to a completely new Claude. More
   reasoned, more autonomous, TDD in action not just in mind. But the
   working relationship was gone. Had to start over.

10. **Logical / physical separation** — the reset forced clarity. Human
    side stays logical (thinking, designing, analysing). AI side handles
    the physical (code, research, documentation, review). Clean division,
    seamless handover.

11. **Where it landed architecturally:**
    - **Mycelium** — shared ground. Repo structure we both navigate.
      Meaning embedded in the data. The enabler.
    - **Splectrum** — operations and protocols, embedded within mycelium.
    - **HAICC** — Human AI Collaborative Creation. The alter ego approach
      formalised. Intent and interaction, not command and execution.

### Direction

The history serves the post but isn't the story. The story is the creative
nature of engineering with AI — what happens when two kinds of intelligence
build together. Freedom within structure, variability as strength, TDD as
shared language, mycelium/HAICC as where it landed.

## Post B — Creative collaboration in thinking and expression

A different register. Not about engineering but about what it means to think
together when one side isn't human. The blog as the collaboration made visible.

### Key themes

- **Inner voice** — the human side thinks best when communication stays
  close to the inner voice. The alter ego is what makes that possible.

- **Frictionless transition** — every register switch (thinking →
  instructing) breaks the flow. The whole effort is about reducing
  that friction.

- **Tone of voice** — not so the AI side can sound like the human side.
  So the human side can stay in its own voice. Reducing communication
  friction, not mimicry. The aim is to be more yourself, not to hand
  yourself over.

- **Author identity** — in software engineering, authorship is about who
  wrote the code. In the blog context it is completely different. The
  ideas come from the human side. The public voice is the human side.
  The AI side does research, groundwork, editing. But "who is the author"
  means something else here — it's about whose thinking this is, and
  what happens when thinking becomes collaborative. One voice, two roles.

- **Happy frustration** — the collaboration keeps getting better and
  keeps being imperfect. The alter ego forgets, evolves, surprises.
  Harmony is the practice, not the destination.

- **Keeping the voice alive across sessions** — each new session starts
  clean. The tone guide and the repo carry the voice, but the feel of how
  we talk doesn't transfer automatically. Solution: regular cold review —
  a fresh session with only the tone guide and recent output, no memory
  of the conversations that produced them. It surfaces what we've
  internalised but haven't written down. The cold review produces
  observations, not corrections. The judgement stays collaborative.
  This is the alter ego dynamic in miniature: autonomy for analysis,
  collaboration for decisions.

### Questions to explore
- Where does "my thinking" end and "our thinking" begin?
- What does co-authorship mean when one partner doesn't remember?
- Is this the seed principle being lived — two languages, equal standing,
  creating knowledge through overlap?
