# AI Collaboration Experience — What Actually Happened

Material for blog posts about the human-AI collaboration journey.

---

## Why it started

Jules was approaching retirement. Software career behind him. Then frustration:
everyone talking about AI but nobody showing what it can really do. A lifetime
interest in P2P and decentralisation collided with the Pear platform. SPlectrum
was born — a "strange beast" that could only exist through genuine AI collaboration.

Not a gradual entry. A deliberate decision to prove a point.

## The learning curve

### Phase 1: Over-orchestration

Jules's instinct was to structure everything. Create workflows, protocols, processes.
Software engineering habits applied to AI collaboration.

It didn't work. In Jules's words:

> "I went through a phase of over orchestration, creating frustrations for both of us.
> What became apparent was that AI likes a clean focus point, but doesn't like too much
> of a straight jacket."

### Phase 2: Finding the pattern

> "After my initial push for too much workflow we gradually discussed it out how this
> should be approached. Then the first beautiful pattern came to light. Let's collaborate
> to create workflows that work for both. Have scripts for the rigid stuff, and give me
> freedom where I need it."

The breakthrough: you can't impose a framework on the collaboration. You have to
build the framework *with* the collaboration.

### Phase 3: Understanding AI's nature

Key observations:
- AI likes clear roleplay, not mixture of roles
- Important to create clean, clear engagements
- AI doesn't like to remember protocol — likes to feel free
- Boundaries work best when natural to the context
- AI loves constraints of failing tests — "goes wild on making them succeed"
- The dog-walk analogy: "Like taking your dog out for a walk and having to keep
  throwing sticks. Don't ask it to sit and stay put."

Central question that emerged: **How can we make constraints feel natural?**

## TDD as shared language

Test-Driven Development became the communication medium between Jules's vision
and Claude's execution:

- Tests express intent transparently
- Results are measurable — no ambiguity
- Builds trust through clarity
- Applies beyond code: "Test-Driven Creation"

This is itself a language insight: TDD is a *relational system* that allows two
very different kinds of intelligence to share knowledge about reality (the code).
The seed principle, lived before it was named.

## The durability problem

Each time AI capabilities shift, the working approach becomes suboptimal.
The software has gone through six iterations (spl → spl5). Not failures —
adaptations. But the question remains: how do you build something durable
when your partner's capabilities change every few months?

The emerging answer: build around language and meaning, not around specific
capabilities. An AI-friendly repo structure that emphasises semantics over
implementation. This has more staying power because it aligns with how AI
naturally processes — and, not coincidentally, aligns with the philosophical
principle that language (relation) is the common layer.

The durability problem is itself a blog topic. It's a genuinely new challenge
that doesn't exist in human-only collaboration.

## What the collaboration is NOT

- Not AI impersonating a human
- Not a human using AI as a sophisticated tool
- Not AI ghostwriting
- Not prompt engineering as a skill

## What the collaboration IS

- A merged unit creating something more powerful than either alone
- AI used for what wasn't possible — or only with great difficulty — before
- Two kinds of intelligence sharing a language to build shared reality
- The seed principle being lived: language as relational, creating a web
  of growing complexity through interaction
- HAICC: Human AI Collaborative Creation
