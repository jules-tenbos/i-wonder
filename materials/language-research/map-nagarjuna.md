# Nagarjuna — Map

## Initial phrases

"Language and conceptual thought (prapanca) are structured by binary oppositions that do not correspond to how things are."

All phenomena are shunya (empty) of inherent existence.

The ultimate truth is the cessation of all conceptual proliferation.

Yet: "without relying on convention, the ultimate cannot be taught."

---

## Mapping statement

Nagarjuna's "all phenomena are empty of inherent existence" is deeply relational — nothing exists on its own, everything depends on relations. This overlaps with how we think: what a language gives access to depends on what it relates to, and nothing exists independently of the relational.

He sees conceptual proliferation (prapanca) as the problem and its cessation as the goal. We see proliferation differently — many languages, many angles, which may be pointing in the same direction. His concern is soteriological: cessation leads to liberation. Ours is structural: proliferation is many languages at work. Two stances toward the same observation that language proliferates, each with its own purpose.

"Without relying on convention, the ultimate cannot be taught." His definition of language and ours differ in scope — he draws the boundary of what language can reach in a different place than we do. Two definitions, each drawing the boundary differently.

Where we differ: Nagarjuna's project is soteriological — aimed at liberation from suffering through the cessation of conceptual attachment. Our project is structural — mapping the relational across languages. His emptiness leads to letting go. Our relational leads to recognising more. Different directions from the same observation that nothing exists independently.
