# Heidegger — Map

## Initial phrases

"Language is the house of Being." (Letter on Humanism)

"Man acts as though he were the shaper and master of language, while in fact language remains the master of man." ("...Poetically Man Dwells...")

"Language speaks." (Die Sprache spricht) — not we who speak language, but language that speaks through us. (On the Way to Language)

"To undergo an experience with language means to let ourselves be properly concerned by the claim of language by entering into and submitting to it." (On the Way to Language)

In Being and Time: the distinction between Rede (discourse — language as it opens up the world) and Gerede (idle talk — language that has lost its connection to what it discloses).

Poetry as the primordial form of language — where Being comes to presence.

Language as the place where aletheia (unconcealment) happens — truth is not correspondence but the event of something coming into the open, and that event is linguistic.

## Mapping statement

Heidegger sees language as where what is there comes into view. Language is not a tool for describing a pre-existing reality — it is where reality discloses itself. His world-disclosure, his clearing (Lichtung), his projection (Entwurf) all describe how language opens and closes possibilities, maintains the space in which things can appear at all.

This is close to what we mean by reach — what a language gives access to depends on what it relates to. And the reciprocity is there: language shapes reality, and how we relate shapes the language.

His "language speaks" — language is not our instrument, we are participants in it — is consistent with how we think about the subject's place within language: experiencing through it, not standing outside directing it.

Where we differ: Heidegger's concern is the question of Being — a specific philosophical inquiry rooted in the phenomenological tradition. Our concern is the relational structure of language across all its types. His later work moves toward a listening, receptive stance — letting Being speak through language — which is a different posture from our relational activity. Two concerns, two stances, overlapping where language is constitutive of what comes into view.
