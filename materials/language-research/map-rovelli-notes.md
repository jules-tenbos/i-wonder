# Rovelli — Points of Interest (appended to existing mapping)

The mapping statement in 004 stands. These notes capture additional material from further research into RQM and its current context.

## Interaction density and relational density

RQM's sparse ontology says properties flash into existence at interactions. But the number of interactions matters. A particle correlated with one system is quantum-fragile — barely real. A particle correlated with a billion environmental systems through decoherence is effectively classical — as real as anything gets. Reality thickens with interaction count.

This maps directly onto our principle line 5: complexity is relational density, and relational density increases as knowledge grows. The quantum-to-classical transition is relational density at work — what looks like the same structural pattern — whether it holds across every level is part of what we explore.

---

## The third-person problem and the private language argument

RQM's central difficulty: if facts are only relative to one system, how do two observers compare notes and find consistency? There is an analogy with Wittgenstein's private language problem — purely private meaning is incoherent, meaning must be grounded in shared practice. Both concern how what is private or relative becomes shared.

One proposed resolution: the observed system sits in the middle as a shared node where both interactions are a reality. The density of physical interaction (decoherence) makes facts effectively shared — not through an abstract postulate but through the accumulation of physical correlations. This is one direction being explored; the third-person problem remains an open question in quantum foundations, with competing proposals and no settled resolution.

---

## The field is moving toward relational views

The discussion around quantum foundations has shifted. The debate is no longer primarily anti-RQM. No-go theorems (Bell-Wigner mashups, Frauchiger-Renner) have produced results suggesting that absolute, observer-independent facts about measurement outcomes are untenable. The pressure is pushing the whole field toward some kind of observer-relativity.

There is now a family of related approaches: RQM, QBism, perspectival quantum realism, Healey's pragmatism, the Zeilinger-Brukner information-theoretic view. They differ in calibration — how relational you need to be — but they form a cluster converging on the same territory. The central open problem in quantum foundations is now how to make a relational picture work, not whether to have one.

This shift is additional support for us. The most elementary level of physical description is moving toward relational — independently, from within its own domain, driven by its own evidence. That the same structural pattern emerges there, arrived at from physics rather than from philosophy of language, strengthens the convergence claim.

## Generalisation across levels

The relational pattern holds across domains: quantum interactions (properties only in relation), chemistry (bonding as interaction, molecular stability as interaction density), biology (autopoiesis — a cell constitutes itself through the game of metabolism), ecology (niche and role as relational — a predator without prey is not a predator), social reality (fields, institutions, capital — all constituted through interaction). At each level, reality presents itself contextually, and the robustness of what presents itself scales with interaction density.

From where we stand, this supports the principle's extension claim — that the relational pattern spans the full range from physical to social. Whether these are instances of the same structural pattern or analogies that share a family resemblance is part of what the work explores.

---

## Epistemological, not ontological

The strongest framing is epistemological: reality in context. Not claiming what reality IS in itself, but how it is accessible — always in context, always through interaction, always within a language. The attempt to strip away context and see the thing in itself is not a difficult task we haven't yet accomplished but a structural impossibility. This aligns with our epistemological reality (identity observation 7) and with Kant's thing-in-itself read as reality in potential.

## Rovelli and Nagarjuna

Rovelli has explicitly connected RQM to Nagarjuna's Buddhist philosophy of emptiness (sunyata) — the idea that nothing has intrinsic, independent existence. This is a connection Rovelli himself draws, not one we impose. It supports the convergence claim from yet another direction: physics, philosophy of language, and Buddhist philosophy arriving at the relational independently.
