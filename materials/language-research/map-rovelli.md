# Rovelli — Relational Quantum Mechanics — Map

## Initial phrases

"Quantum mechanics is a theory about the physical description of physical systems relative to other physical systems, and this is a complete description of the world." (Relational Quantum Mechanics, 1996)

"The world is not a collection of things, it is a collection of events... the world is made of relations, not of things."

Properties of a physical system — position, momentum, spin — exist only relative to another physical system. There are no observer-independent properties. What is real for one system need not be real for another.

"The world described by quantum mechanics is a world of interactions, not of objects with definite properties."

---

## Mapping statement

Rovelli's relational quantum mechanics states that properties of a physical system exist only relative to another physical system. There are no observer-independent properties. From where we stand, this is the same structural pattern we see across all language types: a subject's reality is what their language gives access to. What is real within one interaction need not be real within another.

The claim follows from taking quantum mechanics at face value without adding hidden variables or observer-independent states. The resistance to it is large, because established thinking wants observer-independent reality: a view from nowhere, facts that hold regardless of who is looking. From within our principle, this is the same resistance we encounter: the expectation that there is a reality independent of the language through which it is accessed. The field is moving, however — no-go theorems for observer-independent facts are pushing quantum foundations toward relational views, and a family of related approaches (RQM, QBism, perspectival realism) is converging on the same territory.

Where Rovelli stays within physics, we recognise the pattern across the full range of language types. His framework does not require our extension, and ours does not depend on his. But the structural parallel is there, arrived at from very different starting points — physics and the philosophy of language — which makes it a case of independent convergence on the relational.
