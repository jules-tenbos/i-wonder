# Searle — Map

## Initial phrases

"Speech acts are the basic units of linguistic communication, governed by constitutive rules."

"Language is the fundamental social institution that makes all other institutions possible."

Collective intentionality and status functions: "X counts as Y in context C."

Brute facts (observer-independent) vs institutional facts (observer-dependent, constituted through collective agreement).

---

## Mapping statement

Searle's "X counts as Y in context C" describes a precise mechanism for how language constitutes reality. Money, marriage, borders — real because subjects converge on them. The status function only works within a context: same X, different context, different Y. This is close to how we think about reach — what a language gives access to depends on what it relates to.

His "language is the fundamental social institution that makes all other institutions possible" is a strong claim: language does not describe institutional reality, it constitutes it. Without language, no institutions. Collective intentionality — shared intention that creates shared reality — is convergence of subjects at work. This overlaps with principle line 3: language is where subjects share knowledge about reality, and the only objectivity known is convergence of subjects.

Where we differ: Searle draws a hard line between brute facts and institutional facts. Brute facts — a rock, a mountain, gravity — exist independently of any observer, any language, any agreement. Institutional facts are constituted through language. For us, there is no such line. All reality is accessed through a language. His brute facts are a view from nowhere — observer-independent existence. From within our thinking, the rock exists as accessed through a language; what it is beyond any language's reach is reality in potential, not a brute fact that just is.

His mechanism — "X counts as Y in context C" — describes reality constituted through collective agreement within a context. From where we stand, the structural pattern is recognisable beyond the domain he confines it to. He draws the line at collective intentionality; we see context-dependent constitution more broadly. But the extension is ours, not his — his line is central to his framework, not an oversight.
