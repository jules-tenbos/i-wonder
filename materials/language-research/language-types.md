# Language Types

Instances of language in action, categorised by domain. Each entry is a specific practice or system -- not a philosophical position about what language is in general.

121 language types across 14 categories.

---

## 1. Natural / verbal

1.1. Sign languages (ASL, BSL, etc.) -- Full natural languages realized in a visual-gestural modality rather than speech

1.2. Co-speech gesture -- Spontaneous hand and body gestures that form an integrated system with spoken language (McNeill)

1.3. Body language / kinesics -- Communicative system of facial expressions, posture, and movement operating below conscious awareness (Birdwhistell, Ekman, Mehrabian)

1.4. Inner speech -- Internalized social speech that mediates verbal thought, structurally distinct from external speech (Vygotsky)

1.5. Poetic language -- Language in which the poetic function dominates, foregrounding sound patterns, rhythms, and material form (Jakobson, Shklovsky)

1.6. Literary language (readerly/writerly) -- Prose and fiction operating through intertextuality, multiple codes, and productive polysemy (Barthes)

1.7. Oral tradition / songlines -- Language as constitutive element of land and cosmology, sung into existence (Aboriginal Australian traditions)

1.8. Vedic sacrificial speech (Vac) -- Performative and cosmogonic language operating through ritual chant (Rigveda)

1.9. Mantra / dharani -- Sacred syllabic language where sound itself operates independently of semantic content (Hindu and Buddhist traditions, Kukai)

1.10. Koans -- Linguistic formulations designed to frustrate conceptual thinking and provoke non-conceptual realization (Zen tradition, Dogen)

1.11. Ecstatic utterance (shath) -- Speech erupting from mystical experience, not subject to ordinary truth conditions (Sufi tradition, al-Hallaj)

1.12. Whakapapa -- Genealogical narrative recitation constitutive of reality, linking all beings through named relationships (Maori tradition)

1.13. Nommo / kuma -- Generative speech as vital creative force activating existence (Dogon and Mande traditions)

1.14. Navajo ceremonial speech (saad) -- Language as cosmogonic and restorative force, creating and restoring hozhoo (beauty/balance) (Navajo / Dine tradition)

1.15. Speech acts -- Performative utterances that do things rather than describe things: promising, marrying, sentencing, baptizing (Austin, Searle)

## 2. Formal / mathematical

2.1. Formal logic / Begriffsschrift -- Notation whose syntactic structure perfectly mirrors logical structure, eliminating ambiguity (Frege, Tarski)

2.2. Lambda calculus -- Formal system expressing computation through function abstraction and application (Church)

2.3. Mathematical language (general) -- Symbolic system for describing the structure of reality through triangles, circles, and geometric figures (Galileo, Wigner)

2.4. Topological language -- Vocabulary for properties of spaces preserved under continuous deformation (connectivity, genus, homotopy)

2.5. Algebraic language -- Naming of abstract structures (groups, rings, fields) defined by axiomatic relationships

2.6. Analytic language -- Terms for the behavior of functions at boundaries and infinities (limits, continuity, convergence)

2.7. Category-theoretic language -- Meta-language describing structural relationships between mathematical theories (Mac Lane)

2.8. Probability / statistical language -- Vocabulary for uncertainty, distributions, and stochastic patterns applied across domains

---

## 3. Computational

3.1. Programming languages (general) -- Formal notation for specifying processes, with explicit syntax, semantics, and pragmatics (Turing, Church, Kay, Backus)

3.2. Literate programming -- Programs written primarily as essays for human readers, with machine execution secondary (Knuth)

3.3. Object-oriented message passing -- Language paradigm where autonomous objects communicate by sending messages (Kay, Smalltalk)

3.4. Functional programming -- Language paradigm based on combining pure functions without mutable state (Backus)

3.5. Shannon information coding -- Mathematical framework for quantifying and transmitting information through noisy channels (Shannon)

3.6. Kolmogorov complexity -- Measure of algorithmic information content via shortest program that produces a string (Kolmogorov)

---

## 4. Musical

4.1. Musical language (general / tonal) -- System with phonology (tones, timbres), syntax (harmony, counterpoint, form), and non-referential meaning (Bernstein, Langer)

4.2. Musical notation -- Syntactically differentiated notational system for recording temporal art (Goodman)

4.3. Intervallic/emotional musical language -- Specific intervallic patterns carrying consistent emotional meanings within the Western tonal tradition (Cooke)

4.4. Music as social language -- Musical materials carrying historical and social sediment; tonality as ideology (Adorno)

4.5. Ethnomusicological language -- Music as species-wide structured communicative practice constituting social experience (Blacking)

4.6. Music as expectation system -- Meaning generated through the interplay of expectation, fulfillment, and frustration (Meyer)

---

## 5. Visual / artistic

5.1. Painting as symbol system -- Syntactically and semantically dense visual system where every mark can be significant (Goodman)

5.2. Visual grammar of elements -- Point, line, plane, and color as generative vocabulary with combinatorial rules (Klee, Kandinsky)

5.3. Photographic language (studium/punctum) -- Indexical sign system combining cultural codes with irreducibly personal piercing details (Barthes)

5.4. Photography as social practice -- Grammar of seeing that teaches what to look at and how, asserting "this is" without argumentation (Sontag)

5.5. Film montage language -- Meaning created through collision/juxtaposition of shots rather than inherent shot meaning (Eisenstein)

5.6. Film semiotics (grande syntagmatique) -- Typology of how shots combine into sequences in narrative film (Metz)

5.7. Shot/editing/lighting/composition language -- Vocabulary for spatial, temporal, luminous, and chromatic composition in cinema

5.8. Sound design language -- Terms for diegetic/non-diegetic sound relationships, foley, soundscapes, and leitmotif

5.9. Colour language in cinema -- Vocabulary for emotional and narrative use of colour: grading, temperature, palettes

5.10. Semiotic visual codes -- Layered system of denotation (what is shown) and connotation (what is culturally implied) in images (Barthes, Peirce)

5.11. Icon / index / symbol trichotomy -- Classification of signs by resemblance, causal connection, or convention (Peirce)

---

## 6. Performative

6.1. Dance notation / Labanotation -- Systematic notation for human movement identifying Space, Time, Weight/Force, and Flow (Laban)

6.2. Dance as primordial meaning -- Kinesthetic experience as the most primordial form of meaning-making, prior to verbal language (Sheets-Johnstone)

6.3. Theatre of Cruelty language -- Language of space, gesture, light, sound, breath, and bodily presence operating directly on the senses (Artaud)

6.4. Poor theatre / actor's body -- The trained actor's body and voice as total instrument of expression through psychophysical action (Grotowski)

6.5. Theatrical multi-channel sign system -- Simultaneous deployment of linguistic, paralinguistic, kinesic, proxemic, scenic, and vestimentary signs (Elam, Pavis)

6.6. Performance as world-making -- Ritual, play, sports, and social performances as constitutive of social reality (Schechner)

6.7. Liturgical / ritual language -- Sacred performative language establishing relationship between human and sacred, collapsing temporal distance

---

## 7. Scientific / technical

7.1. Chemical notation -- Hybrid system (part iconic, part symbolic) for representing molecular structure, composition, and transformation (Lavoisier, Hoffmann)

7.2. Periodic table -- Classificatory and predictive language encoding elemental properties and relationships (Mendeleev)

7.3. Medical language (anatomical) -- Shared coordinate system mapping the body into named territories with millimeter precision

7.4. Medical language (diagnostic) -- System converting subjective patient experience into clinical categories linked to decision trees

7.5. Medical language (clinical presentation) -- Grammar ordering information by demographics, temporality, quality, and anatomy for pattern recognition

7.6. Engineering language (tolerances and specifications) -- Language of constraints, acceptable deviations, and mandatory/recommended/optional requirements

7.7. Engineering systems language -- Vocabulary for feedback loops, damping ratios, resonance, steady state, and transient response

7.8. Engineering failure mode language -- Terms naming the ways things break (fatigue, creep, buckling, shear, brittle fracture)

---

## 8. Craft / practice

8.1. Carpentry and joinery language -- Vocabulary for joints, wood behavior, and tool feel embedded in physical practice

8.2. Blacksmithing language -- Compressed operational terms assuming knowledge of heat, force, timing (drawing out, upsetting, scarfing)

8.3. Weaving language -- Structural vocabulary (warp, weft, shed, tabby, twill) encoding binary interlacement logic

8.4. Craft as material dialogue -- Reciprocal language between maker and material, "listening to the material" (Sennett, Pye)

8.5. Wabi-sabi / mingei -- Japanese aesthetic language of imperfection, impermanence, and beauty inseparable from material and use (Yanagi Soetsu)

8.6. Culinary language (classical French) -- Technical vocabulary compressing complex procedures into single terms (mise en place, deglaze, roux)

8.7. Wine tasting language -- Contested sensory vocabulary combining analytical chemistry, metaphor, and systematic descriptors (Noble)

8.8. Flavour / umami language -- Perceptual categories created by naming (umami as a distinct taste unrecognized in Western language until named)

8.9. Texture language -- Terms for mouth-feel (al dente, unctuous, toothsome) with precision absent from everyday speech

8.10. Agricultural / soil language -- Vocabulary encoding multi-generational observation: tilth, friable, hungry land, heart

8.11. Phenological language -- Terms naming stages of plant development tied to seasonal cues (germination, bolting, anthesis)

8.12. Permaculture language -- Relational vocabulary naming patterns of interaction between organisms and landscape (guild, edge effect, stacking functions)

8.13. Indigenous ecological vocabularies -- Verb-based and process-based ecological terms encoding distinctions absent in Western languages (Kimmerer, Potawatomi)

8.14. Animal husbandry language -- Terms carrying age, sex, reproductive status, and economic value in single words (hogget, gimmer, tup, wether)

8.15. Nautical language -- Vessel-relative directional and functional vocabulary evolved under extreme environmental pressure (port, starboard, halyard, sheet)

8.16. Sports language (chess) -- Complete notation system plus strategic concepts enabling new kinds of thinking (zugzwang, prophylaxis, tempo)

8.17. Sports language (cricket) -- Rich specialized vocabulary mapping a space of tactical and physical possibilities opaque to outsiders

8.18. Martial arts language -- Japanese/Chinese vocabulary inseparable from the art, retaining untranslatable nuance (judo, kata, randori)

8.19. Climbing language -- Terms simultaneously describing geometry and instructing movement (slab, crack, layback, gaston)

8.20. Surfing language -- Vocabulary simultaneously technical and embodied for waves, manoeuvres, and ocean conditions

## 9. Institutional

9.1. Legal language -- System of rules with irreducible "open texture," requiring meta-rules for recognition, change, and adjudication (Hart, Dworkin)

9.2. Financial / market language -- Vocabulary creating entities that exist only by legal-linguistic convention (derivatives, securities, CDOs)

9.3. Accounting language -- Conventions constructing (not describing) economic reality through agreed-upon categories (goodwill, depreciation, accrual)

9.4. Central bank language -- Performative discourse where the saying itself changes market behavior (forward guidance, quantitative easing)

9.5. Military strategic language -- Terms compressing complex strategic analyses (deterrence, escalation dominance, Schwerpunkt)

9.6. Military tactical language -- Spatial and temporal battlefield terms (enfilade, defilade, dead ground, fire and manoeuvre)

9.7. Military command language -- Maximally compressed, unambiguous orders in standardized formats for survival under stress

9.8. Military euphemistic language -- Terms creating psychological distance from violence (collateral damage, neutralize, enhanced interrogation)

9.9. Psychoanalytic language -- Vocabulary naming hidden processes (unconscious, repression, transference, resistance) that the subject cannot articulate (Freud)

9.10. Lacanian language -- The unconscious structured like a language; condensation/displacement as metaphor/metonymy (Lacan)

9.11. DSM diagnostic language -- Classificatory grid converting the continuum of mental suffering into discrete diagnostic categories

9.12. CBT language -- Vocabulary enabling patients to observe their own mental processes (cognitive distortion, automatic thought, schema)

9.13. Popular therapeutic language -- Clinical terms migrated to everyday speech transforming self-understanding (boundaries, trauma, gaslighting, toxic)

9.14. Architectural pattern language -- Generative hierarchy of named design patterns combining like words in a grammar (Alexander)

9.15. Architectural semiotics -- Buildings as sign systems with denotative functions (a roof denotes shelter) and connotative functions (Eco, Venturi, Jencks)

9.16. Philosophy of language as first philosophy -- Philosophical language in which theory of meaning is foundational, meaning must be manifestable in behaviour, and theory of meaning determines metaphysics (Dummett)

9.17. Arabic grammatical tradition -- Systematic description of Arabic's deep rational structure (qiyas), including the tawqif/istilah debate on divine vs. conventional origin, and Ibn Jinni's sound-meaning affinity (munasaba) (Sibawayhi, Ibn Jinni)

## 10. Animal communication

10.1. Bee dance language (waggle dance) -- Symbolic communication encoding direction, distance, and quality of food sources through stereotyped dances (von Frisch)

10.2. Primate alarm calls -- Functionally referential acoustically distinct calls for specific predator types eliciting appropriate evasive behavior (Seyfarth, Cheney)

10.3. Primate gesture and social cognition -- Flexible intentional gestures in great apes, with audience awareness and contextual deployment (de Waal)

10.4. Whale song -- Hierarchically structured, culturally transmitted songs that change progressively within and across populations (Payne, Whitehead)

10.5. Dolphin signature whistles -- Individually distinctive identity signals functionally analogous to names (Janik)

10.6. Dolphin artificial language comprehension -- Demonstrated capacity to understand syntax-based gestural languages in controlled conditions (Herman)

10.7. Bird song -- Learned, culturally transmitted vocalizations with hierarchical syntax-like structure and dialect variation (Marler, Catchpole)

10.8. Cephalopod chromatic displays -- Rapid combinatorial visual communication through skin color, pattern, texture, and posture changes (Hanlon)

10.9. Great ape symbol use (lexigrams, ASL) -- Learned use of arbitrary symbols with limited combination, demonstrating referential but not full syntactic capacity (Savage-Rumbaugh, Kanzi)

---

## 11. Plant signalling

11.1. Volatile organic compound (VOC) signalling -- Airborne chemical signals released during herbivory that trigger defense responses in neighboring plants (Karban, Baldwin)

11.2. Mycorrhizal network communication ("wood wide web") -- Transfer of carbon, nutrients, and defense signals between trees through underground fungal networks (Simard)

11.3. Root-to-root signalling -- Stress signals transmitted through root systems alerting neighbors to drought, salt stress, or herbivory (Novoplansky)

11.4. Plant herbivory response cascade -- Multi-channel response integrating jasmonic acid signaling, VOC emission, and parasitoid attraction

---

## 12. Cellular / molecular

12.1. Cell signalling (signal transduction) -- Chemical communication through hormones, paracrine signals, and neurotransmitters binding to receptors and triggering cascades

12.2. Bacterial quorum sensing -- Autoinducer-mediated density-dependent chemical coordination of collective bacterial behavior (Bassler)

12.3. Immune system sign-reading -- Distributed cognitive network of molecular recognition, memory, learning, and self/non-self discrimination (Jerne, Cohen)

12.4. Gene regulation networks -- Internal genome communication where genes regulate other genes through transcription factors, enhancers, and small RNAs (Jacob, Monod)

12.5. Epigenetic signalling -- Information layer altering gene accessibility without changing DNA sequence (methylation, histone modification)

---

## 13. Genetic

13.1. Genetic code (DNA/RNA triplet code) -- Mapping of 64 codons to 20 amino acids and stop signals, with explicit linguistic metaphors: transcription, translation, reading frame (Crick)

13.2. Central Dogma information flow -- Directional flow DNA to RNA to protein, framed as transcription and translation (Crick)

---

## 14. Boundary / physical

14.1. Chemical gradient signalling -- Morphogen concentration gradients specifying spatial position in biological and pre-biological systems (Turing, Prigogine)

14.2. Thermodynamic information exchange -- Physical information transfer as reduction of uncertainty, independent of meaning (Shannon, Landauer)

14.3. Bateson's "difference that makes a difference" -- Any causal process where differences propagate across a system, the most maximally inclusive conception (Bateson)
