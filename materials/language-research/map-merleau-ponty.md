# Merleau-Ponty — Map

## Initial phrases

"The body is our general medium for having a world." (Phenomenology of Perception)

"Speech is a genuine gesture, and it contains its meaning in the same way as the gesture contains its." (Phenomenology of Perception)

"It is the body which points out, and which speaks." — thought comes into being through the act of expressing.

Parole parlante (speaking speech — creative, living language in the act of expressing) and parole parlée (spoken speech — sedimented, habitual, already-said).

"Motor intentionality" — speaking is rooted in the lived body's relation to the world.

The chiasm (The Visible and the Invisible) — the reversibility of touching and being touched, seeing and being seen. Extended to language: no clean separation between expressing and being expressed, between the speaker and what is spoken.

Flesh (chair) — not subject, not object, but the tissue of which both are made.

---

## Mapping statement

Merleau-Ponty's chiasm — the reversibility of touching and being touched, seeing and being seen — describes a world where there is no clean separation between the one relating and what is related to. The relation is prior to the terms it relates. Extended to language: the speaker and what is spoken are not separate; they are woven together in the act of expression.

His flesh (chair) — not subject, not object, but the tissue of which both are made — is a specific claim: the relational is prior to the distinction between subject and object. James arrives at a similar observation from radical empiricism: pure experience as one primal stuff, with subject and object as different roles within it. Two traditions, phenomenological and pragmatist, converging on the same structural point.

His parole parlante (speaking speech) and parole parlée (spoken speech) distinguish the creative act of expression from sedimented, habitual language. Language in the act of creating and language already settled. This parallels Saussure's langue/parole but from the lived body rather than the system: Saussure sees the structure and the use; Merleau-Ponty sees the living gesture and the habit.

"Thought comes into being through the act of expressing" — expression is not reporting a pre-existing thought. The thought forms in the expressing. This connects to Dewey's observation that events pass from "external pushing and pulling" to revealing themselves through language. Expression is constitutive, not descriptive.

His framework — body as medium, expression as bodily gesture, motor intentionality — places language in the body's relation to the world. This is consistent with Lakoff's embodied cognition and with the embodied cognition research on sensorimotor reactivation. The body is where language lives, and language is how the body reaches toward the world.
