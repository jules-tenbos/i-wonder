# Saussure — Map

## Initial phrases

"Language (langue) is a system of differences without positive terms."

A sign unites a signifier and a signified — the link between them is arbitrary.

"In language there are only differences." Meaning arises from oppositions within the system, not from any sign in isolation.

The distinction between langue (the system, the structure) and parole (actual speech, individual use).

---

## Mapping statement

Saussure's langue is a system of differences — no sign has meaning on its own, meaning arises from relations within the system. His separation of langue (the structure) and parole (the use) echoes the static and the dynamic: the system and what happens when you engage with it.

"In language there are only differences" — meaning arises from distinguishing, not from any positive content in the sign itself. This is the same structural observation that Shannon arrives at from information theory (information as difference against a background), Bateson from ecology and cybernetics (a difference which makes a difference), and Xunzi from 3rd century BCE Chinese philosophy (the purpose of names is to distinguish similarities and differences). Four thinkers, four traditions, four domains — converging on distinguishing as what language fundamentally does.

A pillar in the language field, and the relational nature of meaning — arising from relations, not residing in isolated terms — overlaps directly with how we think.
