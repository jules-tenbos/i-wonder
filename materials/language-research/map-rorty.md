# Rorty — Map

## Initial phrases

"Language is not a medium of representation."

There is no "way the world is" independent of our descriptions.

Languages are contingent tools to be evaluated by usefulness, not accuracy in mirroring nature.

No vocabulary is a "final vocabulary" — none captures how things really are.

---

## Mapping statement

Rorty says language is not a medium of representation — there is no "way the world is" independent of our descriptions. This overlaps with how we think: each language's reach is only accessible through that language. There is no view from nowhere.

His languages are contingent — they evolve on local criteria and could well have been different. We read this as adaptive systems: languages adapt to what they relate to, shaped by local context. Not necessary, not universal, not ranked — contingent, adaptive, each with their own standing.

Rorty's anti-foundationalism rejects essences, final vocabularies, and any claim that one vocabulary captures reality better than another. This is a rejection of content claims — no vocabulary's content mirrors reality. Our claim for a common layer is purely structural: language is relational, whatever its contents. We are not claiming our vocabulary gets reality right. We are claiming all vocabularies work the same way — through relation. He comments on the contents of cloth; we observe that all cloth is woven.

His critique does not touch our structural claim, and our structural claim does not contradict his. He is right that no vocabulary represents reality. We agree. Our claim for a common relational structure across vocabularies is at a different level from his critique — he operates on content, we operate on structure. Two levels, not in each other's way.
