# Standing

Principle statement: **Languages have equal standing in potential**

4 positions pre-categorised under this concept.

---

1. Ibn Arabi | The cosmos is the self-disclosure (tajalli) of God through His Names; everything that exists is a "word" (kalima) of God; language is a barzakh (isthmus) between meaning and sound, between the intelligible and the sensible; poetic and symbolic expressions convey truths that discursive prose cannot.

2. Franz Boas | Each language must be described on its own terms, not forced into categories of another language; languages are not ranked from "primitive" to "advanced"; all languages are equally complex and capable of expressing any thought; linguistic categories are largely unconscious.

3. Alan Turing | A language, at its most reduced, is a set of symbols plus rules for their transformation; a sufficiently powerful symbol system can simulate any other (universality); language is not fundamentally about specific symbols but about the structural relationships and transformation rules those symbols participate in.

4. Alonzo Church | Language is fundamentally about binding and substitution (function abstraction and application); the Church-Turing thesis implies that radically different surface structures can express identical underlying capacities; language is, in some sense, independent of its specific notation.
