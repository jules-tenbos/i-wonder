# Lakoff — Map

## Initial phrases

"Language is embodied cognition; meaning arises from embodied human experience."

Abstract concepts are systematically understood through conceptual metaphors grounded in bodily experience. "More is up" comes from the physical experience of piling things. "Time is money" maps temporal experience onto economic transaction. "Argument is war" structures discourse through conflict.

Image schemas — container, path, balance, up-down — basic relational patterns grounded in bodily interaction. The primitives from which abstract meaning is built.

There is no sharp boundary between language and general cognition.

---

## Mapping statement

Lakoff shows how language structures experience — through what mechanism. Conceptual metaphors map abstract concepts onto bodily experience: quantity onto spatial orientation, time onto economic exchange, reasoning onto physical movement. The abstract is understood through the embodied. This describes the relational at work in subject experience — meaning arises from the body's interaction with the world, not from a separate mental faculty.

His image schemas — container, path, balance, up-down — are relational primitives. They are not words or concepts but patterns of bodily interaction that structure how meaning is built. The building blocks of language are themselves relational, grounded in the body's engagement with what it relates to.

His "no sharp boundary between language and general cognition" connects directly to how we think: language is the medium through which a subject experiences reality, not a layer placed on top of experience. For Lakoff, language and experience are not separate. The body thinks through language, and language thinks through the body.

Conceptual metaphor maps one domain of experience onto another, connected through embodied interaction. "More is up" links quantity and spatial orientation — two domains that have no logical connection, bridged by the body's experience of piling. From where we stand, this cross-domain mapping is recognisable as a pattern: two domains connected through interaction, each retaining its own structure while the connection produces new meaning. Lakoff describes this in his own terms as conceptual metaphor grounded in embodied experience.

The chemical signalling dimension is worth noting. Hormones, neurotransmitters, immune responses are already part of our own embodied experience, though we tend to privilege the electrical and neural. The body Lakoff describes is richer than just the brain. This richness suggests that the gap between human embodied experience and other forms of embodied interaction — plant signalling, cellular processes — may be smaller than assumed. The mechanism may be more general than his specific scope.
