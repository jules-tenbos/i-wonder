# Bhartrhari — Map

## Initial phrases

"Brahman (ultimate reality) is essentially linguistic (Shabda-Brahman)."

All cognition is permeated by language. The sentence, not the word, is the primary unit of meaning, grasped in a flash of intuition (pratibha).

Speech has three levels:
- Pashyanti: visionary, undifferentiated
- Madhyama: mental, internal
- Vaikhari: external, articulated

---

## Mapping statement

Bhartrhari's Shabda-Brahman: ultimate reality is essentially linguistic. Not language describing reality, but reality itself linguistic at its core. This is a stronger claim than ours — we say language is the medium through which a subject experiences reality; he says reality and language are the same thing. His claim is grounded in Vedic metaphysics; ours is structural. Two frameworks, both placing language at the centre, but at different levels: his ontological, ours epistemological.

His "all cognition is permeated by language" — there is no cognition outside language. Sellars arrives at the same observation from analytic philosophy: "all awareness is a linguistic affair." Two traditions, independently, placing language prior to cognition rather than as its instrument.

The sentence, not the word, is the primary unit of meaning — grasped in a flash of intuition (pratibha), then unfolded into parts. The whole is prior to the parts. Ricoeur makes the same structural observation from within hermeneutics. Three traditions (Indian, analytic, hermeneutic) converging on the sentence as the unit where meaning lives — meaning in the relation between terms, not in terms alone.

His three levels of speech — from the undifferentiated vision of pashyanti through the internal formation of madhyama to the articulated expression of vaikhari — describe a movement from the unformed to the formed. In his framework, these are levels of the same linguistic reality manifesting. The movement is from potential to expression — language coming into articulation.
