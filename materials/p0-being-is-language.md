# P0 — Being Implies Language

Discussion notes from the software languages editing session, April 2026.

## How we got here

The software languages post revealed something unexpected: at every level of the software stack, language is already there with full power. Binary isn't primitive — it has the full computational power of any language. Higher languages don't add power, they reduce complexity of expression. The simplest level is the most powerful.

This led to: language is always present. There is no level without it. The full power is an initial given. Creation starts with full potential present and evolves to express itself.

P1-P5 describe what language does. They presuppose being. They never say where language comes from or why it's there. Something is missing at the foundation.

## The progression

**"Being implies language"** — wherever there is being, there is language. You can't have one without the other. Defensible, careful.

**But do we know of anything present that isn't language?** Heidegger's being is already embedded in the world, not standalone. Being means differentiation: something against a background of the rest. A bubble in liquid — there are differences, and there's an interface between the two faces.

**Being without differentiation is nothing.** Differentiation is relation. Relation is language. There's nothing in between. The interface doesn't get added to being — it constitutes being.

**The subtle distinction.** "Being is language" collapses the two into identity. But there's a reason to keep them apart:

- If being IS language, then subject equals being and is always personal. But we recognise being elsewhere — in every entity.
- Being = entity. Not subject — entity. Every entity has being.
- An entity has encapsulated structure (the inside) that determines what buttons appear on its interaction surface (the outside).
- The buttons ARE the language — the relational dynamic the entity exposes.
- Being is more than language: it includes the encapsulated inside that language can't fully express.

**Therefore: P0 Being implies language.** Every entity necessarily exposes an interaction surface. That surface is language. No being without language. But being is more than its surface.

## The bubble model

**Entity as bubble.** An entity is a bubble — encapsulated inside, interaction surface outside. The nature of the inside determines the buttons on the surface.

**Subject = being inside the bubble.** The subject isn't a special kind of entity. It's the view from inside. Sitting inside, looking out through the buttons. Any entity is a subject when you're inside it.

**Everything happens at the surface.** The subject never leaves its bubble. It doesn't reach into the world:
- Buttons get pushed on its outside (relational inward) — the world arriving at the surface
- The subject pushes buttons that project outward (relational outward) — acting in the world
- Both happen at the bubble interface. The subject does not exist beyond.

**Other entities arrive as projections.** The relational nature of language is the projection of other bubbles onto my bubble surface so I can interact with them. I never touch the other entity — only its projection onto my interface.

**Mycelium = the fabric that manages the projections.** What gets projected onto your surface, what your button pushes project onto others. The interface management.

**Heidegger made precise.** The subject doesn't exist beyond its bubble — but through the projections on its surface, it IS in the world. Not by reaching out. By the world arriving at its interface. Being-in-the-world as structural engineering.

## The presocratic connection

The question: what is the fundamental substance? Thales: water. Anaximander: the boundless. Heraclitus: fire/logos. P0 says: wherever there is being, there is language. Not language as words — language as relation, as interaction, as the relational dynamic at the surface of being.

Heraclitus had logos — the rational structure of reality. Rovelli reads Anaximander. Gadamer: "Being that can be understood is language." P0 is in that lineage.

Heidegger: "Language is the house of Being." Close but maintains a distinction — the house and the dweller aren't the same. P0 maintains the distinction too: being implies language, being is not identical to language. The inside is more than the surface.

## What P0 does to the seed

P0 is the prerequisite. P1-P5 describe properties of language. P0 says: language is there in the first place, necessarily, wherever there is being.

- P0: Being implies language (the ground — every entity exposes an interaction surface)
- P1: Language is relational (the nature of the surface)
- P2: Language is the medium of experience (the subject — the view from inside)
- P3: Language is where subjects share (projections between bubbles)
- P4: Languages are inter-relational (the web of projections)
- P5: Together they form growing complexity (the growth — in expression, not in power)

## The inversion

The usual picture: things evolve from simple to complex, from less to more. Binary is primitive, Python is evolved.

P0 inverts this. The power was always there. What evolved is not the capacity but the expression — the vocabulary, the concepts, the thinking tools. Evolution didn't add power. It added perspectives on what was always there.

Creation starts with full potential present and evolves to express itself.

P5 reread: the web of growing complexity isn't growing in power. It's growing in expression. More languages, more perspectives, more ways of engaging what was always there. The complexity is in the articulation, not in the substrate.

## Entities come with buttons to push

Entities aren't inert data that language gets applied to. They come with interaction surfaces — affordances, ways to engage. The slab has weight, shape, graspability. The processor has switches, inputs, states. The word has pronunciation, context, usage.

The nature of the encapsulated inside determines what buttons appear. Language is what happens when entities' buttons get pushed by other entities. The buttons were always there. The full power was always there.

Entity is the static word for being. Being is the verb, entity is the noun. P0 bridges them: an entity in motion implies language. Static entity does nothing. The moment it's in motion — interacting, differentiating, relating — language appears at the surface.

## Outward and inward relational

Both happen at the same surface. The subject never leaves its bubble.

- **Inward**: the surface receives projections from the fabric. Other bubbles' buttons arrive as projections on my interface.
- **Outward**: the surface emits changes that the fabric picks up and projects to others. I act at my surface. The medium carries it. It arrives at their surface.

No spooky action at a distance. Local action only. Surface → medium → surface. The medium (mycelium) shapes what gets carried, how, where. It's not neutral plumbing — it's part of the language.

Like speech: you don't put words into someone's ears. You vibrate air at your location. The medium carries it to their surface.

Like a cell: it expresses proteins on its membrane. Other cells' signals arrive at its receptors. Everything at the membrane. The extracellular medium carries everything.

## The holographic connection

The entity model — encapsulated inside, everything accessible only through the surface — parallels the holographic principle in physics. Bekenstein and Hawking showed that the information content of a black hole is encoded on its boundary surface. 't Hooft and Susskind generalised it. The inside IS what the surface expresses.

You know the subject by its imprint on the fabric. You don't look inside — you read the interface. That's the holographic principle applied to information architecture.

## One entity, multiple realities

An entangled system is one bubble from the perspective of its entangled properties, but two bubbles from the perspective of its decohered properties. Entity boundaries are property-dependent. Encapsulation isn't absolute — it's contextual. The same stuff is encapsulated differently depending on which language game you're in. That's P4 at the deepest level.

And this isn't strange. We live it daily. I as a family person, as a work person, as a hobby person — one entity participating in multiple realities. Each has its own surface, its own buttons, its own language game. They're orthogonal as long as they don't share participants.

It only gets "strange" when realities collide — when a work colleague shows up at the family dinner. Two previously orthogonal surfaces meet and reconciliation is needed. That's the measurement problem in human terms. Decoherence is the reconciliation.

The quantum case isn't strange. Our daily life is already quantum-like. We just don't notice because we call it "wearing different hats."
