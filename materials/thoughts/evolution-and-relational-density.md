# Evolution and Relational Density

## The Statement

Evolution seems to strive to maximise the density of relations/interactions. This is visible on two tracks:

**Knowledge density:** speech → writing → printing → transmission → AI participation. Each step compresses more relational capacity into less effort.

**Physical density:** evolution produces more concentrated systems — more interactions per unit — when the energy cost can be sustained. A cell is denser than a chemical soup. A brain is denser than a gut. A city is denser than a scattered population. But each step has to pay for itself — it's a tendency that runs until the cost can't be met.

The evolution of life in general shows this drive toward higher complexity — not as purpose or teleology, but as an observed pattern.

## How This Fits — Research Findings

The pattern is well-documented across multiple fields. What is distinctive is unifying both tracks (physical and knowledge) under a single metric (density of relations), with the cost constraint as the governor. None of the existing frameworks use "density of relations" as the primary metric applied uniformly across both domains.

### Closest Full Matches

- **Chaisson (Energy Rate Density)** — Single quantitative metric across cosmic history: galaxies (~0.5 erg/s/g) → stars → planets → life → brains (~150,000 erg/s/g) → societies. Energy throughput per unit mass increases by orders of magnitude. Explicitly non-teleological. The closest existing metric to "density of relations" — but uses energy, not relations.
- **Maynard Smith & Szathmary (Major Evolutionary Transitions)** — Each transition (replicating molecules → chromosomes → eukaryotes → multicellular → social → language) involves smaller units combining into larger wholes with new modes of information storage and transmission. Each increases the number and kinds of interactions coordinated. Explicitly non-teleological — contingent events, not a directed programme.
- **Wright (Nonzero)** — Both biological and cultural evolution driven by increasing scope of non-zero-sum interactions. More complex systems enable more mutually beneficial interactions. Very close to the position, but uses game theory. Criticised for flirting with teleology.
- **McShea & Brandon (Biology's First Law / ZFEL)** — In any system with variation and heredity, diversity and complexity tend to increase spontaneously as a statistical default. Not a force — just what happens when variance accumulates.

### On the Energy Cost Constraint

- **Nick Lane** — Mitochondria provided a ~200,000-fold increase in available energy per gene, unlocking eukaryotic complexity. Without this, no multicellularity. The prokaryote-eukaryote transition was an energy bottleneck. Complexity increases only when the energy budget permits.
- **Prigogine (Dissipative Structures)** — Systems far from equilibrium can spontaneously generate order by dissipating energy. Life is a dissipative structure. The more complex, the more energy must be dissipated to maintain it. If energy flow stops, structure dissipates.
- **West, Brown & Enquist (Metabolic Scaling)** — Metabolic rate scales with body mass to the 3/4 power due to fractal resource distribution networks. Formalises why complexity must "pay for itself."
- **Bejan (Constructal Law)** — Flow systems evolve toward configurations that maximise throughput. Predicts that architectures increase the density and efficiency of connections. Controversial — some regard it as a genuine physical law, others as a tautology.

### On the Knowledge Density Track

- **Boyd & Richerson (Dual Inheritance Theory)** — Cultural evolution operates as a second inheritance system alongside genetics. Faster than genetic evolution because of horizontal transmission. Explains why the knowledge track accelerates beyond the biological track. Culture is a faster channel for relational information.
- **Kauffman (Adjacent Possible)** — Each state of a system opens new possible states. Biospheres expand into the adjacent possible as fast as they can without destroying internal organisation. Matches the position: maximise relational possibilities, constrained by maintaining existing organisation.
- **Shannon (Channel Capacity)** — The knowledge density track can be read as a history of increasing channel capacity — each step increases rate and reliability of relational information transmission.
- **Deacon (Incomplete Nature)** — Information, life, and mind emerge through nested levels of constraints. Each level enables the next level of relational complexity. Explicit that this is not teleological.

### Complexity Science

- **Friston (Free Energy Principle)** — Biological systems minimise surprise by building internal models of their environment. Organisms with richer models maintain denser relational maps. The cost constraint appears as the trade-off between model complexity and prediction accuracy.
- **Cronin & Walker (Assembly Theory)** — Quantifies complexity by the minimum steps needed to construct an object. Higher assembly indices indicate more accumulated historical interaction. Attempts to quantify "accumulated relational density." Still controversial.
- **Arthur (Complexity Economics)** — Positive feedback loops cause networks to self-reinforce and grow denser past a threshold. The cost constraint appears as path dependence.

### Biology

- **Dunbar (Social Brain Hypothesis)** — Neocortex size correlates with social group size. The brain evolved to manage relational complexity. Dunbar number (~150) is the cost-limited ceiling on stable social relations.

### The Main Dissent

- **Gould (Full House / Drunkard's Walk)** — The strongest objection. Apparent complexity increase is a statistical artefact: life started near minimum complexity, random walk from a wall can only go one direction. The mean hasn't increased — the tail has. Bacteria remain dominant by biomass, number, and metabolic diversity. Counter-argument: the pattern is too consistent across independent domains (biological, cultural, technological) to be purely a boundary-effect artefact.

### Philosophy of Biology

- **Dennett** — Accepts that evolution produces increasing complexity but insists the mechanism is algorithmic ("cranes, not skyhooks"). Would accept the pattern but reject any hint of "striving."
- **Conway Morris** — Convergent evolution demonstrates evolution is deeply constrained and directional. But draws theistic conclusions — actual teleology. The position stays observational.
- **Teilhard de Chardin** — Evolution has inherent direction toward complexity and consciousness (Omega Point, noosphere). Maps onto both tracks. But explicitly teleological and theological. Largely rejected as unfalsifiable metaphysics. The position explicitly avoids this by saying "seems to strive" — an observation, not an attribution.

### Where This Diverges from Mainstream

| Mainstream tendency | This position |
|---|---|
| Neo-Darwinism: natural selection optimises locally for fitness, not globally for complexity | Observes a pattern of increasing relational density across domains |
| Gould: complexity increase is a statistical artefact of a random walk from a boundary | The consistency across independent domains suggests more than a boundary effect |
| Teleological readings (Teilhard, Conway Morris): actual purpose or design | "Seems to strive" — observed pattern, not attribution of agency |
| MEPP / Bejan: frame tendency in terms of energy flow and entropy production | Uses "density of relations" as the metric — more general than energy alone |
| Existing frameworks address one track (biological or cultural) | Unifies both tracks under a single metric with cost constraint as governor |
