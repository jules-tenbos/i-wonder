# The Recursive Subject

## The Statement

The subject in the principle recurses. An individual is a subject — experiencing reality through a language, from their own position. But a group of individuals sharing a language becomes a subject in its own right, with its own experience, its own knowledge. Groups of groups likewise. The pattern repeats at every scale.

Language games have no prescribed boundaries. Growing food, cooking food, eating food — different games, all intertwined. The person growing is already thinking about how it will cook. The cook is already thinking about how it will be eaten. The eater's experience feeds back into what gets grown. They are separate languages with their own vocabularies, their own expertise, their own standards — but they bleed into each other continuously.

Reality as we experience it is already a web of intertwined languages. We live in this web every day — switching between the language of work, the language of home, the language of the body, the language of food — and we treat it as seamless. It is only when something breaks (a misunderstanding, a culture clash, a translation that does not work) that the boundaries become visible. The inter-relational is not something that has to be constructed. It is already the case. We just do not notice it because we are inside it.

## How This Fits — Research Findings

This thought is an early exploration, not yet fully researched. The connections below indicate directions, not settled positions.

### What the Principle Already Contains

The recursion is implied by statements 2, 3, and 4 of the founding principle taken together:

- **Statement 2** — "Language is the medium through which a subject experiences reality." The subject is a role in the relation, not a kind of entity. Nothing prescribes what can occupy that role.
- **Statement 3** — "Language is where subjects share knowledge about reality." When subjects share, they form a group — and that group has its own shared reality, its own knowledge. It has become a subject.
- **Statement 4** — "Languages are inter-relational and have equal standing in potential." Languages overlap, interact, inform each other. No clear boundaries between them.

The individual-and-group-reality thought already identifies the two-level structure (individual experience, group-level reality). This thought extends it: the two levels are not fixed — they recurse. A group is an individual at the next scale.

### Resonances

- **Elias (Figurations)** — Interlocking networks of interdependent individuals. "Individuals always come in figurations and figurations are always formed by individuals." Neither level reducible to the other. But Elias does not make the recursive move — groups of figurations forming their own figurations.
- **Wittgenstein (Language Games)** — The principle's use of "language" already carries this. Language games are not neatly bounded — they overlap, nest, shift. But Wittgenstein does not develop the group-as-subject recursion.
- **Maturana (Structural Coupling)** — Organisms coupled through interaction produce a domain of coordinated conduct — a language. That domain is itself a new entity that can couple with other domains. The recursion is present in Maturana, though he does not frame it this way.
- **Luhmann (Social Systems)** — Social systems as self-referential, autopoietic systems that operate through communication. A social system is a subject in its own right — it observes, it learns, it has its own reach and limits. The closest systematic treatment of groups as subjects.

### What This Opens

The key observation is that the principle is not describing something exotic or theoretical. It is describing what ordinary experience already is. The intertwining is not a consequence of the principle — it is the starting condition. The principle names the structure that everyday life already has.
