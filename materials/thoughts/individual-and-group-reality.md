# Individual and Group Reality

## The Statement

Reality — as we know it — is experienced personally but exists at the relation (group) level. The individual is always subject to the group-level reality, sometimes unavoidably so — sometimes a victim of it.

The world we know is constructed through relations — shared commitments, collective language, group-level agreements. But access to this reality is always personal. You experience from your own position, never from someone else's. The individual cannot escape the group-level reality they are embedded in.

Two common misunderstandings:
1. Collapse to the individual (solipsism, radical subjectivism) — "my experience is all there is"
2. Collapse to the group (naive realism, scientism) — "what we've agreed on is what's really there"

Both miss that you need both levels and they are not the same level. The relation produces the world we know. The individual is where it lands. Neither is reducible to the other.

## How This Fits — Research Findings

### Closest Full Matches

- **Berger & Luckmann** — *The Social Construction of Reality* (1966). The most systematic match. Three-stage cycle: externalisation (humans create cultural products), objectivation (products take on a reality of their own), internalisation (subsequent generations inherit them as given truth). "Society is a human product. Society is an objective reality. Man is a social product." Institutions "are experienced as possessing a reality of their own, a reality that confronts the individual as an external and coercive fact."
- **Durkheim** — Social facts are "manners of acting, thinking, and feeling external to the individual, which are invested with a coercive power by virtue of which they exercise control over him." The coercion "asserts itself as soon as one tries to resist."
- **Elias** — Figurations: interlocking networks of interdependent individuals. "Individuals always come in figurations and figurations are always formed by individuals." Neither level reducible to the other. Individuals embedded in processes they didn't design and can't fully control.
- **Mannheim** — Relationism (not relativism): all knowledge is bound to a particular location within social structure. Explicitly distinguished from both pure subjectivism and naive objectivism.

### On Being Subject To / Victim Of

- **Fanon** — The sharpest account. "What is called the black soul is a construction by white folk." The colonised individual acquires their body schema under the white gaze — identity imposed from outside, before they can respond. Cannot escape by individual will.
- **Bourdieu** — Habitus: dispositions shaped by social structures, operating below conscious awareness. Symbolic violence: power imposed through language, culture, and norms without the individual knowing. The constraint operates below the level of awareness — experienced as natural disposition, not coercion.
- **Gramsci** — Hegemony: dominant worldview permeates institutions until it appears as common sense. You consent to your own subordination because the constructed reality appears natural.
- **Foucault** — "One is always inside power." Discursive formations determine what can be said, by whom, with what authority. No outside position.
- **Adorno & Horkheimer** — "Totally administered lives." The culture industry produces conformity and undermines individuality. Among the most pessimistic accounts — almost no exit.
- **Marx** — False consciousness: individuals internalise the ideology of the system without knowing it. Commodity fetishism obscures actual social relations.

### On Two-Level Irreducibility

- **Heidegger** — Thrownness (Geworfenheit): the individual is thrown into a pre-existing world of meanings, conventions, and duties they didn't choose. Dasein "stands in subjection" to the anonymous public interpretation of reality (das Man). But Heidegger offers an individualist exit (authentic existence) — this position doesn't.
- **Giddens** — Duality of structure: social structures both constrain and enable individual action. More symmetrical than this position, which leans into the constraint.
- **Bhaskar** — Critical realism: social structures are real, have emergent causal powers, pre-exist individual action. "People never create society from scratch." But Bhaskar is ontologically realist; this position stays epistemological ("as we know it").
- **Schutz** — The lifeworld is intersubjective (meaning negotiated socially), yet each individual carries their own personal lifeworld. Maps precisely onto the two-level structure.

### Phenomenology

- **Merleau-Ponty** — Intercorporeality: the social world is woven into the fabric of our bodies. Perception itself is socially constituted through embodied interaction.
- **Gadamer** — Understanding always occurs from within a horizon shaped by tradition and prejudice. You cannot step outside. But horizons can shift through dialogue.

### Philosophy of Language

- **Wittgenstein** — The private language argument: meaning requires shared practices and public standards of correctness. Even the identity of a private sensation derives from shared practices. There can be no purely individual reality-construction.

### Psychology

- **Vygotsky** — "Every function in the child's cultural development appears twice: first on the social level, and later on the individual level." Cognition is a social product that gets internalised.
- **Mead** — "There can be no self without a prior social group." The self arises through taking the attitude of the generalised other.
- **Gergen** — "All human intelligibility is generated within relationships." The self is constituted by relationships.

### Ethics / Alterity

- **Levinas** — The self is constituted through encounter with the Other. Responsibility is the formative experience of subjectivity — the relation is prior to the individual.

### Non-Western Philosophy

- **Nagarjuna** — Dependent origination: all phenomena arise dependently, nothing has intrinsic independent existence. But Buddhism's no-self doctrine is more radical — it questions even the individual as a real locus of experience.

### Where This Position Is Distinctive

Most traditions lean toward one of two emphases:

1. **Constructionist** (Berger & Luckmann, Gergen, Mead): reality is socially constructed, but the "subject to" and "victim of" dimensions are underplayed. Descriptive, not tragic.
2. **Critical** (Marx, Bourdieu, Foucault, Fanon, Frankfurt School): the individual is subject to and victimised by group-level reality, but these traditions specify a mechanism (class, discourse, habitus, race) and sometimes an exit (revolution, counter-hegemony).

This position combines both without committing to either:
- Insists on irreducibility of both levels
- Insists on the constraint/victimisation dimension
- Stays epistemological ("as we know it"), not ontological
- Does not specify a mechanism or promise an exit
