# Life as Facilitator of Expression

## The Statement

Evolution is a system-level process of increasing relational density, not a species-level process. Gould's objection (bacteria are still dominant, complexity hasn't increased) makes a category error — it measures species when the relevant unit is the whole system. Bacteria aren't a counterexample; they're nodes in a denser network. Mitochondria were bacteria. Gut microbiomes are bacteria. They're embedded in denser relational networks than they were 3 billion years ago.

Life itself is not the subject of evolution but a facilitator of expression — a particularly effective medium for concentrating relations. Chemistry was doing it before life. Life accelerated it. Culture accelerated it further. AI accelerates it again. Complexity and adaptive systems are at the centre, not biology. Biology is one chapter.

Life as we know it is contingent — it didn't have to take this form. But it has occupied Earth so thoroughly that other expressions of relational density could well have been suffocated out of potential existence. Life's success may have closed off alternative paths.

## How This Fits — Research Findings

### Evolution as System-Level Process

- **Maynard Smith & Szathmary (Major Transitions)** — Each transition (replicating molecules → chromosomes → eukaryotes → multicellular → social → language) involves formerly independent entities becoming unable to replicate outside a larger whole. Explicitly about system-level reorganisation. The relevant unit shifts upward at each transition.
- **Okasha (Levels of Selection)** — Major transitions move from aggregate fitness (MLS1) to emergent group fitness (MLS2). The system becomes a genuine unit of selection.
- **Knoll & Bambach (Evolutionary Megatrajectories)** — Linked discrete trends of expanding ecospace. Structured system-level expansions, not random walks. Each megatrajectory opens new relational possibilities.
- **Levin** — Ecosystems and the biosphere are complex adaptive systems. Evolution operates across scales from molecules to the biosphere, not reducible to any single organisational level.
- **Smith & Morowitz** — Life as "the fourth geosphere." A planetary process — a series of non-equilibrium phase transitions opening new channels for energy flow.
- **Lovelock & Margulis (Gaia)** — The biosphere operates as a self-regulating system. Updated by Lenton & Latour's "Gaia 2.0" (2018): human self-awareness adds a new layer to planetary self-regulation.
- **Niche Construction (Odling-Smee, Laland, Feldman)** — Organisms actively construct their environments, generating evolutionary feedback. Cyanobacteria creating Earth's oxygen atmosphere is the canonical example. Evolution is a coupled organism-environment process.

### Life as One Vehicle, Not the Only One

- **Kauffman (Autocatalytic Sets)** — Self-sustaining chemical reaction networks arise almost inevitably in sufficiently complex systems. Organised complexity before and outside biology.
- **Prigogine (Dissipative Structures)** — Self-organising patterns arise spontaneously in far-from-equilibrium systems. Life is one instance of this class, not a category apart. Convection cells, chemical oscillations, hurricanes are others.
- **Morowitz** — Energy flow through a system leads to at least one cycle. Core biochemical pathways are order parameters in phase transitions. The emergence of organised complexity is physics, with biology as one expression.
- **England (Dissipation-Driven Adaptation)** — Groups of atoms driven by an external energy source spontaneously restructure to dissipate more energy. Life-like organisation is a thermodynamic tendency, not specifically biological.
- **Deacon (Incomplete Nature)** — Hierarchy from homeodynamic (thermodynamic flows) to morphodynamic (self-organising) to teleodynamic (coupled self-maintaining systems). The hierarchy runs through physics and chemistry before reaching biology.
- **Cronin & Walker (Assembly Theory)** — Measures complexity across physics and biology on a continuous landscape. Does not define the boundary of evolution at biology. Biology is one region, not the boundary. (Note: theory has faced criticism — abiotic processes can produce high assembly indices.)
- **Cairns-Smith (Genetic Takeover)** — Self-replicating clay crystals may have been the first evolving system, later displaced by organic chemistry. The substrate of evolution can change — one medium can hand off to another.

### On Gould's Category Error

- **Gould (Full House)** — Complexity increase is a statistical artefact. Life started near minimum complexity; random walk from a wall can only go rightward. The mode stays at bacteria.
- **McShea** — Even if individual lineages show no driven trend, the system demonstrably produces new hierarchical levels (cells within cells, organisms within societies). Gould measured the right thing (species complexity distributions) but asked the wrong question. The question is not "has the average species become more complex?" but "has the system's relational architecture deepened?"
- **McShea & Brandon (ZFEL)** — Complexity and diversity tend to increase as a statistical default under variation and heredity. Not an illusion of random walks but a default tendency always present, even when forces oppose it.
- **The mitochondria rebuttal** — Well-established in major transitions literature. Bacteria were incorporated as organelles into eukaryotic cells. Gut microbiomes are functional components of animal physiology. The persistence of bacteria is evidence for system-level complexity increase — they are now embedded in denser relational networks than 3 billion years ago. (The explicit framing as a rebuttal to Gould in terms of "wrong unit of measurement" is original.)

### Life Crowding Out Alternatives

This is the least-developed area in the literature, but several threads converge:

- **Shadow Biosphere Hypothesis (Cleland & Copley, Davies et al.)** — LUCA's descendants may have monopolised resources so thoroughly that alternative biochemistries couldn't survive or would be unrecognisable. Motivated by the concern that familiar life may have dominated to the exclusion of other possibilities.
- **LUCA and Biochemical Monopoly** — All known Earth life descends from a single common ancestor. The standard interpretation: LUCA's lineage outcompeted or consumed all rival chemical systems.
- **Prebiotic Competitive Exclusion** — Recent research (npj Complexity, 2025) shows dominant autocatalytic cycles competitively exclude alternatives without spatial compartmentalisation. Once a successful chemical system takes hold, it crowds out alternatives.
- **England** — In a chemical soup, some configurations "take over at the expense of others" as they prove better at harvesting energy. A general physical principle of competitive exclusion among self-organising systems.
- **Cleland (Quest for a Universal Theory of Life)** — All known life is N=1 (one ancestor). We may need examples of "life as we don't know it" to develop any adequate general theory. Earth life may be unrepresentative.
- **Alternative Biochemistries** — Actively studied in astrobiology: silicon-based life, ammonia solvents, alternative genetic polymers. Different environments may yield fundamentally different biochemistries.
- **The gap:** No major published work explicitly argues the full claim — that life as a category may have closed off non-biological paths to relational density. The prebiotic competition literature addresses competition between chemical systems. The shadow biosphere literature addresses whether alternative life survived. But the specific claim that life may have suffocated non-biological paths to complex organisation is implied by several lines of work but not stated.

### Where This Diverges from Mainstream

| Mainstream tendency | This position |
|---|---|
| Biology treats life as the subject of evolution | Life is a facilitator — one medium for concentrating relations. Biology is one chapter |
| Gould: complexity increase is a statistical artefact | Category error — measures species when the relevant unit is the system |
| Major transitions school: system-level reorganisation (accepted) | Goes further: the system is the only meaningful unit, species are components |
| Thermodynamic approaches (Prigogine, England): complexity as physics | Aligned — but adds "relational density" as the metric rather than energy dissipation |
| Shadow biosphere: alternative life forms may exist | Goes further: life as a category may have closed off non-biological paths to relational density |
| Assembly theory: evolution extends beyond biology | Aligned — life is one region in a continuous complexity landscape |
