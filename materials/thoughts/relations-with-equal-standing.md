# Relations with Equal Standing in Potential

## The Statement

Languages have equal standing in potential. When one language (e.g., human ethics) imposes itself as the standard for another (e.g., animal relations, indigenous practices, religious traditions), it violates this equal standing. This is the same structure whether it is science imposing on religion, Western ethics on indigenous practice, or human frameworks on non-human systems.

Different relational systems — wolf pack dynamics, ant colonies, human legal systems, religious traditions — each have their own rules of engagement, their own "ethics" in the broadest sense. Imposing one system's standards on another is structurally the same move as what Fanon described: constructing the other's identity from your framework. The anthropomorphic projection of human ethics onto animals is an example — like the black soul.

This is not relativism in the sense of "anything goes." Each system has its own internal standards. It is a refusal to let one system claim inherent authority over another — to treat another language as disqualified by nature rather than as a legitimate contender that may differ in development or rigour.

## How This Fits — Research Findings

### Closest Full Matches

- **Uexkull (Umwelt)** — Each organism inhabits its own perceptual and meaningful world, constructed according to its sensory capacities. A sign only has meaning within its Umwelt. The biological grounding for the claim that different relational systems each have their own rules of engagement.
- **Luhmann** — Modern society consists of functionally differentiated subsystems (law, economy, science, politics), each operationally closed with its own binary code (legal/illegal, true/false, payment/non-payment). No subsystem can legitimately impose its code on another.
- **Santos (Ecology of Knowledges)** — Non-hierarchical coexistence of knowledge systems. "Global social justice is not possible without global cognitive justice." Coined "epistemicide" for the destruction of other knowledge systems. "Abyssal thinking" — the invisible line dividing whose knowledge counts.
- **Stengers (Ecology of Practices)** — Different knowledge systems coexist without any claiming authority over others. Distinguishes experts (whose knowledge is accepted as relevant) from diplomats (who negotiate between practices).
- **Feyerabend** — Science has no privileged epistemic status over other forms of knowledge. "Many philosophers gave science privileged status over other epistemological methods, without any argument."

### On Framework Imposition

- **Fanon** — Colonialism constructs the colonised subject's identity from the coloniser's framework. The move of imposing one system's standards on another is structurally what Fanon described.
- **Spivak** — "Epistemic violence": systematic denial of a social group's ability to formulate its own epistemology. Even attempts to "give voice" can reinscribe this violence by translating positions into terms intelligible only to the dominant framework.
- **Plumwood** — Identified a common structure ("master rationality") across human/nature, mind/body, civilised/primitive, reason/emotion dualisms. The same logic of domination operating across domains.
- **Mignolo** — "Epistemic delinking" from Western epistemological hegemony. "Pluriversality" — a plurality of voices and knowledge systems.
- **Smith** — Western research paradigms are "inextricably linked to European imperialism." Indigenous epistemological traditions "are no less rigorous and worthy of respect."
- **Chakrabarty** — European Enlightenment categories presented as universal but historically particular.

### On Anthropomorphism and Animal Relations

- **De Waal** — Identified the double bind: both projecting human categories onto animals (anthropomorphism) and denying continuity (anthropodenial/mechanomorphism) are distortions.
- **Nagel** — We cannot know what it is like for bats to be bats. Each conscious organism has a point of view that cannot be captured by description from outside.
- **Bekoff & Pierce** — Animals exhibit their own moral behaviours — fairness, empathy, trust, reciprocity. But still described in human moral vocabulary, which the position would note as potentially importing one framework's terms.
- **Kohn** — Semiosis (meaning-making) is not exclusively human. All living beings create, interpret, and respond to signs. "An anthropology beyond the human."
- **The wild animal suffering debate** — A live example: utilitarian ethics claiming authority over predator-prey dynamics. Exactly the violation the position describes.

### On One Language Claiming Universal Authority

- **Wittgenstein** — Language-games are autonomous, each governed by its own rules. No meta-game adjudicates from above.
- **Winch** — You cannot judge Azande witchcraft by the standards of Western scientific rationality without committing a category error.
- **Lyotard** — "Incredulity toward metanarratives." Grand stories claiming to legitimate all knowledge should give way to local narratives, each with its own legitimacy.
- **Goodman** — No single correct description of reality. "Conflicting true versions correspond to different worlds."
- **Haraway** — The "god trick": the pretense of seeing from nowhere.
- **Descola** — Western "naturalism" (one nature, many cultures) is one of four ontological schemas, not the default.
- **Viveiros de Castro** — "Multinaturalism": all beings apprehend reality from distinct points of view. All beings are potential subjects.

### Philosophy and Epistemology

- **Kuhn** — Paradigms are incommensurable. Observation is theory-laden.
- **MacIntyre** — All rationality is tradition-constituted. But MacIntyre believed one tradition can prove rationally superior. The "equal standing in potential" claim does not deny this — it denies that any tradition starts disqualified. The difference is about starting conditions, not outcomes.
- **Berlin (Value Pluralism)** — Multiple genuine, irreducible values can conflict. This is not relativism because pluralism accepts constraints on what counts as a value.
- **Gadamer** — No framework can claim a view from nowhere. Understanding is always situated.

### Ecology and Environmental Philosophy

- **Naess (Deep Ecology)** — All living beings have equal inherent value. But makes ontological claims about intrinsic value; this position stays within epistemological reality.
- **Maturana & Varela** — Each organism brings forth its own meaningful world through structural coupling. Operational closure means each system generates its own standards.
- **Biosemiotics** — Meaning-making across all biological organisms. Every organism lives within its own meaningful world.

### Religious Studies

- **Hick** — Diverse religious traditions are various finite responses to a single transcendent reality. All are "on equal footing." But Hick posits an underlying Real; this position does not require that commitment.
- **Kimmerer** — "Indigenous knowledge and Western science are both powerful ways of knowing." Neither should be subordinated to the other.

### Where This Position Diverges from Mainstream

| Mainstream tendency | This position |
|---|---|
| Animal ethics (Singer, Regan) extends human moral categories to animals | This is itself the violation — one framework imposing its standards on another |
| Western philosophy assumes some frameworks are inherently superior (MacIntyre, Habermas) | Equal standing in potential — no framework is disqualified by nature, though they may differ in development |
| Postcolonial theory limits the critique to human power relations | Generalises the structure to all relational systems, including non-human |
| Deep ecology makes ontological claims about intrinsic value | Stays within epistemological reality — more modest and more consistent |
| Science studies (Kuhn, Feyerabend, Latour) stay within human knowledge systems | Extends to all relational systems |
