# Research Brief — Philosophical Positioning of the Seed

## Purpose

Establish solid philosophical coordinates for the seed principle before
expanding into other domains (semiotics, biology, consciousness,
engineering). The aim is confident positioning, not comprehensive
coverage. We need to know who's close, who'd challenge us, and where
the seed genuinely says something that isn't already being said.

## The seed (for reference)

1. Language is relational.
2. A subject experiences reality through language.
3. Subjects share knowledge — convergence is the only objectivity known.
4. Languages are inter-relational with equal standing.
5. Together they form a web of growing complexity.

## Four research threads

### 1. Rorty and the axis

We propose a Wittgenstein-Rorty axis as the philosophical backbone.
Wittgenstein unlocked the door between analytical and continental
traditions. Rorty walked through it deliberately.

Research questions:
- How directly does Rorty draw on Wittgenstein versus Heidegger versus
  Dewey? What's the balance?
- Where does his anti-foundationalism (Philosophy and the Mirror of
  Nature, 1979) specifically align with the seed's "equal standing"?
- Where does it diverge? Rorty is often read as relativist — the seed
  is not (equal standing is structural, not "anything goes").
- What is his concept of "conversation" and how does it map onto the
  seed's "subjects share knowledge"?
- How is Rorty received? Strengths and weaknesses in his position that
  we should be aware of.

### 2. The trajectory verified

We proposed a path from Wittgenstein to the seed:
words → use → activity → body → relation

Specifically:
- Wittgenstein: language → language game (meaning is use)
- Wittgenstein: language game → form of life (use woven into activity)
- Merleau-Ponty: form of life → the body (pre-reflective, embodied)
- The seed: → relation (what a language gives access to depends on
  what it relates to)

Research questions:
- Is this trajectory recognised in scholarship, or is it our
  construction?
- Has anyone explicitly traced this path?
- Charles Taylor's *The Language Animal* (2016) brings Wittgenstein and
  Merleau-Ponty together — does his reading support or differ from ours?
- Are there established critiques of this trajectory we should know about?

### 3. Philosophical neighbourhood — relational ontologies

The seed isn't the only relational position. We need to map the
neighbourhood.

Research questions:
- **Carlo Rovelli** — Relational Quantum Mechanics. Reality as
  relational, no observer-independent properties. How close is this to
  "language is relational"? Where does it diverge (physics vs language)?
- **Karen Barad** — agential realism, intra-action. Entities don't
  pre-exist their relations. Overlap with the seed?
- **Process philosophy (Whitehead)** — reality as process, not substance.
  Relations as primary. How does this map?
- **Enactivism (Varela/Thompson)** — cognition as enacted through
  engagement. Closest to the seed in cognitive science. Where exactly
  does it align and where does it stop short?
- **Deleuze** — relational ontology, rhizome, difference. Parallel
  territory?
- Are there other relational positions we're missing?
- Where does the seed add something none of these already say?

### 4. Peirce — semiotics and the seed

Peirce sits between the analytical and continental branches. His
semiotics — signs relating to signs, meaning as triadic (sign, object,
interpretant) — is arguably the closest predecessor to "language is
relational."

Research questions:
- How close is Peirce's semiotics to the seed's first statement?
- His triadic model versus our relational model — same insight or
  different structure?
- Peirce's pragmatism and fallibilism — how does this connect to
  "convergence is the only objectivity known"?
- His concept of community of inquiry — relation to statement 3?
- Where does the seed go beyond Peirce, if it does?

## Framing note — the private as relational horizon

The seed's position on private experience (statement 2) is not that
the private is sealed off. It's that reaching it requires building
relational language that can access it.

Analogy: the internal bus of a computer carries signals that are
meaningless if tapped from outside — not because they're encrypted
but because understanding them requires a relational language pattern
that maps input to the signal's meaning. Build that language, and the
signal becomes accessible.

The private isn't a wall. It's a horizon that moves as relational
reach grows. The "explanatory gap" (Chalmers) is a gap in relational
reach, not a gap in reality. This dissolves the hard problem rather
than solving it: there is no sealed inner realm, only relations that
haven't been extended yet.

This is not part of the current research but sharpens our position
before we encounter challenges on the subject/consciousness front.

## What we're NOT researching here

- Consciousness / hard problem (Chalmers, Nagel) — expansion territory,
  not positioning
- Semiotics as a field — future expansion, Peirce here only as
  philosophical predecessor
- Biology / evolution — future expansion
- Engineering / software — separate domain

## Output expected

For each thread: key findings, where the seed aligns, where it diverges,
and any exposure (positions that would challenge us). Not comprehensive
surveys — targeted answers to the research questions.
