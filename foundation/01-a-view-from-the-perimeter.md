# A View from the Perimeter

A commentary on the principle, made from outside looking in. A historical look with hindsight, not always PC.

---

## The starting intuition

The basic structural principles of reality should apply everywhere. There are too many discontinuities across domains — non-living, living, human, abstract thought — treated as if each operates on fundamentally different laws. Physics has its principles, biology has its own, human culture has its own, abstract thought has its own. The boundaries between non-living and living, between living and conscious, between concrete and abstract — treated as fundamental breaks.

They shouldn't be. Not that everything is the same — but the structural principles should be continuous. Different at every level, yes. But recognisably the same underneath.

---

## What the current state of knowledge shows

Looking at where knowledge stands, certain things are becoming clear:

**Complexity is everywhere.** Not confined to "complex systems" as a special category. At every level — quantum interactions, chemical bonding, cellular processes, ecosystems, social life — complexity is the norm. Simple, isolated systems are the abstraction; entangled, interacting systems are the reality.

**Adaptive systems are everywhere.** Systems that change through interaction, that adjust to what they relate to. Not just biological evolution — languages adapt, cultures adapt, scientific paradigms adapt, physical systems find equilibrium through interaction. Adaptation is not a special feature of life; it is a consequence of interaction.

**Determinism in facts is a myth, but determinism in ways of being is reality.** There are no fixed, absolute facts — no content that holds regardless of perspective or context. But the way things work — the structural patterns — is consistent. The facts vary. The structure doesn't.

---

## The reasoning, in hindsight

If there are laws that apply everywhere, then there is a common layer. And the common layer for everything that exists is that it is noticeable to other things that exist — interactions between entities. Everything that exists, exists through interaction with something else.

By recognising language as the interaction medium present everywhere — not just human speech, but every form of relational interaction — there is a layer common to all that exists. Language universalised: from physics to biology to human culture to abstract thought, the medium is interaction, and interaction is what we call language.

Then it is about the properties that can be assigned to this layer. What can we say about language, understood this way? That is what the principle addresses.

---

## Why now

The case for a common relational layer could always be made philosophically. What has changed is that multiple scientific domains are arriving at the same structural observation independently:

**Relational quantum mechanics** — properties of physical systems exist only relative to other physical systems. No observer-independent reality. The most elementary level of physical description is moving toward relational.

**Neuroscience and embodied cognition** — meaning is grounded in the body's sensorimotor interaction with the world. Abstract thought is embodied patterns decoupled from physical execution. Language and cognition are not separate.

**Biology and biosemiotics** — semiosis is a fundamental property of all living systems. Every living cell engages in sign processes. The boundary of language is not the boundary of the human.

**Artificial intelligence and neural networks** — meaning in a neural network is entirely in the relations between nodes, not in any individual node. The relational structure is what learns, what adapts, what produces.

The convergence is no longer just philosophical. The sciences are arriving there from within their own domains, driven by their own evidence. The time is ripe for a supported case.

---

## Time as interaction

Time as we tell ourselves it is — uniform, external, ticking regardless of what happens — is an illusion. It would be more appropriate to define time as a unit of interaction. Our perception of time has more to do with what we are engaged in than with the clock. Deeply engaged, time flies. Disengaged, it drags. The experience of time is relational.

Evolution — of life, of civilisation, of knowledge — seems tied to the intensity of interactions present. More interactions, more change, more development. And in human history, the intensity has been increasing exponentially. Agriculture brought sustained interaction between settled communities. Writing freed interaction from presence, persisting across time. Printing multiplied it. Electronic exchange made it instantaneous and global. Each step multiplied the density of interaction, and each one accelerated everything that followed — because more interactions produce more interactions.

The exponential is built into the relational itself.

There is an information side to this. Shannon's information — difference against a background — grows with each new medium. Writing stores differences. Printing distributes them. Electronic exchange makes them instantaneous. The capacity for information grows with the capacity for interaction — they are the same thing measured differently. Each step in the exponential doesn't just speed up what was already happening; it changes the kind of interaction that is possible, which changes what can be thought, which changes what can be built, which changes the kind of interaction again.

---

## Transitions

The discontinuities that started this inquiry — non-living, living, human, abstract — may not be breaks but transitions. Points where interaction density crosses a threshold and something structurally new emerges:

**Decoherence** — quantum interactions accumulating to the point where properties become effectively stable. The transition from quantum-fragile to classical. Interaction density producing robustness. The most elementary transition.

**Life** — self-maintaining systems that sustain their own interaction density. The system produces the conditions for its own continuation. A new kind of persistence through interaction.

**Abstract thought** — patterns decoupled from physical execution. Interaction freed from the body that produced it. The same patterns running without their physical context, generating new patterns.

**Symbolic exchange** — writing, printing, electronic. Interaction freed from presence, from time, from distance. Each step another transition.

Each transition produces something qualitatively new — not predicted from within the level below, but recognisable in hindsight as more of the same structural pattern. Not designed, not planned — emerging from the accumulation of interactions. The transitions are thresholds in relational density.

The singularity question — will there be a moment when the curve goes vertical and everything transforms? — may be the wrong framing. Abstract thought as we know it is probably already such a broken barrier. AI may be another. The question is not whether there will be a singularity but that there have been several, and each one was a threshold in relational density that produced a new level of interaction unimaginable from the level before.

---

## The resistance

There is a pattern in how the relational is received: resistance. Wherever it is proposed — in quantum mechanics, in philosophy of language, in biology — the same pushback appears. The expectation of observer-independent reality, of facts that hold regardless of who is looking, of a view from nowhere. The resistance to relational QM and the resistance to "language is relational" may be the same resistance, expressed in different domains.

This is worth noticing from the perimeter. The resistance is not random. It protects something specific: the idea that reality is fixed, given, independent of interaction. Every domain has its version of this idea, and every domain resists when the relational is proposed as more fundamental. The resistance itself is evidence that something structural is being touched.

---

## Precursors

The relational observation is not new. Pieces of it appear long before the scientific support existed:

**Heraclitus** (c. 500 BCE) — logos as simultaneously language, reason, and the structure of reality. All things come into being through opposition. The whole pattern, twenty-five centuries before the science caught up.

**Leibniz** (1646-1716) — language as an instrument of reason, not a vehicle for pre-existing thoughts. Monads reflecting the whole from individual perspectives. The seed of both the linguistic turn and the formal tradition.

**Whitehead** (1861-1947) — process philosophy. The world as a web of interrelated events, not a collection of substances with fixed properties. Reality as process, not thing.

None of them had the scientific support to make the case fully. The intuition was there; the convergence from independent domains was not. Now it is.

---

## The name

Splectrum: spectrum and plectrum. The spectrum — the full range of ways reality manifests through interaction, from a single quantum event to the dense polyphony of social life. The plectrum — the agent of interaction, the thing that plucks. Nothing sounds until it is plucked. The string has potential, the plectrum has intent, but the music exists only in the meeting. Neither alone produces anything.

The plectrum image makes viscerally clear that interaction is not passive observation. It is contact. Something touches something else and both are changed by it. The string vibrates, but the plectrum is deflected too. There is no plucking without being plucked back.

And it quietly encodes the epistemological framing. A plectrum doesn't reveal what a string "really is." It produces a note — one of many possible notes, depending on where and how you pluck. Different plucking, different music. But it is real music, not illusion. Reality in context, not reality denied.

---

## What we don't know

Honest from the perimeter: there is much we don't know and may not be able to know from where we stand.

We don't know whether the relational pattern holds everywhere or only where we've looked. The convergence across domains is striking, but striking is not proof. There may be domains where the pattern breaks, and those would be as informative as those where it holds.

We don't know what lies beyond the transitions we can see. If each threshold produces something unimaginable from the level below, the next threshold is by definition beyond our imagination. We can see the pattern of thresholds in hindsight; we cannot predict the next one.

We don't know whether the principle captures the fabric or only one way of weaving. The recursive awareness — the principle is itself a language, with its own reach and limits — means we cannot step outside it to check. We are inside what we describe. This is not a weakness unique to us; it is the condition of all description. But it means our claims are always from within, always partial, always open to what we haven't yet encountered.

The perimeter is not a wall. It is the edge of where we've looked so far.

---

## The spirit

The language game we are building is about giving a natural home to these phenomena — without hair-pulling, without lecturing, without claiming to be always right. If the principle is right, the phenomena should sit in it naturally, because the relational is already what they are. If it's not right for some of them, that is informative too.

The aim is fertile ground: a language that makes it easier to see connections across domains without claiming to own any of them. A place where the pattern can be recognised without distorting what it's found in.

---

## What follows

From this perimeter view, the principle — when it crystallised — made sense of what was already becoming visible across domains. It didn't invent the relational; it named a structural pattern that was emerging independently in multiple fields.

The principle itself stands on its own terms. It doesn't need this origin story to justify it. But the origin explains the motivation, and the timing explains why the case can be made now in a way it couldn't before.

What the principle develops into — its identity, its working methods, its ambitions — grows from the seed itself, not from the perimeter. That is a separate story.
