# Hilbert — Map

## Initial phrases

"Mathematics is a formal game played with symbols according to rules."

Mathematical language does not refer to anything outside itself. Meaning is constituted by the rules of the formal system.

Language is pure syntax — the symbols and the rules are all there is.

Hilbert's programme: to establish mathematics on a foundation of complete consistency — every true statement provable, no contradictions, the system self-sufficient.

---

## Mapping statement

Hilbert defines language at its purest: symbols played according to rules, meaning constituted by the rules themselves. Meaning is in the relations between symbols, not in what the symbols point to. He applies this to mathematics as pure syntax — and the definition itself is broader than his application. Symbols, rules, meaning through structure: this is a relational description of language.

His programme aimed for self-sufficiency — a formal system that is complete and consistent, needing nothing outside itself. This is the strongest possible claim for a single language standing on its own. Godel proved it cannot work: any consistent formal system powerful enough contains truths it cannot reach. Hilbert defines the aspiration; Godel proves the structural impossibility. Together they make the case for inter-relational from opposite sides — one by attempting the self-contained language, the other by proving that no language can be self-contained.

When we talk about one language, we often bring in more than belongs to that language alone — what feels richer may be the inter-relational at work. Mathematics as pure syntax is one language; the richness we sense around it — the intuitions, the visualisations, the sense of meaning beyond the formalism — may come from other languages overlapping with it. Hilbert tried to seal mathematics off from that enrichment. Godel showed the seal cannot hold.

For us, Hilbert sits alongside Turing and Shannon as the formal/minimal group — each stripping language to bare bones, each finding relational structure at the bottom. Hilbert strips to syntax, Turing to transformation, Shannon to difference. Together they describe the minimum, and the minimum is relational.
