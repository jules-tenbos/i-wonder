# Heraclitus — Map

## Initial phrases

"Logos is not merely human speech but is identical with the rational structure of the cosmos itself."

"Although the logos is common, most people live as though they had their own private understanding." (Fr. 2)

"Listening not to me but to the logos, it is wise to agree that all things are one." (Fr. 50)

"All things come into being through opposition, and all are in flux."

---

## Mapping statement

Heraclitus's logos is simultaneously language, reason, and the structure of reality — not three things but one. It is common, shared, the same for all, though most people live as if their understanding were private. All things come into being through opposition — reality created through differences.

His logos is richer and more multivalent than any single reading can capture — it has been read as cosmic law, as reason, as account, as discourse. We read it from within our thinking and see what resonates.

From where we stand, we recognise: the logos as common and shared, reality coming into being through opposition and differences, and the inseparability of language, reason, and the structure of reality. These are visible in his fragments in his own terms. We do not claim to read his fragments definitively — they have supported many readings for millennia. What we note is that from where we stand, what resonates is substantial, and that it comes from a tradition entirely independent of ours.
