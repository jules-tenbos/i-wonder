# Embodied Cognition (Barsalou, Glenberg, Pulvermuller) — Map

## Initial phrases

"Language comprehension involves the reactivation of sensorimotor neural circuits."

Meaning is grounded in modal simulations — perceptual, motor, affective.

Language is deeply integrated with the body's sensorimotor systems.

---

## Mapping statement

Embodied cognition research shows that language comprehension reactivates sensorimotor neural circuits. Words and concepts are associated with patterns that activate on recognition — "hit" activates motor patterns whether you hit or not. Meaning is grounded in the body's interaction with the world, not in an abstract symbol system detached from experience.

The productive observation is decoupling: activation without execution. Sensory and action patterns run without their physical context. This is the mechanism by which embodied experience becomes abstract thought. Abstract thought is not separate from the embodied — it is the same patterns, decoupled from physical execution.

From where we stand, decoupling as a pattern is recognisable in other domains. The programming language hierarchy: each level of abstraction frees the programmer from the specifics of the level below, enabling thinking at a higher level. Lakoff's conceptual metaphors: bodily experience (piling, moving, containing) applied to abstract domains without physical execution. These are analogies, not equivalences — the neuroscience of sensorimotor reactivation, the engineering of abstraction layers, and the cognitive linguistics of metaphor are different fields describing different processes. But the pattern — structures freed from their original context and applied elsewhere — is recognisable across them.

From where we stand, decoupling is how languages extend their reach. A pattern that worked in one context, freed from that context, becomes available for use in another. This is inter-relational at work within a single language — and it is also how new languages emerge from existing ones.
