# Complexity

Principle statement: **Languages form a web of growing complexity**

6 positions pre-categorised under this concept.

---

1. Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz | Language is not merely a vehicle for communicating pre-existing thoughts but an instrument of reason itself; languages embody the collective intellectual labor of peoples and contain more wisdom than any individual speaker possesses.

2. Charles Sanders Peirce | All thought is in signs; a sign stands for something (its object) to someone (the interpretant) in some respect; meaning is an ongoing process of semiosis -- each interpretant is itself a sign, creating an infinite chain; language is irreducibly social, processual, and triadic.

3. Paul Ricoeur | The fundamental unit of meaning is the sentence, not the word; a living metaphor is a semantic innovation that creates new meaning by bringing together previously unrelated domains; narrative is the linguistic form through which human temporal experience becomes intelligible.

4. John Dewey | Language is the "tool of tools" -- the instrument that makes all other instruments possible; it is fundamentally social, arising from cooperative activity; language transforms biological impulses into intelligent, reflective inquiry.

5. Bhartrhari | Brahman (ultimate reality) is essentially linguistic (Shabda-Brahman); all cognition is permeated by language; the sentence, not the word, is the primary unit of meaning, grasped in a flash of intuition (pratibha); speech has three levels: Pashyanti (visionary/undifferentiated), Madhyama (mental/internal), and Vaikhari (external/articulated).

6. Jain Tradition (Kundakunda, Siddhasena, et al.) | Reality is infinitely complex (anekanta); language can only capture it partially, from one perspective (naya) at a time; the syadvada (sevenfold predication) acknowledges that any statement is conditionally true and that there is an aspect of reality (avaktavya) that language cannot capture at all.
