# William James — Map

## Initial phrases

"The meaning of a concept consists in its practical consequences — what difference it would make to experience."

Language is instrumental: ideas and words are tools for navigating experience, not mirrors of reality.

## Radical empiricism

"The relations between things, conjunctive as well as disjunctive, are just as much matters of direct particular experience, neither more so nor less so, than the things themselves."

"To be radical, an empiricism must neither admit into its constructions any element that is not directly experienced, nor exclude from them any element that is directly experienced."

## Pure experience

"If we start with the supposition that there is only one primal stuff or material in the world, a stuff of which everything is composed, and if we call that stuff 'pure experience,' the knowing can easily be explained as a particular sort of relation towards one another into which portions of pure experience may enter."

Subject and object are not separate things but different roles within the same experience.

## Stream of consciousness

Thought flows — not chopped into bits. "Substantive parts" (stable images) and "transitive parts" (feelings of relation, direction, tendency). Language systematically under-describes the transitive:

"We ought to say a feeling of and, a feeling of if, a feeling of but, and a feeling of by, quite as readily as we say a feeling of blue or a feeling of cold."

## Language and relations

"Philosophy has always turned on grammatical particles. With, near, next, like, from, towards, against, because, for, through, my — these words designate types of conjunctive relation."

Prepositions and conjunctions — the most relational part of language — as evidence that relations are experienced directly.

## On abstraction

"Vicious abstractionism": reducing rich phenomena to bare concept-labels. Concepts should guide into richer experience, not close inquiry.

---

## Mapping statement

James's radical empiricism insists that relations are directly experienced — as real as the things they relate. From where we stand, this speaks to the core of what we think: the relational is not an abstraction layered on top of experience, it is experience. His own terms make the case.

His pure experience — one primal stuff, with subject and object as different roles within it — describes reality constituted through relations with no clean separation between knower and known.

His stream of consciousness — substantive parts and transitive parts, with language systematically under-describing the transitive — identifies something specific: the relational dimension of language (the "and," "if," "but," "by") is as real as its substantive content, though harder to name. Language has a structural bias toward the substantive; the relational is there but under-expressed.

His warning against vicious abstractionism — reducing the rich to the bare concept — is relevant to how we work: reasoning within a reduced scope while noting broader reach when we see it.

James's radical empiricism, his pure experience, and his attention to the transitive all carry the same structural observation in his own terms.
