# Derrida — Map

## Initial phrases

"There is no outside-text" (il n'y a pas de hors-texte) — there is no access to meaning that bypasses language.

Differance — meaning is never fully present. It is constituted by differences and perpetually deferred.

"There is no transcendental signified" — no ultimate meaning outside the play of language that anchors it.

The properties traditionally attributed to writing — absence, iterability, displacement — belong to all language.

---

## Mapping statement

Derrida's differance describes meaning as constituted by differences — like Saussure — but adds that meaning is perpetually deferred, never fully present, always in play. Where Saussure describes the system of differences, Derrida describes the movement: meaning is never settled, never arrives at a fixed point. It is relational and in motion.

His "no outside-text" — there is no access to meaning that bypasses language — parallels how we think: each language's reach is only accessible through that language. There is no stepping outside to check meaning against something beyond language.

His "no transcendental signified" — no ultimate meaning anchoring the play from outside. There is no fixed point outside the play of language that could stop it. From where we stand, we recognise a parallel: there is no view from nowhere, no fixed point outside the relational. His way of stating this and ours differ — his dismantles the desire for the anchor, ours observes the structural condition that makes anchoring impossible.

His iterability — any sign can be repeated in a new context, and repetition changes it — describes expression freed from its original context and gaining new relational life. This is the same structural observation Ricoeur makes with distanciation and Dewey makes with meanings becoming independent of their original objects. Three traditions noting the same thing: once expressed, meaning is no longer bound to its origin.

Derrida's project is deconstructive — his concern is to show that all claims to fixed meaning, presence, and foundation are undermined by the play of differance. Our project is constructive — we claim a structural pattern across languages. His work is a reminder that the structural claim we make is itself within language, within the play, not above it.
