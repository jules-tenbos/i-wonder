# Shannon — Map

## Initial phrases

"The fundamental problem of communication is that of reproducing at one point a message selected at another point."

Information is measured by surprise — the less expected a message, the more information it carries.

Language can be modelled as a stochastic process transmitting information through a noisy channel.

"The semantic aspects of communication are irrelevant to the engineering problem."

---

## Mapping statement

Shannon strips communication to bare bones: a message transmitted from one point to another through a noisy channel, deliberately agnostic about meaning. He removes semantics entirely — and what remains is still relational structure. Information is the difference of a message against an expected background. No background, no difference, no information. The relational is prior to the semantic.

This is the thinnest possible slice, and it is still relational. That is the strength. Like Godel's narrowness, Shannon's minimalism makes the structural point harder to dismiss — if the relational is there at bare bones, it is deeply structural.

His information-as-difference formalises what Bateson later generalised: "a difference which makes a difference." Shannon defines it for engineered communication. Bateson extends it across all levels of physical organisation — human, animal, ecosystem. The same relational core, at different scopes.

Rovelli draws explicitly on Shannon's information theory in building relational quantum mechanics. In RQM, an interaction between two physical systems is a physical event in which one system's state becomes correlated with the other's — and quantum mechanics is fundamentally about the constraints on information that one system can have about another. Shannon's information-as-difference becomes, in Rovelli's hands, information as physical correlation between systems. Properties exist only relative to another system, and information is what connects them.

Shannon → Bateson → Rovelli: engineered communication, ecology and cybernetics, quantum physics. Three domains, each arriving at information as relational, building from the minimum. Shannon provides the formal foundation that the others extend.

For us, Shannon's transmission sits alongside Turing's transformation — both describe language in action, stripped to its relational machinery. Turing shows that the substance of language is the relationships and transformations, not the symbols. Shannon shows that the substance of information is difference and correlation, not content. Together they describe the bare bones of the relational.
