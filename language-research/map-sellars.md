# Sellars — Map

## Initial phrases

"Language is the medium of the 'space of reasons' — the normative domain of justification and inference."

The "myth of the given" is rejected: all awareness is a linguistic affair.

"To be a sentient being in the epistemic sense is to be a player in the language game of giving and asking for reasons."

---

## Mapping statement

Sellars rejects the myth of the given: the idea that there is a raw, pre-linguistic contact with reality that provides a foundation for knowledge. There is no such contact. All awareness is a linguistic affair — we do not first experience and then describe; experience is already within language. From where we stand, this speaks directly to how we think: language is the medium through which a subject experiences reality, and each language's reach is only accessible through that language.

His space of reasons describes how knowledge works: giving and asking for reasons. Knowledge is not a private event — it is constituted through the exchange of reasons between subjects. To know something is to be able to place it within the normative game of justification. This is a specific mechanism for what we think about as sharing: knowledge is what survives the overlap of experience, and the space of reasons is where that overlap happens.

What Sellars describes in his own terms is the connection of experience and knowledge through language in a single move. There is no experience outside language (myth of the given rejected), and knowledge is constituted through linguistic exchange (space of reasons). Language is simultaneously the medium of experience and the medium of knowledge — which is what our principle's first three lines say from within our framework.

His scope is human epistemic practice — the space of reasons is specifically about propositional knowledge, justification, and inference among rational agents. Our ambition extends beyond the propositional and beyond the human. His "linguistic" is narrower than ours. But within his scope, the structural observation holds: no experience without language, no knowledge without sharing.
