# Cassirer — Map

## Initial phrases

"Man is animal symbolicum" — the animal that lives through symbols.

Language is one of several "symbolic forms" — alongside myth, art, science, religion. Each is a way of constituting the world, not representing it.

Humans don't encounter raw reality; they encounter it through symbolic forms. Each form shapes experience into an intelligible world in its own way.

---

## Mapping statement

Cassirer's symbolic forms — myth, art, science, religion — each constitute the world rather than represent it. Each creates its own domain of meaning, its own way of experiencing. The constitutive insight — language shapes what it gives access to, rather than mirroring something already there — overlaps with how we think.

His multiple symbolic forms, each with its own way of constituting experience, open naturally toward treating different domains as languages in their own right. Myth constitutes one world, science another, art another — each valid in its own terms. This is close to how we think about language types and equal standing.

Where we differ: Cassirer's scope is the human — his "animal symbolicum" places symbolic activity as the defining characteristic of human life. Our scope extends beyond the human to all language types. His framework also allows for something behind the symbolic forms — a reality that the forms shape but do not create entirely. Our framework sees reality as accessible only through a language, with nothing behind it that can be reached independently. Two scopes, two positions on what lies beyond the forms.
