# Inter-relational

Principle statement: **Relations between languages**

9 positions pre-categorised under this concept.

---

1. Thomas Aquinas | Words signify things through the mediation of concepts; crucially, certain terms can be predicated of radically different domains analogically -- with a meaning that is proportionally related rather than univocal or equivocal.

2. Ludwig Wittgenstein (later) | Language is a collection of "language-games" embedded in "forms of life"; the meaning of a word is its use; there is no single essence of language, only family resemblances; a private language is incoherent.

3. W.V.O. Quine | There is no sharp boundary between analytic and synthetic truths; meaning is not a determinate property of individual sentences (holism); translation between languages is radically indeterminate; language is a social art acquired on publicly available behavioral evidence.

4. Paul Ricoeur | The fundamental unit of meaning is the sentence, not the word; a living metaphor is a semantic innovation that creates new meaning by bringing together previously unrelated domains; narrative is the linguistic form through which human temporal experience becomes intelligible.

5. Jain Tradition (Kundakunda, Siddhasena, et al.) | Reality is infinitely complex (anekanta); language can only capture it partially, from one perspective (naya) at a time; the syadvada (sevenfold predication) acknowledges that any statement is conditionally true and that there is an aspect of reality (avaktavya) that language cannot capture at all.

6. Alan Turing | A language, at its most reduced, is a set of symbols plus rules for their transformation; a sufficiently powerful symbol system can simulate any other (universality); language is not fundamentally about specific symbols but about the structural relationships and transformation rules those symbols participate in.

7. Alfred Tarski | Defining truth for a language requires a metalanguage; no sufficiently powerful language can fully describe its own semantics within itself; language is inherently stratified, hierarchical, and self-referential but with limits.

8. Kurt Godel | Any consistent formal system powerful enough to express basic arithmetic contains true statements that cannot be proved within it; meaning exceeds any formalization of it; no single formal language can be both consistent and complete for a sufficiently rich domain.

9. Adorno | Musical materials carry historical and social sediment; tonality is not a neutral system but an ideology; language (including musical language) is never innocent of its social context; a form can "say" something about reality by enacting or refusing structural possibilities.
