# Peirce — Map

## Initial phrases

"All thought is in signs."

A sign stands for something (its object) to someone (the interpretant) in some respect. Meaning is irreducibly triadic.

Semiosis is an ongoing process — each interpretant is itself a sign, creating an infinite chain.

Language is irreducibly social, processual, and triadic.

---

## Mapping statement

Peirce's sign is irreducibly triadic: sign, object, interpretant. You can't remove any term. The interpretant is always there — and is itself a sign, generating further signs. The process never terminates from within.

His infinite chain of semiosis — each interpretant becoming a sign — is a consequence of the triadic structure. Signs generating more signs, the chain extending by its own logic. This connects to complexity: the chain extends, each sign-event creating the conditions for the next.

For us, the triad overlaps with how we think about experience: the subject in "language is the medium through which a subject experiences reality" is analogous to Peirce's interpretant — always part of the sign process, never detached from it. Peirce is a realist who believes in mind-independent reality and the long-run convergence of inquiry toward truth; our analogy is with the structural position of the interpretant within the process, not with his broader epistemology.

His triadic sign is also a structural contrast with Saussure's dyadic sign (signifier/signified). Saussure describes the system of differences within a language. Peirce adds the interpretant — the one for whom the sign operates — and makes the process ongoing. Both are relational; Peirce's version builds in the subject and the process. Two descriptions of the relational, each capturing a different aspect.
