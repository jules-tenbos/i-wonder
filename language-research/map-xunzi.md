# Xunzi — Map

## Initial phrases

"Names are conventional (yue), established by agreement, not by nature."

But once conventions are established, there are objective standards for correct use.

The purpose of names is to distinguish similarities and differences.

"When names are correct, things are in order." Correct naming leads to correct governance — his concern is social order through language.

---

## Mapping statement

Xunzi sees names as conventional — established by agreement, not by nature. But once established, they are binding: there are standards for correct use. The convention is contingent (could have been different) but once created, it constrains. This connects to how we think about language as "committed ways of expressing relation" — a language, once established, has its own logic, its own reach.

His objective standards are not absolute — they are what the community converges on. We read this as our objectivity: convergence of subjects. Knowledge is what survives the overlap of experience. Xunzi describes the same mechanism: agreement creates the convention, the convention creates the standard, the standard is held in common.

The purpose of names is to distinguish similarities and differences — the basic relational operation. This is the same structural observation as Saussure (meaning arises from differences within a system), Shannon (information as difference against a background), and Bateson (a difference which makes a difference). Xunzi arrives there from 3rd century BCE Chinese philosophy. Four thinkers, four traditions, four centuries apart, converging on distinguishing as what language fundamentally does.

Making differences is also the essence of measuring in the exact sciences: distinguishing, comparing, establishing where one thing ends and another begins. A measurement is a name given to a distinction — "this is 3.2 meters" distinguishes this length from all others by agreement on a convention (the meter). The exact sciences are systematic distinguishing by convention — Xunzi's description of language, formalised.
