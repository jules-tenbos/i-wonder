# Hoffmeyer — Map

## Initial phrases

"Semiosis is a fundamental property of all living systems, not limited to human language."

Every living cell engages in sign processes. Human language is a species-specific elaboration of a universal biological semiosis.

Life and semiosis are coextensive — where there is life, there is sign activity.

---

## Mapping statement

Hoffmeyer's central claim: semiosis is a fundamental property of all living systems. Every living cell engages in sign processes. Human language is one elaboration among many, not the standard against which others are measured. This removes the human from the centre and places all living sign activity on the same footing — each a valid expression of semiosis within its own form of life.

His "life and semiosis are coextensive" is a structural claim: wherever there is life, there is sign activity. The boundary of semiosis is the boundary of life. This extends Peirce's semiosis (signs generating signs in an ongoing process) from the conceptual to the biological — sign activity is not a human invention but a property of living systems as such.

From where we stand, this overlaps with how we think about language types and equal standing in potential. Hoffmeyer's biosemiotics points in the same direction as our ambition: that language extends beyond the human, and that each language stands in its own right. His work provides concrete cases — cellular signalling, immune recognition, plant communication — where sign processes operate without human language.

Hoffmeyer's scope is living systems. Ours extends to boundary/physical processes — where sign activity may not require life. Two scopes. Within his, the overlap is direct, and his concrete cases give substance to what we claim structurally. Bateson's communication "at every level of physical organisation" points in the same direction but from cybernetics rather than biology. Together they support the extension claim from two independent disciplines.
