# Bateson — Map

## Initial phrases

"Information is a difference which makes a difference."

Communication occurs at every level of physical organisation — not just human, not just animal, but ecosystems, societies, any system with sufficient complexity.

"Mind" is an immanent feature of any system with sufficient complexity of feedback and self-correction.

"The map is not the territory."

"The major problems in the world are the result of the difference between how nature works and the way people think."

---

## Mapping statement

Bateson's "a difference which makes a difference" is relational at its core — information is not a thing but a relation. His communication occurs at every level of physical organisation: human, animal, ecosystem, any system with sufficient complexity of feedback. Our ambition is that language spans the same range.

His approach is what draws us most: working across boundaries — anthropology, ecology, cybernetics, psychiatry — looking for the pattern which connects. This is close to how we think about inter-relational. He saw learning and evolution as parallel processes, which we want to explore further.

Bateson sits between Shannon and Rovelli in a convergence thread. Shannon formalised information as difference for engineered communication. Bateson generalised it: a difference which makes a difference, across all levels of physical organisation. Rovelli took information into physics: quantum mechanics as fundamentally about the constraints on information one system can have about another. Three domains — engineering, ecology/cybernetics, quantum physics — each arriving at information as relational, each building from the same structural core. Bateson is the bridge: he took Shannon's formal definition and showed it working everywhere.

His "the map is not the territory" is relevant to how we think. Every language is a map — a relational structure that gives access to some territory. The map is not the territory, but there is no access to the territory except through maps. Different maps, different access, different reach.
