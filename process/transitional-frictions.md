# Transitional frictions

An internal note about a specific kind of friction in the current phase of SPLectrum work. Worth re-reading when something on the site feels off in a way that's hard to name — it may be this.

## The current phase

SPLectrum is embryonic. Personas are defined as concepts but are not yet operational. The architecture (a repo owns its data; personas are structured behaviours that transform that data) holds together, but the system isn't running it yet — we are. Every transition (a draft moving toward publication, a definition shifting, a new vocabulary term landing, a page reorganising) is currently hand-rolled.

## What that means in practice

- Page conventions are being worked out one page at a time.
- Definitions feel provisional because they often are — they're in motion until the surrounding work catches up.
- Bookkeeping (`scheduled-tasks.md`, status fields on posts, manual tracking of what's evolving where) is doing work that personas will eventually do natively.
- Judgements about "is this finished or still in flight" require human attention on each page.

## The reflex to watch for

Mainstream reference style says: hide the work, present the result, speak with finality. Authority comes from looking settled. Hedges bad, polish good.

This site is built against that. The unfolding *is* the content. Personas come into operation. Vocabulary matures. Posts carry status fields. The publishing cadence is buffered so the storyline can evolve. Trying to "tidy" pages into a finished-looking state quietly erases the evolutionary character that makes the site coherent with its own philosophy.

So: state firmly what is settled, mark openly what is still moving, and let the page show which is which.

## Why these frictions are temporary

The scaffolding ends when personas are operational. Once they are:

- A transition isn't something we describe or worry about; it's the persona running its behaviour on the data.
- "Evolution" stops being a stylistic concern and becomes literally what the system does.
- The site doesn't need to tell the reader it's evolutionary — the personas enact evolution, transparently, on visible data.
- Bookkeeping collapses: what currently lives in `scheduled-tasks.md` becomes data the public conscious persona reads.

What looks like a problem to solve in pages and conventions is actually friction we're absorbing while we build toward the system that absorbs it natively. The right move is usually to keep going, not to over-engineer page conventions to compensate.

## The deeper coherence

Stability through movement, not despite it. The "final" thing in this work is the structured way movement happens — transitions are the load-bearing architecture, not the snapshots between them. The current pages are temporary expressions of those transitions; the persona system will be their permanent home.
