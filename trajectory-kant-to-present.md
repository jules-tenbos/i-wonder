# The Trajectory — From Descartes to the Present

Working material for the bridge post "From the Turn to the Present" (April 6).

---

## Descartes (1641, Meditations)

Descartes is the hinge. He starts from the observer — "I think, therefore I am." The cogito puts the individual mind at the centre. He discovers the observer. But then he uses God as guarantor to rebuild the outside view. God wouldn't deceive us, so our clear and distinct ideas do correspond to reality. He also splits mind from body, freeing science to treat matter as pure mechanism — three centuries of spectacularly productive science follow from that move. So Descartes discovers the observer and immediately neutralises the discovery. The mind mirrors nature because God made it that way. The outside view is saved.

## Kant (1781, Critique of Pure Reason)

Kant works within Descartes' framework — the mind at the centre of knowledge — but removes the God guarantee. The trigger was Hume, who showed that even causality can't be proven from observation alone. Kant's response: causality isn't in reality, it's in us. Before Kant: the God's-eye view — seeing reality complete and objective. Kant says: there is no God's-eye view. Only the human perspective, structured by our categories (space, time, causality), limited by our apparatus. We see from somewhere, not from everywhere. But he keeps a safety net — the thing-in-itself, reality as it is independent of us, forever unknowable but definitely there. The outside view is cracked but not abandoned.

## Hegel (early 1800s)

Took Kant's categories and put them in motion. If we only ever see through our categories, Hegel asked: what if those categories themselves develop? Understanding isn't fixed structures — it develops historically, through contradiction and resolution. Consciousness and reality unfold together. The thing-in-itself dissolves — there's no hidden reality behind experience, because experience IS reality becoming aware of itself. Hegel socialised and historicised Kant's turn. The outside view isn't just cracked, it's moving.

## Husserl (early 1900s)

Rejected the grand systems of Hegel. His move: go back to experience itself. Phenomenology — examine what actually appears to consciousness, without theoretical baggage. "To the things themselves." But Husserl stayed dualist at heart — consciousness is always consciousness *of* something, but the two remain separated by what he called an abyss. He opened the door to the inside-out view but kept one foot in the Cartesian picture. The method was radical; the metaphysics was cautious.

## Heidegger (1927, Being and Time)

Husserl's student, but went where Husserl wouldn't. The question isn't "what does consciousness experience?" — it's "what does it mean to be?" We don't observe the world from a distance. We're thrown into it — Dasein, being-there. Understanding isn't contemplation, it's activity: using tools, caring, being with others. The hammer isn't an object you observe — it's something you use, and you only notice it as an object when it breaks. Language isn't a system for describing reality — it's "the house of being," the medium in which we exist. Heidegger closes the gap Husserl kept open. There is no outside position to stand on.

## Whitehead (1929, Process and Reality)

Comes from a completely different direction — mathematics and physics, not phenomenology. Trained as a mathematician, collaborated with Russell on Principia Mathematica. But arrived at a strikingly similar place: reality is not made of substances (fixed things with properties). It's made of processes — events, occasions of experience, relations. Every entity is what it is because of its relations to everything else. Nothing exists in isolation. An Anglo-American mathematician-philosopher, starting from science, arriving at process and relation — independent of Heidegger, independent of phenomenology. The turn wasn't only a continental affair.

## Wittgenstein

Callback. The analytical tradition's own turn. From the logical straightjacket of the Tractatus to meaning in use, language games, forms of life. The reader has the full story.

## Rorty

Callback. Drew on Wittgenstein from the analytical side and Heidegger and Dewey from outside it. Took the turn public. No mirror, no privileged vocabulary, conversation replaces epistemology. Breached the dam between traditions.

## Merleau-Ponty

Callback. Husserl's phenomenology taken further, like Heidegger but in a different direction. Put the body back in. Before the conversation, before the game, there is a body engaging the world.

## The pattern

Descartes found the observer and saved the outside view with God. Kant removed God and the crack opened. Hegel set it in motion. Husserl turned inward but kept the gap. Heidegger closed it. Whitehead arrived independently from science. Wittgenstein turned within the analytical tradition. Rorty opened the door between traditions. Merleau-Ponty grounded it in the body. Different starting points, different vocabularies, no coordination. Same structural move: the outside view giving way to the relational, the participatory, the embedded.
