## The mapping exercise (affinity-ring method)

The method the pluralism affinity page is built around. Run each pluralistic stream's own language into SPLectrum-speak and ask: does it come out coherent? Track two things per stream, without pre-sorting them onto a "face":

- **What the stream claims** (explicit, answerable, asserted).
- **What its language carries unclaimed** (enacted in the discourse, not posited).

Any register can implicitly say what it does not explicitly claim — this is the live phenomenon, and it is especially diagnostic for *pluralistic* streams, because their claim is the no-privilege / many claim, and they are the streams most exposed to making that claim from a privileged overview.

- **Rorty is the calibration corner:** sparse, static, claims little, works the concepts/ontology register, carries the relational unclaimed. Strong *because* he refrains from the terrain where the claim could break. Every other stream is placed by how far it sits from this corner.
- **Whitehead is the contrast case:** claims the dynamic, but lodges it inside the static concept-register (process built as ontological furniture — actual occasions, eternal objects), which incurs the external-engine debt (God valuating eternal objects to supply the aim). The divergence with SPLectrum is *locational*, not doctrinal: motion claimed in the register of rest needs an imported engine; SPLectrum claims it in the medium that already moves.

Read each stream honestly on its own terms first; the SPLectrum reading sits beside it, never scoring it against the seed as a checklist. The moment the exercise scores streams against the seed, it has become the overview it is meant to be diagnosing.
