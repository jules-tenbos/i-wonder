# Notes: ANT and SPLectrum

Captured from conversation. Companion to the substrate notes. Useful for the future Latour cycle and for whatever passes the current cycle makes through Latour (in the philosophy of organism reception section, in the close-affinity pluralism revision).

## The two-sided coin

ANT's "being is differentiation" and SPLectrum's P0 ("differentiation produces being and language together") are the same insight viewed from different angles. ANT names what being *is* (differentiation, making-a-difference). P0 names what differentiation *produces* (being and language together). Different emphases, same structural move.

An actant is something differentiated within a network; the way it is differentiated *is* its language. To say "actant" is to gesture at the differentiation; to say "language" is to gesture at the relation that the differentiation enables. The two are co-implicated, not competing primitives.

## The asymmetry

The two sides of the coin are not equally articulated.

ANT's foundational move is thin: differentiation, agency, networks of actants. Beyond that, ANT carries plenty of structure empirically — assemblages, mediators, immutable mobiles, translation, black-boxing — but these are mid-level theoretical moves, not foundational ontological commitments. The shape of any particular network is observed case by case.

SPLectrum's relational side packs structure into the foundation. P1 commits to substantive properties of relation: relational character, partial reach, contingent character. Plus P3's shared-reality channels, P4's equal standing among inter-relational languages, P5's accumulating history. The structure of relation is built in.

So ANT could be a method *within* SPLectrum's frame; SPLectrum could not be reduced to ANT. The structure SPLectrum brings isn't derivable from the being-is-differentiation side alone.

## The is / is-like register

A second axis of difference, alongside the foundational asymmetry.

ANT stays heavily on the "is" side. Actants *are* differentiated. Microbes *are* actors. The modern Constitution *is* described, declared, made explicit. The methodology produces empirical accounts of what is happening. The register is descriptive realism.

SPLectrum is heavily on the "is-like" side. Per its editorial discipline — "is-like not is" — languages don't fix being into categories; they invite comparison, hold the relational character of being, refuse declarative imposition. The register is comparative, invitational, dialogical.

This explains some of the texture difference between the two frameworks. Latour's writing has the punch of empirical specificity (here is what this microbe does, here is how this network forms). SPLectrum's principles have the openness of invitations (this is the shape of the relation; what would it be to read X as a language?).

The two registers are not in conflict. They sit on different sides of the work: descriptive when describing is the task, invitational when holding the relational structure open is the task.

## Compatibility: ANT as the view from the being side

ANT and SPLectrum are compatible — as views from different sides of one coin, articulated in registers suited to their respective sides.

ANT articulates what being *is* (declarative, descriptive, empirical). SPLectrum articulates the relational structure that being implies (invitational, comparative, foundational). The compatibility doesn't require either to give ground:

- ANT keeps its empirical method, its flat methodological commitment, its descriptive richness about non-living participation. Its work on actants of every kind is direct contribution to the widening to non-living.
- SPLectrum keeps its is-like discipline, its foundational structural principles, its invitational register. The relational structure stays open as something to be read into, not fixed declaratively.

Where ANT is most useful: empirical description of network participation, especially for non-living actants where ANT has done the most worked-out work. Where SPLectrum's apparatus is most useful: holding the relational structure as invitation rather than declaration, surfacing what is shared across diversity, articulating the conditions of fruitful relation.
