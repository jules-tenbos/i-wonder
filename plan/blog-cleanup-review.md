# Blog review — inconsistencies and inaccuracies

Cleanup pass across the 16 published posts in the sitemap. Scope per request: inconsistencies and inaccuracies only. Editorial / voice / register items are not included.

Findings are organised in three tiers:

1. **Material issues** — factual errors and a substantive cross-post inconsistency that affect the published record's accuracy.
2. **Mechanical cleanup** — typos and clear text-level errors.
3. **Soft flags** — items worth a second look but not clearly wrong; included so a verification pass has the full surface.

## 1. Material issues

### 1.1 Cross-post inconsistency — number of original seed principles

**Posts involved:** `SPLectrum is Born` (March 7) and `The Birth of P0 — Being Is Tension across the Interface` (May 10).

- `SPLectrum is Born` (March 7) lists the seed as **six** principles, P0 through P5, with P0 ("Being implies language") already in place.
- `The Birth of P0` (May 10) opens with: *"When SPLectrum was born the seed had only five principles about language and the relational… And so P0 was born."*

The two posts disagree on the historical fact of whether P0 was part of the seed at SPLectrum's birth or added later. As the published record currently stands, the March post shows P0 in the original seed; the May post claims it wasn't there. One of the two is now inaccurate — most likely the March post was retroactively updated to include P0, and the inconsistency lives on because the original text wasn't preserved or annotated. A reader following the timeline gets contradictory accounts of when P0 entered.

Possible fixes (not in scope, but flagging for awareness): either restore the original five-principle version on the March post and let `The Birth of P0` carry the change as a narrative event, or annotate the March post to show that P0 was added later. Either way, the contradiction is what needs resolving — pick a version of the history and keep it consistent across posts.

### 1.2 Factual error — Rorty's training and career path

**Post:** `Philosophy as Conversation` (March 27).

Two claims in adjacent sentences are wrong:

> "Rorty was an analytical philosopher trained at Princeton, one of their best."

Rorty was **not** trained at Princeton. He took a BA (1949) and MA (1952) at the University of Chicago, then a PhD (1956) at Yale under Paul Weiss. Princeton was where he taught (1961–1982), not where he was trained.

> "He turned his back and left Princeton, moved on from philosophy to comparative literature at Stanford."

Skips the University of Virginia entirely. The actual trajectory:
- 1961–1982: Professor of Philosophy at Princeton.
- 1982–1998: Kenan Professor of Humanities at the University of Virginia (16 years).
- 1998–2005: Professor of Comparative Literature at Stanford.

The Virginia move (1982) was the one that took him out of a philosophy department. The Stanford move came 16 years later. The post collapses two career moves into one and gets the timing wrong.

### 1.3 Factual issue — Tractatus publication year

**Post:** `From Language to Form of Life` (March 22).

> "His Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus (1921) is the most rigorous version of that picture ever built."

Not strictly wrong, but worth noting: the German text appeared in 1921 in *Annalen der Naturphilosophie*; the English edition (Ogden/Ramsey translation, with the now-standard parallel German/English layout) is 1922. Either date is defensible; flagging only because both are in common circulation and the post doesn't qualify the choice.

### 1.4 Quoted material — verbatim vs paraphrase

**Post:** `From Language to Form of Life` (March 22).

> *"I tried to force my thoughts into a unified whole but couldn't — the best that I could write would never be more than philosophical remarks."*

Presented in quotation marks as Wittgenstein's words. The second half is close to the Preface of *Philosophical Investigations* ("The best that I could write would never be more than philosophical remarks"), but the first half is a paraphrase, not Wittgenstein's wording. The PI Preface actually reads: *"After several unsuccessful attempts to weld my results together into such a whole, I realized that I should never succeed."* A reader treating the quote as verbatim will not find that sentence in Wittgenstein.

Either restore the verbatim passage, or drop the quotation marks and present it as a paraphrase.

### 1.5 Factual claim — Rayleigh–Jeans derivation

**Post:** `From Clockwork to Open Questions` / *How Science Became Relational* (April 26).

> "The Rayleigh-Jeans radiation law, derived from Maxwell's equations, predicted that energy should increase without limit at high frequencies — the ultraviolet catastrophe."

Rayleigh–Jeans is derived from classical electromagnetism *combined with* the equipartition theorem from classical statistical mechanics. The equipartition step is the load-bearing one (and is where the catastrophe enters); Maxwell's equations alone don't yield the law. Either qualify ("derived from classical electromagnetism and the equipartition theorem") or drop the attribution to Maxwell specifically.

## 3. Soft flags (worth a second look)

These are items that may be fine but sit in a zone where verification or qualification would be useful. None are clearly wrong.

### `The Birth of P0 — Being Is Tension across the Interface` (May 10)

The body contains a stray editing fragment that reads more like a planning note than a finished sentence:

> "Saussure is in the field of structural linguistics. He takes it a level up - differentiation between signs: signs behave as beings in their own right - creations"

The dashes and colon make this read as in-progress prose. Not strictly an inaccuracy, but mechanically rough enough to flag.

A similar pattern appears in the introductions to several other formulations ("Fichte is part of German Idealism.", "Heidegger is part of phenomenology.", "Spencer Brown's formulation comes from within the field of mathematical logic.") — these are sentence-stubs that don't quite read as finished prose. Flagging as a category, not item-by-item, since the issue is consistent.

Also: **Spencer Brown** vs **Spencer-Brown**. Both spellings appear in the post (the standard form is hyphenated: *G. Spencer-Brown*).

### `The Relational Reality of RQM` (May 1)

- "Bell never got a Nobel (he died in 1990, before the experimental confirmation was complete)."
  Bell died in 1990 — verified. "Before the experimental confirmation was complete" leans on "complete" doing real work: Aspect's 1981–82 experiments had already strongly confirmed the violations by the time of Bell's death; the loophole-free confirmations came in 2015. The phrasing is defensible if "complete" means loophole-free, but a reader is likely to take it as "before any experimental confirmation," which would be wrong. A small qualifier ("before the loophole-free confirmation") would tighten it.

- "Then in 2010, Bas van Fraassen … published 'Rovelli's World,' a 27-page engagement"
  The paper is in *Foundations of Physics* 40 (2010), 390–417 — that's 28 pages by the standard convention. Minor; could be either depending on how you count.

- "Bohm (1952) restored determinism through hidden variables … The price: non-locality, **faster-than-light influence on trajectories**."
  Standard popular characterisation, but technically Bohmian non-locality concerns the pilot wave's dependence on the configuration of distant particles; it doesn't transmit signals faster than light and doesn't quite involve FTL "influence on trajectories" in the way the phrasing suggests. Defensible at the popular-science register but loose if accuracy to Bohmian mechanics matters.

### `From Clockwork to Open Questions` (April 26)

The post pairs naturally with `The Relational Reality of RQM` and the two are mostly consistent. Note that this post just says "Bohm proposed hidden variables" without dating it, while the RQM post gives 1952. Not inconsistent, just asymmetric — a reader going from one to the other gets the date from only one of the two.

### `A Simple Language Game, Really?` (April 16)

Wittgenstein's Slab game in *Philosophical Investigations* §2 has four words (*block*, *pillar*, *slab*, *beam*), not one. The post simplifies to one word for didactic purposes — this is an explicit adaptation, not an inaccuracy per se, but a reader checking against Wittgenstein will find the divergence. If accuracy to PI §2 matters in this post, a single sentence noting the simplification ("Wittgenstein's original uses four words; I'm reducing to one to keep the example simple") would close the gap.

### `Language, Experience and Reality` (April 21)

"Heidegger, who was his student" — Heidegger's doctorate (1913) and habilitation (1915) were under Heinrich Rickert at Freiburg, not Husserl. He became Husserl's assistant from 1919 and eventually succeeded him at Freiburg in 1928. The common shorthand "Husserl's student" is widespread and defensible, but if precision matters, "Husserl's junior colleague and protégé" or "Husserl's assistant and successor" is more accurate.

`From the Turn to the Present` (April 6) repeats the same phrasing ("Heidegger, Husserl's student"), so this is a consistency point: both pages compress the relationship the same way. Either fix both or leave both.

### `The Body as Medium` (April 1)

- "Husserl still kept consciousness separated from reality by what he called an abyss"
  The "abyss" vocabulary is more readily associated with Heidegger (*Abgrund*). Husserl's relevant move is the *epoché* / transcendental reduction — bracketing the natural attitude, not crossing an abyss. The post's attribution of "abyss" to Husserl is loose. Defensible as a metaphor, less so as Husserl's own term.

- "responded: 'I do not see much that separates us.'"
  The Royaumont 1958 colloquium is well documented; the historical account is that Merleau-Ponty pressed for rapprochement and Ryle gave a more dismissive response. The quote attributed to Merleau-Ponty fits the spirit of his overture but isn't easy to source as a verbatim line. Worth checking against a printed source (the *Cahiers de Royaumont IV: La Philosophie Analytique*, 1962) if the quote is to be kept inside quotation marks.

- "another two decades before Rorty would shatter the mirror"
  *Philosophy and the Mirror of Nature* is 1979; Merleau-Ponty died 1961. That's 18 years. "Two decades" is rounded up — fine as a phrasing, flagged only because the post is unusually precise elsewhere.

### `From the Turn to the Present` (April 6)

"All three [traditions] were strongly influenced by mathematics at their inception, though that influence only survived long term in the analytical tradition."
The claim is unobjectionable for analytical (Frege, Russell) and defensible for continental (Husserl was a mathematician under Weierstrass) and partly for pragmatism (Peirce was a logician/mathematician; James and Dewey less so). The "all three" framing is a compression that a careful reader might want softened — but the underlying point holds for the figures most often cited as founders.

### `Let's Talk Software Languages` (April 11)

"The first assembler — mnemonics mapper — was 31 instructions long written by hand in binary — David Wheeler."
The reference is to Wheeler's *Initial Orders* for EDSAC (Cambridge, 1949), 31 orders. Calling Initial Orders "the first assembler" is a defensible historical claim (the Initial Orders translated single-letter mnemonics into machine code at load time), though some historians reserve "assembler" for later, more sophisticated tools. The substance is fine; flagging only because the term carries a bit of contestability.

## Cross-post consistency check (other than 1.1)

A broader check across the seed posts and the philosophy-tradition posts found no further internal contradictions:

- **Seed wording** (P0–P5) is consistent across `SPLectrum is Born`, `SPLectrum and First Principles`, and `First Principles — the Social Core`. The "Social Core" post elaborates on P3–P5 in ways that extend but do not contradict the original phrasings.
- **Wittgenstein's trajectory** (Tractatus → return 1929 → PI published posthumously) is told consistently in `From Language to Form of Life` and `Philosophy as Conversation`.
- **The three-tradition split** is presented consistently in `From the Turn to the Present` and `The Body as Medium`, though the latter calls pragmatism a tradition that "cut across both" analytical and continental, while the former lists pragmatism as one of three distinct traditions. These are compatible framings (cutting across vs being a third), not contradictory.
- **The 2022 Nobel attribution** (Aspect, Clauser, Zeilinger) is consistent across `From Clockwork to Open Questions` and `The Relational Reality of RQM`.
- **Mirror neurons in the 1990s** (`The Body as Medium`) is consistent with the historical record (Rizzolatti et al., Parma, published 1996).
