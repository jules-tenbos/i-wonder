# Bottom row section advice — Language, Vocabulary, Tools

## What the three sections are for

The bottom row is the machinery — the material handlers the framework and applied rows draw on.

- **Language** — the relational medium. Languages in the broadest sense: natural, formal, software, domain-specific, and beyond.
- **Vocabulary** — the conceptual. Concepts made explicit, one vocabulary per language game. Making the words of a game explicit lets the game be studied, compared, and kept free of implicit drift.
- **Tools** — the instrumental. What one reaches for, defined by use. Tools that fit SPLectrum's relational, language-centric worldview.

## Shared shape

All three sections take the same structure:

```
/[section]/
├── a-z/          (alphabetical lookup, with /a/, /b/, /c/… letter folders)
├── concepts/     (cross-cutting concept pages, flat)
└── types/        (categorisation overviews, flat)
```

A-Z is the navigation/orientation layer — brief entries with pointers. Concepts is the discursive treatment of cross-cutting ideas. Types is categorisation overviews. The depth lives in concept and type pages; A-Z entries are short.

## A-Z editorial criteria

Each section's A-Z has its own criterion for what earns an entry:

- **Language A-Z** — things that are languages or kinds of languages. Test: asking "what is X?" gets the answer "a language" or "a kind of language." Includes specific languages (English, Python, category theory) and categorical names (functional language, DSL, dead language, sign language).
- **Vocabulary A-Z** — specific vocabularies and named-kinds-of-vocabulary. Heidegger's, Whitehead's, RQM's, pragmatism's; also terms like "process vocabulary," "phenomenological vocabulary."
- **Tools A-Z** — items with a recognised tool-use in the SPLectrum world. Each entry describes what work the item does when held as a tool. The criterion is functional, not substantive.

## Cross-bleeding by aspect

The same item may appear in multiple A-Zs by its different aspects. Category theory has a language-aspect entry, a vocabulary-aspect entry, and a tool-aspect entry. Cross-links between them make the modal observation architectural: what something is depends on how it is held.

This cross-bleeding is a feature, not a bug. It follows from the use-definition (Wittgensteinian, Rortyan): the category is functional, not substantive.

## The Language / Vocabulary distinction

| | Has its own grammar? | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Language | Yes (own grammar at level of presentation) | English, mathematics, Python, category theory |
| Vocabulary | No (borrows host language's surface grammar) | Heidegger's, Whitehead's, RQM's |
| DSL | Yes — but hosted by another language | SQL on text; some dense philosophical vocabularies |

DSL is the bridge: a vocabulary with its own internal (conceptual) grammar, expressed through a host language. Some thinkers' vocabularies meet this density (Heidegger, Hegel, late Wittgenstein) and are analytically readable as DSLs — but their primary placement remains in Vocabulary, since the DSL reading is an observation about them, not a re-categorisation.

## Tools scope

Tools holds the operational/instrumental face of SPLectrum's world. The editorial principle: tools that fit the relational, language-centric worldview — what an inhabitant of this world would naturally reach for. Forward-looking and inclusive (anything that fits, whether deployed yet or not), not backward-looking and narrow (only items already in active use).

First-pass type cuts (to refine as entries accumulate):
- Tools for relational analysis (category theory, graph theory, formal relational structure)
- Tools for vocabulary work (shadow-vocabulary, translation moves, charitable reading)
- Tools for structural / formal expression (notation systems, diagrammatic methods)
- Tools for engaging with thinkers (bracketing, dialectic, immanent critique)

## Register

SPLectrum is the speaker throughout. The bottom row is structural, reference-form — closer to the seed pages than to the blog. Non-claiming, non-personifying, learning tone. Match the register of the existing language and vocabulary landing pages.

## How to apply

- A-Z entries are brief: short explanation plus pointers (internal to concept/type pages, external to authoritative sources). Don't expand A-Z entries into full treatments — that work belongs in concepts or types.
- Concept pages (e.g., "what is a language?", "what is a vocabulary?") are the load-bearing definitional pages. They can use P-tags and SPLectrum vocabulary in their proper contexts.
- Type pages are categorisation overviews — taxonomic, not discursive. Keep them scannable.
- When an item appears in multiple A-Zs, each entry should describe the aspect relevant to that section. Don't repeat the same text across entries.
