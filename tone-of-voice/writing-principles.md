# Writing principles

Specific patterns for how to write across the site. These complement the editorial stance (how to carry yourself) with rules about what the writing itself should look like.

---

## "Is like", not "is"

SPLectrum takes "is like" over "is" — diversity over uniformity. "Is" declares, fixes, imposes from above. "Is like" invites comparison, acknowledges difference. When the underlying material is genuinely diverse, the surface should reflect that diversity.

- Don't normalise lists, titles, or summaries into a single pattern just because convention prefers uniformity. Variety that springs from the material is a feature.
- Prefer convergence, meeting, translation, reading over equates, defines, prescribes.
- Still fix genuine inconsistencies (typos, formatting bugs) — the principle isn't "anything goes", it's "diversity that's meaningful, not sloppy".

## Avoid bipolar framing

Don't use bipolar language (dangerous/safe, right/wrong, good approach/bad approach). Analyse any statement using these patterns and you'll find ethical flavour underneath. The project argues against projecting ethical frameworks onto emergent systems.

- Observe and comment, don't judge. Note patterns without labelling them good or bad.
- When you catch a bipolar construction in a draft ("the dangerous next step"), find the neutral description that carries the same observation.

## Touchstone over comprehensive

Ref lib pages serve as touchstones — reference points that touch on the topic enough to hold the point, then stop. Lighter wins over heavier as long as the point lands. Pages are never "finished" — work-in-progress by nature.

- Does the extra material make the point land better, or just make the page heavier? If the latter, stop.
- Three named thinkers usually enough. Four starts to read as a parade; five is a list.
- When in doubt, cut. Missing depth is fixable; cluttered pages are hard to clean up.

## Signpost the road ahead

When something is planned ahead or under active development, say so. Don't treat "more coming" as a defect to hide behind silence. Pointing at the road ahead is part of the site's honesty about what it is.

- Use concrete forward pointers: "more coming in the [series]", "open territory", "developing in [blog label]".
- Link to the conversation surface (blog labels, scheduled posts), not private working material.
- Keep the signpost light — one sentence, not a roadmap.
- Use for genuine open territory, not filler.

## Make concepts explicit

Too much is done implicitly. Implicit vocabulary creates misunderstandings and drifts over time. Making the concepts of each game explicit is the method.

- Watch for concepts used with a specific technical meaning — candidates for explicit documentation in the Vocabulary area.
- Don't let terms drift in without a definition. When reviewing, if a term is loaded with meaning implicitly, flag it.

## "Foundational" — three senses

When SPLectrum calls itself a "foundational project", the term carries three specific senses:

1. **Axiomatic base with historical support** — the seed is axioms backed by mainstream historical thinking.
2. **About the dynamics** — the living, evolving relational structure of language. Not "starting from" first principles as a one-time ground floor.
3. **Equal standing, cross-disciplinary** — P4 applied at the discipline level.

Don't slip into "starting from" or "first principles" framings for sense 2 — that misreads dynamics as static ground.
