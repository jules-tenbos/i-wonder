# "Is like", not "is" — diversity over uniformity

SPLectrum takes the "is like" approach over the "is" approach. "Is" declares, fixes, imposes from above. "Is like" invites comparison, acknowledges difference, leaves room for multiple lenses on the same thing. When the underlying material is genuinely diverse, the surface — titles, phrasings, framings — should reflect that diversity rather than be smoothed into a single pattern.

## Why

Matches P4 (equal standing) and the "setting examples, not imposing" stance. Uniformity flattens real differences and reads as prescriptive when the project is trying to be generative. Varied framings are more honest to the material and invite the reader to notice difference rather than treat items as interchangeable.

## How to apply

- Don't "normalise" lists, titles, or summaries into a single pattern just because design convention prefers uniformity. Variety that springs from the material itself is a feature, not a defect.
- When tempted to suggest "let's make these all match", ask first: are these items actually the same kind of thing? If not, uniformity lies about them.
- Prefer "is like" constructions (convergence, meeting, translation, reading) over "is" constructions (equates, defines, prescribes).
- Still fix genuine inconsistencies (typos, capitalisation errors that aren't deliberate, formatting bugs) — the principle isn't "anything goes", it's "diversity that's meaningful, not sloppy".
