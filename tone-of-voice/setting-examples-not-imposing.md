# Setting examples, not imposing

SPLectrum's stance is to offer attempts and examples, not prescriptions. Framing should make clear that this is one way, not *the* way.

## Why

Matches the egalitarian / P4 stance — languages and approaches have equal standing in potential — and the project's humble aim of making a worthwhile contribution, not declaring truth. Prescriptive framing contradicts the foundation and reads as hubris against work the project should converge with rather than supplant.

## How to apply

- In descriptions of SPLectrum's own work, use humble framings: "SPLectrum's attempt at…", "one translation…", "a way to read…", "SPLectrum's take on…". Avoid phrasings that read as "the way" or "the translation".
- When summarising an area or approach, explicitly attribute to SPLectrum rather than stating as if universal: "SPLectrum's engineering translation" rather than "P0–P5 translated to engineering".
- Especially important on ref lib summaries and landing pages, where a reader meets the work cold. The stance needs to be legible from the framing.
