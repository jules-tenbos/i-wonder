# Learning tone, not magister, not humble

When writing about material outside SPLectrum's own work — positioning pages especially — the register is that of someone engaged with the material on equal standing. Learning from it. Noticing what's there. Not asserting authority over it (magister). Not disclaiming authority through excessive deference (humble).

Both magister and humble are off-balance. One speaks from above, the other bows from below — same structure, opposite direction. Learning tone is on the level.

## Why

The project operates on equal standing (P4 applied at the working level). When positioning describes other thinkers or frameworks, it's a peer reading them — not a teacher pronouncing, not a student bowing. Either extreme signals an unequal relation the project doesn't hold.

## How to apply

- **Say what's observable**: "what these thinkers share is X", "similar direction", "the shared move is Y", "they land in overlapping territory". Specific, descriptive.
- **Avoid magisterial claims**: "they arrive at the same observation", "convergence unlikely to be projection", "being IS tension", "there IS no outside view". These assert as established what's actually interpretive.
- **Avoid humble disclaimers**: "we make no claims", "it may just resonate", "the seed perhaps echoes", "for the reader to judge". Excess deference reads as performative humility.
- **Place SPLectrum's own work with observational honesty**: "the seed lands in this territory", "arrived at independently", "close affinity, not lineage". Factual about self-positioning, not triumphalist, not apologetic.
- The tone is: *"here's what we see, here's what we take from it"*. Engaged, noticing, on the level.

## Part-of, not us-vs-them

When writing about traditions SPLectrum draws on — philosophy, science, the thinkers of the relational turn — frame the project as *part of* the same ongoing movement, not a separate thing arriving at similar conclusions from outside.

- Traditions aren't external reference points. Philosophy and science are the main pillars of SPLectrum's interdisciplinary approach — the project stands in them, not alongside them.
- Collective movements (a "turn", an "arc") aren't made by individual thinkers each separately arriving somewhere. They're constituted by the thinkers collectively. SPLectrum contributes to the movement; it doesn't parallel it.
- Prefer "continues", "extends", "joins this family", "contributes to" over "lands in", "arrives at", "converges with", "shares the same conclusion as" — the latter all create separation between SPLectrum and the material.
- The "outside voice" stance (honouring traditions on their own terms, not bending them toward SPLectrum) coexists with "part-of". We don't bend the thinkers' work; we do participate in the ongoing conversation they belong to.

## Stand back when the content speaks

Positioning is about laying the material out, not commenting on it. The stronger the placement, the less commentary is needed.

- **Close affinity pages stand back completely.** The placement in the close-affinity category already says SPLectrum holds the affinity; the content — the thinkers, the arc, the shape — speaks for itself. No closing paragraph about how SPLectrum relates; that would weaken the presentation. Let the material sit.
- **On the fence pages can speak more.** These pieces straddle in and out of alignment with the project. The friction is the point, and the friction often has to be named to be visible. SPLectrum's reading of where the overlap and divergence sit is part of what the page offers.
- **Wider landscape is undecided** — how much commentary belongs there depends on what ends up in the section and how it relates to SPLectrum's work. Follow the material.

The unifying rule: don't comment where the placement already carries the meaning. Commentary earns its place only when it adds something the content doesn't already show.
