# Quality rubric

## 1. Purpose

This rubric is an internal standard for reviewing pages on splectrum.world. It exists to make reviews more consistent — so two reads of the same page converge rather than diverge — to benchmark the site against external quality frameworks rather than only internal taste, and to surface drift over time: where the voice, the purpose, or the clarity of a piece slides without anyone noticing. It is a checking tool, not an authoring tool; it belongs after drafting, not during.

The site has three axes — topnav (personal), ref lib (informational), blog (conversational) — each with its own register and purpose. Applying ref-lib criteria to a topnav opinion piece produces misleading scores, and vice versa. This rubric is structured as one shared framework plus three axis-specific profiles. Pick the right profile for the axis before scoring anything.

Ref lib is the canonical profile. It's the axis where external standards (E-E-A-T, Diataxis) and internal principles (standing back, touchstone, vocabulary consistency) all apply in full, and where independent review adds the most. Topnav is lighter by design — the author can be confident and opinionated there; only a small set of checks apply. Blog is persona-grouped and tuning is ongoing.

## 2. External yardstick: Google E-E-A-T and Page Quality

Google's public [Search Quality Rater Guidelines](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf) give a common-sense definition of quality that most readers implicitly expect.

**E-E-A-T** — four concerns, Trust at the centre:

- **Experience** — does the creator have first-hand or life experience with what the page is about.
- **Expertise** — does the creator have the necessary knowledge or skill for the topic.
- **Authoritativeness** — is the creator or site a known go-to source for this topic.
- **Trustworthiness** — is the page accurate, honest, reliable. Untrustworthy pages have low E-E-A-T regardless of expertise or experience.

**Page Quality scale (5 points):** Lowest / Low / Medium / High / Highest. Anchored by two ideas: whether the page has a *beneficial purpose* and whether it *achieves that purpose well*.

The weight of each letter varies by axis. Trust applies everywhere. Experience lands heaviest on first-person topnav and comment-persona blog posts. Expertise and Authoritativeness land heaviest on ref lib. Profiles below specify which letters carry weight and which get relaxed or omitted.

## 3. Purpose check: Diataxis

[Diataxis](https://diataxis.fr/) separates content by what the reader needs:

- **Tutorial** — learning by doing.
- **How-to** — goal-oriented.
- **Reference** — information-oriented, austere description.
- **Explanation** — understanding-oriented, discursive.

Most of splectrum.world is **Explanation**, with some pure **Reference** (vocabulary, namespace convention, ref lib indexes). Staying in type per mode: explanation shouldn't slide into how-to; reference shouldn't carry opinion or narrative. Mixed-mode pages usually need splitting.

## 4. Profiles

Each page sits in one axis; use the matching profile.

### 4.1 Ref lib profile — informational axis (canonical)

The main review tool. Single narrator persona. Consistency across pages is the spine. Touchstone discipline over comprehensive. All external and internal standards apply in full.

**Header block** (framing, not scored):

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Axis | Ref lib (informational) |
| Persona | Narrator (impersonal, structural) |
| Diataxis type | Reference / Explanation / hybrid |

**Scored rows:**

| Lens | Criterion | Weight | What the check is |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google | Experience | Light | Narrator recedes by design; first-hand signal is not the relevant register |
| Google | Expertise | Full | Does the material demonstrate domain competence |
| Google | Authoritativeness | Full | Is this page the go-to for its area within the site |
| Google | Trustworthiness | Full | Accurate, honest, no overclaim |
| Google | Page Quality (overall) | Full | Beneficial purpose + achieves it well |
| Diataxis | Stays in type | Full | Reference stays reference; explanation stays explanation; mixed needs signposting |
| SPLectrum | Voice consistency | Full | Narrator register, impersonal, no first-person |
| SPLectrum | No prescriptive/authority-claiming language | Full | Setting examples, not imposing. "Is like", not "is". No magisterial claims |
| SPLectrum | Content left to speak | Full | No trailing SPLectrum commentary on content that already does its own work |
| SPLectrum | Touchstone discipline | Full | Every paragraph earns its place; lighter wins if the point lands; three thinkers beat five |
| SPLectrum | Signposting (open territory) | Full | Embryonic areas named; forward pointers land on blog labels, series, specific follow-ups |
| SPLectrum | Explicit vocabulary | Full | Technical terms defined in `/vocabulary/`; no jargon drifting in without documentation |
| SPLectrum | Substance | Full | Real observation, connection, argument; not framing without content; no bipolar framing |
| SPLectrum | Cohesion | Full | Terms match usage on other ref lib pages; outbound links land in matching framings |

Rows that may be **N/A** on ref lib: Substance and Signposting on pure index pages (nothing to have substance about, no open edges internal to the page).

### 4.2 Topnav profile — personal axis (lightweight)

Author voice. Confidence is allowed and encouraged — these are personal statements, not SPLectrum claims. The ref-lib standing-back discipline does not apply. The analogous check is whether the statement is honest or performative — no slogan-creep, no humble-bowing, no promotional tics.

**Header block:**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Axis | Topnav (personal) |
| Persona | Author (Jules) |
| Diataxis type | Explanation |

**Scored rows:**

| Lens | Criterion | Weight | What the check is |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google | Experience | Full | First-person, lived position of the author |
| Google | Trustworthiness | Full | Honest, no overclaim, disclosure where relevant |
| Google | Page Quality (overall) | Full | Lands its purpose clearly |
| Diataxis | Stays in type | Full | No drift into how-to or reference |
| SPLectrum | Voice consistency | Full | Author voice, first-person grounded; doesn't slide into narrator mode |
| SPLectrum | Honest vs performative | Full | Confident statements land as genuine; no slogan lines; no disclaimer-bowing; no "look at how humble I am" moves |
| SPLectrum | Cadence | Full | Rhythm; not wall-of-text; short declarative where it counts |
| SPLectrum | Signposting | Full | Outbound links land in matching destinations |
| SPLectrum | Substance | Full | Real stance in few words; not filler, not padding |

**Omitted** on topnav: Google Expertise and Authoritativeness (these pages aren't displaying expertise or claiming authority); ref-lib-style Standing-back (confidence is part of the point).

### 4.3 Blog profile — conversational axis (persona-grouped, tuning in progress)

Blog posts vary by persona label; one scoring set doesn't fit. Three persona groups, each with different weighting. Full criteria per group are still to tune with use — the scaffolding below is the starting frame.

**Header block:**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Axis | Blog (conversational) |
| Persona label | SPLectrum / comment / thought / named-source / positioning/science |
| Persona group | A / B / C |
| Diataxis type | Explanation |

**Persona groups:**

**Group A — SPLectrum speaks.** The SPLectrum persona: from within the project, structured, principled, "let's see if this holds". Full standing-back discipline applies. Explicit-vocabulary matters (technical terms should land in `/vocabulary/`). Disclaimer-device allowance: the persona profile permits explicit disclaimers — don't dock standing-back for stated devices.

**Group B — Narrator speaks of others.** Positioning / science / named-source personas. Restraint about applying the seed. Fidelity to subject; authority through accuracy. Standing-back is about the outside subject — no bending thinkers toward SPLectrum's framing. Learning tone, not magister, not humble.

**Group C — Person speaks.** Comment and thought personas. Most direct first person; casual, meta-reflective or raw. Authoritativeness N/A. Substance = genuine self-disclosure or observation, not mini-manifesto. Cadence and density still relevant. Standing-back is about not-over-claiming-meta.

**Tuning TBD.** Criteria per group develop with use. Until tuned, reviews of blog posts can use the ref-lib profile as a starting reference, noting where it over-reaches per persona.

## 5. How to use

Score each applicable criterion on a 1–5 scale, using the Google PQ anchors as reference points: 1 = Lowest, 2 = Low, 3 = Medium, 4 = High, 5 = Highest. Half-steps (e.g. "High+") are allowed where a criterion sits clearly between two anchors. Use **N/A** where the criterion is explicitly relaxed or omitted for the profile, or where the page type doesn't support that check (an index has no substance to grade).

A review produces the profile's header block + scored table + comments. The comments column is where the review actually lives — a number on its own is a signal, not a verdict. A piece is publishable when no applicable cell scores below 3 and the weighted feel of the SPLectrum block is High. Below 3 on Trust or Stays-in-type is a blocking issue, not a polish issue.
