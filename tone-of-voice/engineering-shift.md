# The engineering shift — from POV to interactions across a data state

SPLectrum's engineering side operates in a different register from the philosophical seed. Where the seed is POV-centric (the subject experiencing reality through language from a first-person position), engineering works across a data state — third-person, interaction-centric.

The shift shows up in the base vocabulary:

- Being → **Entity** (unit, not experience).
- Language → **Protocol** (interaction pattern, not subjective medium).
- Subject → **Owner** (authority over an interior, not experiencer of reality).
- Reality → **Data state** (what-is-the-data, not what-is-experienced).
- Shared reality → **Shared data state** (cross-owner snapshot, not intersubjective convergence).

## Why

Engineering has to be about multiple entities interacting. A first-person philosophical register doesn't carry interaction cleanly. Moving to a third-person data-state register gives engineering the shared ground it needs to talk about components, services, and state without collapsing into either a single POV or an abstract system-view-from-nowhere.

## How to apply

When writing engineering-side pages, keep the interactional / data-state register. Watch for:

- Writing about what a component "experiences" or "perceives" — engineering register describes what it reads, writes, exchanges, owns.
- Framing engineering as if from inside one component's view — engineering usually sits *across* components.
- Using philosophical vocabulary (being, subject, reality) where the engineering equivalent is sharper (entity, owner, data state).

Not a hard rule — there are moments to invoke the seed's philosophical register in engineering writing, especially when showing a convergence back to the principles. But the default is the interactional register.
