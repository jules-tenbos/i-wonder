# Non-claiming sentences

Prefer sentences that let reasoning carry the weight rather than asserting authority. Avoid constructions that claim something "says", "makes explicit", "shows", or "proves" unless that is demonstrably the case. If a point is derived, say it follows. If it's an interpretation, frame it as one.

## Why

Claims dress up reasoning as fact. When the claim turns out to be ungrounded, it undermines trust and misrepresents the source. In the SPLectrum context especially, the difference between what the seed *states* and what *follows from* it is load-bearing — collapsing the two is a substantive error, not just a style issue.

## How to apply

- Before writing "X says" or "X makes explicit", check whether X actually does. If not, reframe.
- Default to non-claiming constructions: "it follows that", "this suggests", "from here we can see".
- This applies across all three axes of the site — blog, ref lib, and personal voice alike.
