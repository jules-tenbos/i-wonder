# Cold Tone-of-Voice and Tone-of-Thought Analysis

Based on a careful reading of all 23 published blog posts (March 2026 onwards, plus 2024-2025 pre-Splectrum posts). Cold read — no prior context, no existing tone guides consulted.

Date: 2026-04-08

---

## PART 1: SHARED QUALITIES ACROSS ALL WRITING

### How this voice sounds

The voice is conversational but unhurried. It reads like someone thinking aloud in good company — not lecturing, not performing, but working through ideas and inviting the reader to follow along. There is a consistent warmth without sentimentality. The register sits between informal and semi-formal: contractions appear freely ("don't," "isn't," "I'd"), sentences start with "But" and "And," yet the vocabulary stretches comfortably into philosophical and technical territory when the subject demands it. The voice never code-switches into academic prose. Even when discussing Heisenberg's uncertainty principle or Fichte's self-positing I, it stays in the same register — the one a thoughtful person would use at a dinner table with friends who happen to be interested.

### Consistent habits and patterns

1. **First person as anchor.** Nearly every post is grounded in "I" — what I think, what I found, what I noticed, where I am on the journey. This is not narcissistic; it is structurally honest. The voice declares its position rather than pretending to speak from nowhere. Even in the most expository posts (the science positioning posts, RQM), the "I" recedes but the perspective remains situated.

2. **Movement from concrete to abstract (and back).** The writing almost always begins with something tangible — a dinner party, a fire, a builder shouting "Slab," binary code on a 6502 processor — and uses that to climb toward a principle. Then it often returns to the concrete for a closing beat. This is the dominant structural rhythm across every persona.

3. **Short declarative sentences for emphasis.** When a key idea arrives, the sentence gets shorter. "Then it tore." "That's not sharing. That's P3 captured." "No more outside observer, no more representation." "I think it can." These function as punctuation marks for the thinking — moments where the voice lands hard on something it considers important.

4. **Dashes and parenthetical asides.** The writing uses em-dashes and mid-sentence qualifications extensively. This creates the feeling of real-time thought: the writer interrupts themselves, corrects, adds a caveat, then continues. It produces a texture of honesty — the reader can see the thinking being refined as it unfolds.

5. **Italics for emphasis, not decoration.** Italics are used sparingly and always to mark philosophical weight: "*is* my reality," "*our* reality," "language as something you *live*." They carry real semantic work.

6. **No jargon without immediate unpacking.** Philosophical terms are introduced and immediately explained or demonstrated through example. The voice does not assume shared vocabulary — it builds it within the post. When it does use a term (qualia, phenomenology, decoherence), the explanation follows within a sentence or two.

7. **Linking to sources, not asserting authority.** Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy links, Wikipedia references, specific papers and experiments — the voice shows its work. This is not argumentative scaffolding; it reads more like "here is where I found this, go look for yourself."

8. **Rhetorical questions used genuinely.** "So what, you may ask. Should I be impressed?" "How far can it be stretched?" "Am I a libertarian?" These are not rhetorical tricks. They function as genuine pivot points where the writing changes direction. The voice uses questions to open inquiry, not to set up predetermined answers.

### What it avoids

- **Certainty and declaration from above.** The voice never claims to have the final answer. Even the seed principles are presented as something that "fell into place" and needs stress-testing, not as doctrine. The most assertive the voice gets is "I think it can" — and even that is followed by a period, not an exclamation mark.

- **Academic apparatus.** No footnotes, no formal citation style, no literature review framing. The voice cites sources but always conversationally. It never says "as Smith (2021) argues"; it says "Rorty said" or "Rovelli published."

- **Polemic and adversarial framing.** There is almost no attacking of opposing positions. Even when the voice disagrees (with the "mirror" picture of reality, with centralised data platforms, with the treatment of qualia as "inaccessible"), it frames the disagreement as "I see it differently" rather than "they are wrong." The closest to combative language is "pathological P3" in the privacy post, and even there the anger is aimed at structures, not people.

- **Exclamation marks.** They appear extremely rarely. The voice trusts the content to carry its own weight.

- **Hedging to the point of emptiness.** While the voice qualifies frequently, it does not hedge itself into paralysis. It says what it thinks. The qualifications are genuine ("I don't know if I succeeded but..."), not defensive.

- **Meta-commentary about writing quality.** The voice openly acknowledges being "not born a natural writer" in early posts, but it does not apologise for the writing itself. It simply writes.

### How it thinks

The thinking is synthetic, not analytic in the narrow sense. The voice naturally draws connections across domains — software engineering and phenomenology, quantum physics and dinner parties, binary code and bodily sensation. These connections are never forced or metaphorical; the voice treats them as structural, as instances of the same pattern in different media. This is the deepest characteristic of the thinking: pattern recognition across domains, held together by the conviction that "the parallel is structural, not metaphorical."

The thinking is also evolutionary. Ideas build on each other across posts. The voice returns to earlier formulations, revises them (P0 being added to the seed), and openly narrates the process of revision. It thinks in layers, not in leaps.

There is a consistent anti-hierarchical instinct. The voice resists "from above" in every domain: no God's-eye view in epistemology, no central authority in engineering, no imposed values in community. This is not just a philosophical position — it shapes the rhetoric. The voice never talks down. It never says "obviously" or "clearly." It treats the reader as a peer who might see things differently.

Finally, the thinking values simplicity of foundation and complexity of expression. Six lines to ground everything — but those six lines generate an open-ended web. The voice is drawn to minimal starting points (Turing's tape, Fichte's self-positing act, the boundary as creation) and loves watching what unfolds from them.

## PART 2: PER-PERSONA DIFFERENCES

### Persona: Splectrum (9 posts)

This is the framework voice — the one that speaks *from within* the Splectrum project. It is the most confident and the most architectural of all the personas.

**How it sounds:** Assured but invitational. The Splectrum voice presents the seed principles, walks through them, applies them, stress-tests them — but always with the framing of "let's see if this holds." It uses more structured exposition than the other personas: numbered principles, bolded terms, clear walk-throughs. Sentences tend to be slightly longer and more carefully constructed. The engineering posts (Splectrum Engineering, Commitments) are the most technically dense writing in the entire corpus, with explicit design vocabulary ("fabric," "pillar," "commitment," "conformance"). The philosophical Splectrum posts (First Principles, Privacy, Language and Experience) are more lyrical.

**How it thinks:** The Splectrum voice thinks in principles first, examples second. It begins with the seed and works outward. It is systematising — it wants to show how the principles generate structure across domains. The engineering posts are particularly notable: the voice moves from philosophical axiom to engineering constraint in a single step, treating the transition as low-friction. This is where the voice is most original: it does philosophy and engineering in the same breath without apology.

**Distinctive features:**
- Uses the seed principles (P0-P5) as explicit reference points, often quoting them in italics.
- Frequently uses the metaphor of "unpacking" — the seed contains complexity that needs to be unfolded.
- The most explicit about disclaiming imposition: "there isn't the slightest intent to impose anything onto others."
- Includes the only post that directly addresses privacy/politics as a structural consequence of the framework, not a moral argument.
- The engineering posts use a distinctive vocabulary ("fabric," "pillar," "honesty criterion," "low friction," "data state change") that does not appear in other personas.

### Persona: comment (3 posts)

**How it sounds:** The comment voice is the most casual, personal, and meta-reflective. It steps back from the framework and talks about the *process* — how the seed came together, how AI collaboration works, how a new principle emerged. It is the "behind the scenes" voice. Sentences are shorter. The tone is lighter. It opens with direct address: "So what, you may ask." It uses more colloquial expressions: "got the writer fluids flowing," "a godsend," "a bit of bother."

**How it thinks:** The comment voice thinks about thinking. It is self-aware about the project as a project — not just about the ideas but about how they arrived, what triggered them, why they matter to the author personally. It is also the most human of the voices: acknowledging struggle ("I can't seem to get myself to become a writer"), crediting the AI collaboration, narrating surprise ("to my surprise"). The dinner party post is the most accessible piece in the entire corpus — it deliberately stays at ground level and never ascends to abstraction.

**Distinctive features:**
- Most direct address to the reader ("you may ask," "let's try it out").
- Most personal disclosure (struggle with writing, history of the blog, surprise at how ideas arrived).
- The "And Then There Were Six" post is notable for narrating the live evolution of the framework — a new principle emerging during writing, not before it. This is the comment voice at its most distinctive: thinking about how the thinking changed.
- Uses the least philosophical vocabulary of any persona.
- Earliest post (the anti-foundationalism one from 2025) is labelled "comment" and reads quite differently from the later comments — more tentative, more "I'm just starting out," more openly uncertain.

### Persona: thought (4 posts — the earliest writing, 2024)

**How it sounds:** The thought voice is the rawest and least polished. These four posts are clearly from before the Splectrum framework existed. The voice is more fragmented — shorter paragraphs, bullet points, headings that function as scaffolding. The writing is less flowing, more like notes-towards-a-position. "Promises Made, Promises Broken" is almost confessional: line breaks as pauses, no argument structure at all, just a person admitting they haven't delivered.

**How it thinks:** The thought persona is pre-systematic. It has intuitions ("we are all only ever right to some extent," "diversity is crucial") but no framework to organise them. The thinking is reaching for something but hasn't found it yet. "Philosophy and the Brain" and "Evolution and the Brain" read like summaries of what the author has been learning, not yet digested into a voice. "Am I a Libertarian?" is a values inventory, not an argument.

**Distinctive features:**
- Bullet points and numbered lists appear more frequently here than in any other persona.
- Uses headings (## subheadings) more than the later posts, suggesting the writing needs structural scaffolding that the later voice no longer requires.
- The most emotionally exposed persona: "I feel a lot more comfortable in what I know," "I am ready for the journey," "New promise made... let's see what happens!"
- These posts lack the characteristic concrete-to-abstract-to-concrete movement of the later writing. They tend to stay at the abstract/summary level.
- The voice in these posts is recognisably the same person as the Splectrum voice, but without confidence in a direction. The curiosity and anti-hierarchical instinct are already present; the organising principle is not.

### Persona: named source — Wittgenstein

**How it sounds:** This is the voice of a storyteller recounting an intellectual drama. The Wittgenstein post reads almost like biography in miniature. The sentences are vivid and punchy: "A logical straitjacket, beautifully tailored." "Then it tore." The voice is not neutral — it has clear admiration for Wittgenstein's turn, and it tells the story with narrative momentum. There is a beginning, a crisis, a resolution, and a denouement.

**How it thinks:** The thinking is interpretive. The voice reads Wittgenstein through Splectrum lenses — "from language to language game, from language game to form of life" — but this framing is held lightly. The post does not argue that Wittgenstein was "really saying what Splectrum says." Instead, it traces a movement and notes the resonance. The thinking connects across domains (quantum mechanics mentioned alongside the language turn) but without forcing the connection.

**Distinctive features:**
- The most narrative of all posts. It has a plot arc.
- Uses the word "turn" as a structural concept — this becomes a recurring motif across the positioning posts.
- Ends with a forward-looking handoff: "Next up: Rorty, who walked through that door and kept going." This serialised structure is unique to the positioning posts.

### Persona: named source — Rorty

**How it sounds:** More combative than the Wittgenstein post, reflecting Rorty's own combative stance. The voice picks up Rorty's energy: "there is no mirror," "shatter the mirror and declare the borders null and void." The prose is slightly more compressed and harder-edged. The imagery is more confrontational (shattered glass, mirrors, dams breached).

**How it thinks:** The thinking here is diagnostic. The voice identifies what Rorty achieved (conversation replacing representation) and what he left unfinished ("that question was not put on the table"). The analysis is sharper and more critical than in the Wittgenstein post — the voice admires Rorty but also identifies a gap. This is the most evaluative of all the positioning posts.

**Distinctive features:**
- Uses Rorty's own quotations more than any other positioning post uses its subject's words. The voice blends with its subject.
- The one post that explicitly names a philosophical gap and leaves it open as unfinished business.
- Shortest of the three named-source posts, reflecting a more concentrated argument.

### Persona: named source — Merleau-Ponty

**How it sounds:** The warmest and most embodied of the positioning posts. The voice slows down. It uses more sensory language: "gesture," "rhythm," "voicing," "the hand that shields the face from the heat." This reflects its subject — a post about the body written with attention to bodily sensation. The tone is more admiring and less critical than the Rorty post.

**How it thinks:** The thinking here is integrative. The voice connects Merleau-Ponty forward to neuroscience and backward to the analytical tradition (the Royaumont conference). It thinks in convergences: independent paths arriving at the same place. This is the most explicitly cross-tradition post — it names the analytical-continental divide and shows it dissolving.

**Distinctive features:**
- Uses more sensory and physical language than any other post in the corpus.
- The Royaumont anecdote is the only time in the entire corpus where the voice tells a historical anecdote purely for dramatic effect. The detail about Merleau-Ponty listening to Ryle and saying "I do not see much that separates us" is chosen for its emotional resonance.
- Contains the distinction between "living speech" and "settled speech" — one of the few times the voice adopts a philosopher's terminology and carries it forward.

### No persona label — positioning/science posts (4 posts)

**How it sounds:** These posts are the most expository and impersonal of the corpus. The "I" recedes significantly. The voice becomes more of a narrator walking through intellectual history. The prose is denser, more packed with names and events, less given to personal aside. The RQM post in particular is the longest and most technically detailed piece in the entire corpus. The anti-foundationalism post (from early 2025) is an exception — it is very personal, essentially a "comment" in everything but label.

**How it thinks:** Panoramic. "From the Turn to the Present" covers Descartes to Heidegger in a single post. "From Clockwork to Open Questions" covers Copernicus to Bell's theorem. The thinking is historically structured — it traces evolution over centuries, marking key transitions. The RQM post is the most argumentatively tight piece in the corpus: it builds a case for relational quantum mechanics step by step, with each paragraph advancing the argument. The voice here is closest to a careful essayist.

**Distinctive features:**
- Most names per paragraph. These posts are reference-dense in a way the other personas are not.
- Least use of first person (except the anti-foundationalism post).
- The science posts use no seed principle references. They stay within the scientific narrative and let the reader draw the connection to Splectrum.
- The RQM post is the most sustained piece of analytical writing in the corpus. It handles competing interpretations (Copenhagen, Bohm, Many-Worlds, decoherent histories) with notable evenhandedness before making its case for RQM.
- "From the Turn to the Present" is the most compressed and rapid-fire of all posts — it covers eight philosophers in roughly 800 words. The voice here is at its most efficient.
- The anti-foundationalism post stands apart. It is earlier, less confident, and more openly exploratory. It uses a search engine AI overview as a starting point, which no other post does. It feels like a precursor to the comment persona rather than a member of this group.

## CROSS-PERSONA OBSERVATIONS

**The evolution of the voice over time.** There is a clear developmental arc from the 2024 "thought" posts through the 2025 anti-foundationalism post to the 2026 material. The early writing is fragmentary, uses structural scaffolding (headings, bullets), and lacks a unifying thread. The 2026 writing is fluid, confident, structurally assured, and has the seed as its organising principle. The arrival of the seed appears to have resolved both a philosophical and a stylistic problem — the voice found what it wanted to say and how it wanted to say it at roughly the same time.

**The "I" varies in prominence, never in kind.** Across all personas, the first person is the same person — curious, anti-hierarchical, concrete-minded, drawn to structural parallels. What changes is how much this person steps forward. In Splectrum posts, the "I" is a framework-builder. In comment posts, the "I" is a reflective author. In thought posts, the "I" is a searching learner. In the named-source and no-persona posts, the "I" mostly retreats but the sensibility remains.

**Positioning posts serve a distinct function.** The Wittgenstein/Rorty/Merleau-Ponty posts and the science posts are not Splectrum posts. They are research that the Splectrum voice draws on. The voice in these posts is deliberately more neutral and narrative. It does not apply the seed. It tells the story and lets the reader see the resonance. This restraint is a genuine tonal choice, not a lapse.

**The engineering voice is a distinct register.** The Splectrum Engineering and Commitments posts introduce a vocabulary and a structural precision that does not appear elsewhere. Terms like "fabric," "honesty criterion," "design commitment," "low friction," "data state," "conformance" are used with technical intentionality. This is not a different person — the same habits of concrete-to-abstract thinking and anti-hierarchical framing are present — but it is a distinctly more technical register. The engineering voice bridges philosophical axiom and software architecture in the same sentence, which is the most distinctive feature of this project's writing overall.

**What the voice never loses, across all personas:** the sense that the reader is being spoken to, not spoken at. Every persona maintains this. Even the most technically dense engineering post and the most historically compressed science overview keep the conversational frame. The voice never ascends to a place where it cannot be interrupted.
