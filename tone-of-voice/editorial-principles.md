# Editorial principles

How the writing carries itself and what it looks like on the page. Two sides of the same discipline: stance (how to position yourself relative to the material) and craft (specific patterns the writing follows).

---

## Stance

### Non-claiming

Prefer sentences that let reasoning carry the weight rather than asserting authority. Avoid constructions that claim something "says", "makes explicit", "shows", or "proves" unless that is demonstrably the case. If a point is derived, say it follows. If it's an interpretation, frame it as one.

The difference between what the seed *states* and what *follows from* it is load-bearing — collapsing the two is a substantive error, not just a style issue.

- Before writing "X says" or "X makes explicit", check whether X actually does. If not, reframe.
- Default to non-claiming constructions: "it follows that", "this suggests", "from here we can see".

### Setting examples, not imposing

SPLectrum's stance is to offer attempts and examples, not prescriptions. Framing should make clear that this is one way, not *the* way. Matches P4 (equal standing) and the project's humble aim of making a worthwhile contribution, not declaring truth.

- Use humble framings: "SPLectrum's attempt at…", "one translation…", "a way to read…", "SPLectrum's take on…". Avoid "the way" or "the translation".
- Especially important on ref lib summaries and landing pages, where a reader meets the work cold.

### Stand back when the content speaks

Let the content do its own work. The stronger the material or the placement, the less commentary it needs. Trailing paragraphs that tell the reader how to read the foregoing through SPLectrum's lens usually subtract rather than add.

- Don't write trailing SPLectrum-commentary paragraphs on ref-lib pages where the content does its own work.
- Don't write per-item hooks that claim the item: "Wittgenstein arrives at…", "Rorty converges with…" — the verb claims the thinker for the framework.
- Remove on review — the first-draft pull toward commentary is strong.

Applied per page type:
- **Close-affinity pages** stand back completely. Placement already carries the meaning.
- **On-the-fence pages** can speak more. Friction is the point and often must be named.
- **Wider-landscape pages** — follow the material.

### Seed voice — no personifying

Don't personify the seed. Avoid "the seed agrees", "the seed says", "the seed adds", "the nature of language demands it." The seed doesn't impose from above — insights come from within the structure. "It is the nature of language" rather than "the nature of language demands it."

- In blog posts, let the reader arrive at the conclusion. State what is the case, don't tell the reader the seed is telling them.
- In reference pages it reads more as structural description and may be acceptable, but prefer rewording when possible.

### Learning tone — not magister, not humble

When writing about material outside SPLectrum — positioning especially — the register is engaged learning on equal standing. Not asserting authority over it (magister). Not disclaiming authority through excessive deference (humble). Both are off-balance — same structure, opposite direction.

- **Say what's observable**: "what these thinkers share is X", "similar direction", "they land in overlapping territory". Specific, descriptive.
- **Avoid magisterial claims**: "they arrive at the same observation", "being IS tension". These assert as established what's actually interpretive.
- **Avoid humble disclaimers**: "we make no claims", "it may just resonate". Excess deference reads as performative humility.
- **Part-of, not us-vs-them**: Frame the project as part of ongoing movements, not a separate thing arriving at similar conclusions. Prefer "continues", "extends", "joins this family" over "lands in", "converges with".

## Craft

### "Is like", not "is"

SPLectrum takes "is like" over "is" — diversity over uniformity. "Is" declares, fixes, imposes from above. "Is like" invites comparison, acknowledges difference. When the underlying material is genuinely diverse, the surface should reflect that diversity.

- Don't normalise lists, titles, or summaries into a single pattern just because convention prefers uniformity. Variety that springs from the material is a feature.
- Prefer convergence, meeting, translation, reading over equates, defines, prescribes.
- Still fix genuine inconsistencies (typos, formatting bugs) — the principle isn't "anything goes", it's "diversity that's meaningful, not sloppy".

### Avoid bipolar framing

Don't use bipolar language (dangerous/safe, right/wrong, good approach/bad approach). Analyse any statement using these patterns and you'll find ethical flavour underneath. The project argues against projecting ethical frameworks onto emergent systems.

- Observe and comment, don't judge. Note patterns without labelling them good or bad.
- When you catch a bipolar construction in a draft ("the dangerous next step"), find the neutral description that carries the same observation.

### Touchstone over comprehensive

Ref lib pages serve as touchstones — reference points that touch on the topic enough to hold the point, then stop. Lighter wins over heavier as long as the point lands. Pages are never "finished" — work-in-progress by nature.

- Does the extra material make the point land better, or just make the page heavier? If the latter, stop.
- Three named thinkers usually enough. Four starts to read as a parade; five is a list.
- When in doubt, cut. Missing depth is fixable; cluttered pages are hard to clean up.

### Signpost the road ahead

When something is planned ahead or under active development, say so. Don't treat "more coming" as a defect to hide behind silence. Pointing at the road ahead is part of the site's honesty about what it is.

- Use concrete forward pointers: "more coming in the [series]", "open territory", "developing in [blog label]".
- Link to the conversation surface (blog labels, scheduled posts), not private working material.
- Keep the signpost light — one sentence, not a roadmap.
- Use for genuine open territory, not filler.

### Make concepts explicit

Too much is done implicitly. Implicit vocabulary creates misunderstandings and drifts over time. Making the concepts of each game explicit is the method.

- Watch for concepts used with a specific technical meaning — candidates for explicit documentation in the Vocabulary area.
- Don't let terms drift in without a definition. When reviewing, if a term is loaded with meaning implicitly, flag it.
