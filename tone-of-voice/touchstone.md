# Touchstone over comprehensive — lighter wins if the point lands

Ref lib pages serve as touchstones — reference points to ground and anchor other work. They touch on the topic enough to hold the point, then stop. Lighter wins over heavier as long as the point lands. The pages are never "finished"; they are work-in-progress by nature, and that's appropriate for an organic ecosystem.

## Why

A touchstone doesn't need to be comprehensive. Over-packing a page to cover every angle turns it into a treatment rather than a reference. Treatments belong in the blog (for conversation) or in future documentation (for depth). The ref lib is for structural reference, and that's served better by less.

## How to apply

- When enriching a page, check: does the extra material make the point land better, or just make the page heavier? If it's the latter, stop.
- Three named thinkers making the point are usually enough. Four starts to read as a parade; five is a list.
- When in doubt, cut. The reader can always follow a link or ask elsewhere. Missing depth is fixable; cluttered reference pages are hard to clean up later.
- Accept "work-in-progress" as the final state. Don't aim for "perfect" or "complete".
