# Language, Experience and Reality

## Notes

### What this post does

The reader thinks sensory experience is pre-linguistic — raw input before language processes it. The post shows it isn't. Language and senses are inseparable. The senses are our only gateway into reality, and they are already structured, already relational, already language.

### The example

A physical encounter involving touch and sight. Something we clearly identify with as the physical world — impactful, concrete. Example to be decided — needs to be visceral, immediate, something the reader can feel while reading.

### Three views

1. **The physical experience** — sight, touch, weight, temperature. The body engages before any word forms. The hand shapes itself to the object before the mind narrates the action. No abstract thought needed — the body knows.

2. **The description** — language reaching for what the body already knows. "Heavy," "cold," "rough." The words don't translate a ready-made thought. They gesture toward a bodily experience. But here's the thing: even the "raw" experience is already structured. You don't see wavelengths — you see "red." You don't feel molecular vibration — you feel "warm." The senses already categorise, distinguish, relate. That's language at work before words.

3. **The physics reality** — what feels like solid contact is electromagnetic repulsion between electron clouds. You never actually touch the thing. What feels like cold is thermal energy flowing out of your hand. What you see is photons bouncing. At every scale, it's interaction — relational all the way down. There is no moment where the object is just an object and you are just an observer.

### The equivalence question

The bodily experience, the verbal description, and the physics account are three languages describing the same event. Equivalent — the same content, different languages. The reader has seen equivalence in software languages and in a language game. Now they see it in a physical encounter.

The tension: how much of the structure was already in the experience, and how much did we project? If the abstract description and the physical event carry the same information, the structure was in the physical all along. You can't strip language from experience to find raw reality underneath. There is no language-free experience — just as there was no meaning-free bottom in the software stack.

### The point

The senses are already a language. They structure, categorise, distinguish, relate. Our only gateway into reality is itself a language. And the physics underneath is relational at every scale. Language isn't something we add to experience — it's how experience works.
