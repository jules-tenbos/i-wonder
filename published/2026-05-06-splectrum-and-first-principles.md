# Splectrum and First Principles

Splectrum as a research project was born two months ago. Over this period I have attempted to position the essence of the Splectrum seed within both the philosophical and scientific realm. This culminated last week with my post on relational quantum mechanics (RQM), a project I feel in spirit closely related to — albeit with a wider scope than just scientific. I was feeling my way, not always high in confidence: who are you that you dare to push the seed, these five principles to us as the entirety of your foundation to build on?

The fact that my whole focus suddenly organised itself around some simple principles has been an odd event. The Splectrum name comes from a collaborative AI software engineering project and its contents from my years of interest in all things evolution — physical and mental — where I slowly drifted more and more into language as the expression of all these things. Then suddenly the seed fell into place and to my relief I noticed that this 'turn' was part of a natural evolution in thinking that has taken place over the last few hundred years. And this not only in philosophy, but likewise in science. I don't know if I succeeded but the posts of the last two months were an attempt to lift the curtain on this, while not overloading you with material that is difficult to read. 

Now we can start on the fun part. How far can we push based on the five seed principles, does it give us a consistent view of what we do in science, philosophy, religion, everyday life? If language really is the medium through which the world discloses itself — our personal experiences, shared knowledge and reality, growing insights to an ever-growing ecosystem of languages — then we should be able to apply it using the same principles across all of reality. Let's do some stress testing here!

I very much believe in personal freedom, the freedom to shape one's personal world. I see no place for an authority to tell me what I should think or do, but I am happy to conform within language games — be it religion or social institutions — that I feel at home in or affinity with. Respect for the other is top, irrespective of their (world) views. That doesn't mean I am a die-hard pacifist, unfortunately there are games with brutal rules that we can't always avoid being part of ... The most important out of all of this is: I am not here to judge, whatever statements I make as part of my observations. I am speaking to you from within the Splectrum language and vocabulary — making observations and statements — but do not impose anything on other languages — ways of life — as that is what judgements are: impositions.

Let's take a short walk past the five seed principles:

*P1 - Language is relational.*  
Language is a medium that binds things together — like glue of some sort, it tells a story of togetherness. It creates shape and character, an expression of being. Like words in a sentence, it binds encapsulated items together. We normally approach it with a human in the observer seat. However, this constraint does not exist in the principle — anyone, anything can be 'observer'.

*P2 - Language is the medium through which a subject experiences reality.*
Expect a battleground here! No reality without subject (observer), no outside view seen from nowhere. What you experience is what you get, what is. The funny part is that in everyday life we acknowledge this: 'He lives in a different reality'. But within science and philosophy there is a lot of reluctance about this. What is often overlooked is that lived experience of reality provides richness, representation is in essence dead.

*P3 - Language is where subjects share knowledge about reality.*
Language that is shared converges to a shared understanding — knowledge — because the interactions shape the vocabulary and the way the relational is constructed. It's about hiding complexity within concepts and creating relational patterns to express thoughts, about creating our reality as a community — created at the grassroots level. If we accept being dictated to from above, then it is because we — as a community — have accepted to be dictated to.

*P4 - Languages are inter-relational and have equal standing in potential.*
Languages interrelate in ways that loop around — it is turtles the whole way down. The richness of lived reality creates an endless variation. And languages as forms of life all have equal standing — there is no hierarchy, nothing to be judged or imposed from the outside in. Each is a reality that one can choose to be part of or not. Not necessarily a story with a happy ending: my body may be captured, it may cost me my life, but it only becomes my inner reality if I allow it to.

*P5 - Together they form a web of growing complexity.*
Language is a living thing — in fertile ground it will grow and multiply. It will create uniformity and variety. And go through periods of rich harvests and famines. However, it doesn't deconstruct itself back to its primordial beginning. There is no way it completely loses its history.

Seed principles with a bit more meat on the bone, just the beginning.

## The turn this post achieves

The transitional period (March-April) asked: does Splectrum deserve equal standing — does it make sense in the light of the evolution of thought? The encounters with philosophy, science, and language were tests. Each confirmed the seed belongs in the conversation.

This post marks the turn:
- **Before:** Seed exists → look outward for validation → does it fit?
- **After:** Seed is positioned → look outward from within → observe with equal standing

The direction of reasoning reverses. Statements come from within the principles, not by comparison with outside authority. But the mode stays observational — not evaluative. Looking at other vocabularies (Russell, Wittgenstein, QM interpretations) is done from within P1-P5, with full respect, understanding them in their own language, then observing what the language gives access to and what it doesn't. That's P1 in action.

"Alongside, not above" still holds — but now it's earned. Splectrum has its own ground to stand on. Equal standing means: Splectrum's observations carry the same weight as any other tradition's. Neither ranks the other. Each speaks from its own language, its own principles. The interaction between them — the relation — is where insight lives. That's P4.

The tone of thought guide needs updating to capture this new mode: "building from within" — making statements from the principles, observational but from a declared position. Distinct from the positioning phase but continuous with it.

### The seed as first principles — P1 to P5

- **P1:** Language is relational. What a language gives access to depends on what it relates to.
- **P2:** Language is the medium through which a subject experiences reality. Experience is always within the reach of a language.
- **P3:** Language is where subjects share knowledge about reality. And language is the source of the only objectivity known: convergence of subjects.
- **P4:** Languages are inter-relational. Languages, as committed ways of expressing relation, are not isolated games. They interact, overlap, and inform each other, all having equal standing in potential.
- **P5:** Together they form a web of growing complexity. Relational density increases as knowledge grows.

Naming them P1-P5 gives the blog a vocabulary to work with going forward. "From P3, we can say..." "This is P1 in action..." The reader can track which principle is at work.

### The direction

Future posts build from the principles. Three modes:
- **Commenting** — looking at topics through the lens of P1-P5. The qualia example: P1 applied to the qualia debate dissolves the "inaccessibility" wall.
- **Researching** — exploring domains where the principles reveal something. Science, biology, language structure, experience.
- **Building** — developing positions that follow from the principles. Epistemology, ontology, the nature of shared reality.

Each post grounded in the principles, referencing them explicitly when relevant.
