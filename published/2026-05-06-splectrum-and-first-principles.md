# Splectrum and First Principles

Splectrum was conceived about a year ago. It started as an engineering project around [DSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain-specific_language) and collaborative AI. Splectrum as a philosophical project was born only two months ago. This is when the seed materialised. Since then I have been busy researching how the Splectrum seed is positioned within the philosophical and scientific realm. This culminated last week with my post on relational quantum mechanics (RQM), a project I feel in spirit closely related to — albeit with a wider scope than just scientific. I was feeling my way, not always high in confidence: who are you that you dare to push the principles of the seed, those 6 lines, to us as the entirety of your foundation to build on?

The fact that my whole focus suddenly organised itself around a set of simple principles has been an odd event. The Splectrum name comes from a collaborative AI software engineering project. Its philosophical contents come from years of interest in all things evolution — in the physical and mental realm — where I slowly drifted more and more into language as the core, the medium of expression. Then suddenly the seed fell into place. To my relief I have noticed in my research that this 'turn' fits well with a natural evolution in thinking that has taken place over the last few hundred years. Not only in philosophy, but likewise in science. I don't know if I succeeded but the posts of the last two months were an attempt to lift the curtain on this, while not overloading you with material that is difficult to read. 

Now the fun can start. Let's get building on those principles! How strong is the foundation, does it give us a consistent view of what we do in science, philosophy, religion, everyday life? If language really is the medium through which the world discloses itself — our personal experiences, shared knowledge and reality, growing insights to an ever-growing ecosystem of languages — then we should be able to apply the principles of the seed across all of reality. Let's do some stress testing here!

Before we do a short walk past the principles I would like to add a disclaimer here. It is actually in the principles, but I want to state it upfront to avoid any misunderstanding. When I speak from within Splectrum there isn't the slightest intent to impose anything onto others. On the contrary. Over time I have come to deeply respect all thought systems, beliefs, ways of life including those that are very alien to the person I am. Diversity is a necessity and respect an important core value.

*P0 - Being implies language.*<br/>
There is an intrinsic link between being and language. This is being as in something that is different from the context it resides in. The dualism of 'being' and 'the other'. Language exists in the interface between the two, what expresses the interplay between being and the other. Being is something dynamic, how differences are maintained and evolve. This brings language to anything that exists. Not only humans, or other forms of life, but also the non-living. Within this thought framework one could define life as a self-language-generating context, where non-life depends on outside imposition. There is a lot to unpack here, and a lot to substantiate — looking forward to it.

*P1 - Language is relational.*<br/> 
Language is a medium that binds things together — like glue of some sort, it tells a story of togetherness. How being and the other interrelate, where the other contains other beings. It creates shape and character, an expression of belonging. Like words in a sentence, it binds encapsulated items together. We normally approach it with a human in the observer seat. However, this constraint does not exist in the principle — anyone, anything can be 'observer'.

*P2 - Language is the medium through which a subject experiences reality.*
Expect a battleground here! No reality without subject (observer), no outside view seen from nowhere. What you experience is what you get, what is. The funny part is that in everyday life we acknowledge this: 'He lives in a different world, a different reality'. But within science and philosophy there is a lot of reluctance about this. What is often overlooked is that lived experience of reality provides richness, representation is in essence dead.

*P3 - Language is where subjects share knowledge about reality.*
Where P2 essentially deals with the dynamic between being and the other, P3 deals with the dynamic between beings embedded in a shared part of 'the other'. From the individual interaction emerges the 'being as a group' interactions. The interactions are expressed in language. Language that is shared converges to a shared understanding — knowledge — because the interactions shape the vocabulary and the way the relational is constructed. Vocabulary as a set of concepts, concept as a form of being (in the world of meaning). It's about hiding complexity within concepts and creating relational patterns to express thoughts, about creating our reality as a community — created at the grassroots level. If we accept being dictated to from above, then it is because we — as a community — have accepted to be dictated to.

*P4 - Languages are inter-relational and have equal standing in potential.*
Languages interrelate in (many) different ways. Thinking of a language game as expression of an activity, there are many variations how the rules of a game or activity are expressed. One could see these languages as equivalent, horizontally related. Whenever a language is used to spawn another language, it creates a vertical upwards relationship. When we analyse a language and clarify its concepts and grammar we create a vertical down type of relationship. There is so much richness hidden in the word inter-relational.<br/>
But inter-relational does not imply hierarchy, imposition from the outside inwards. These are concepts that exist within a language. Languages on their own are free standing, and master of their own potentiality.

*P5 - Together they form a web of growing complexity.*
Language is a living thing — in fertile ground it will grow and multiply. The richness of inter-relation is just that: a fertile ground for language to evolve. It will create uniformity and variety. And go through periods of rich harvests and famines. However, it doesn't deconstruct itself back to its primordial beginning. Evolution stays coupled to its history.

Seed principles with a bit more meat on the bone, just the beginning.
