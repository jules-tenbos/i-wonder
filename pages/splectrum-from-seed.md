# Splectrum from Seed

> **Language is relational.**<br>
> What a language gives access to depends on what it relates to.
>
> **Language is the medium through which a subject experiences reality.**<br>
> Experience is always within the reach of a language.
>
> **Language is where subjects share knowledge about reality.**<br>
> And language is the source of the only objectivity known: convergence of subjects.
>
> **Languages are inter-relational.**<br>
> Languages, as committed ways of expressing relation, are not isolated games. They interact, overlap, and inform each other, all having equal standing in potential.
>
> **Together they form a web of growing complexity.**<br>
> Relational density increases as knowledge grows.

### The Seed Unpacked

Splectrum starts from five lines that focus on one thing: how language as a lived experience shapes the reality we live in. This isn't a bolt from the blue. It comes in the wake of a gradual shift in philosophical and scientific thinking — that puts the relational close in with reality.

But this isn't something for philosophers and scientists only. We experience it all around us, every day, as depicted in [Let's Make the Seed Concrete](/2026/03/lets-make-seed-concrete.html).

However, if this reflects the way we now broadly think about this, it hasn't always been the case. The history of philosophy and science shows a clear evolution in thinking. And as far as language is concerned, this kicked off with what is called the Kantian turn — the recognition that the observer is not a passive mirror of reality but actively shapes what counts as experience. Kant placed the subject at the centre: we don't just receive the world, we structure it. This was followed on by Hegel, who made that structuring historical and social, by Husserl, who turned to lived experience itself, and by Heidegger, who placed understanding within the activity of living. And then Wittgenstein, who will be the subject of our next post.
